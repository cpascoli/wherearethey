The writer Tim Ventura interviews Patrick “PJ” Hughes, a second-class petty officer and aviation electronics technician with VAW-117, the USS Nimitz's E-2C Hawkeye squadron, in November 2004. Hughes describes the Hawkeye's data recorders, the “bricks” that recorded its radar and electronic data, being taken from his shop's safe soon after the aircraft that was airborne during Fravor's intercept landed, by his commanding officer and two men in U.S. Air Force flight suits, and signed out but never returned. He believes the two were couriers, not investigators, and relays that the Hawkeye's crew saw the object and signed non-disclosure agreements.

It is his testimony, consistent with his earlier accounts. No record of the recorders' removal has been released, and David Fravor rejects the idea that anyone came to the ships.

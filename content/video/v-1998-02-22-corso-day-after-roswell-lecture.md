Philip J. Corso, the retired Army lieutenant colonel whose *The Day After Roswell* had come out the year before, speaks for nearly four hours to an audience at J.Z. Knight's Ramtha's School of Enlightenment (“the Ranch”) on 22 February 1998, the year he died. He retells the book's story, the body he says he saw at Fort Riley and the Roswell artifacts he says he seeded into industry from the Army's Foreign Technology Division, and adds stories that are not in it, among them a 1957 telepathic encounter at White Sands.

He also contradicts parts of the lore: he denies a cover-up in the usual sense, calls Area 51 alien stories and underground bases fabrications, says the bodies numbered no more than about ten and that none was alive, and dismisses the 1954 Eisenhower meeting as fiction. His account is testimony only: by his own telling the work left “No paper trail”, and historians and the Army dispute much of the book. The audience questions and the host's closing remarks reflect the venue, a spiritual school, and are not evidence.

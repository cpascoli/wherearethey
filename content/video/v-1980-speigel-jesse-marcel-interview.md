An interview with Maj. Jesse Marcel, the 509th Bomb Group's intelligence officer at Roswell in 1947, recorded by the journalist Lee Speigel for his NBC radio reports, about 1980: Marcel gives his age as 73, speaks of 32 years since the event, and has not yet seen *The Roswell Incident*, the book by Charles Berlitz and William Moore published that year. It was published on YouTube in 2025 by the *Somewhere in the Skies* podcast.

It is his own account, at length: the sheriff's call, the night at Brazel's ranch, a debris field three quarters of a mile long, light members with pink-lavender symbols, foil-thin metal no sledgehammer could dent, and Ramey's order not to talk. He separates what he saw from what he presumes, and calls the extraterrestrial origin "a conjecture".

The documents disagree on several points. The Fort Worth Star-Telegram quoted him in July 1947 describing "the remnants of the kite and balloon"; Sheridan Cavitt, who went to the ranch with him, recognised a weather balloon and recalled no sledgehammer test; the weather officer Irving Newton described the pink figures as faded markings on radar-target sticks; and the Air Force found the same wreckage in all the Fort Worth photographs. Researchers have also found that some of his claims about his career, such as a pilot's licence in 1928 and writing the report on the Soviet bomb, are not borne out by his records.

*Source: Lee Speigel's recording, via Somewhere in the Skies (YouTube, 2025). Whisper transcript.*

On the afternoon of 24 June 1947, the private pilot Kenneth Arnold, flying near Mount Rainier in Washington, reported nine bright objects moving in a chain past the mountain at a speed he estimated at well over 1,000 miles an hour. He told reporters at Pendleton, Oregon, and described it on local radio the next day. The wire services carried his story across the country, the press turned his description into “flying saucers”, and hundreds of similar reports followed over the next weeks, among them the Roswell announcement of 8 July.

The Army Air Forces investigated. An early analysis argued his estimates were inconsistent and that the objects may have been known aircraft; the Air Force's final conclusion, recorded in the Blue Book file, was a mirage. Arnold maintained that he had seen real objects.

*Sources: Project Blue Book case file (U.S. National Archives) · Arnold's radio interview (1947)*

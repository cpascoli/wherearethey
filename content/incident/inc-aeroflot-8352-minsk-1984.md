In the early hours of 7 September 1984, by James Oberg's dating, the crew of Aeroflot flight 8352 saw a light about 120 km from Minsk. The Tu-134 was flying from Tbilisi via Rostov to Tallinn with an Estonian crew under Igor Cherkashin. The newspaper *Trud* published their account in January 1985: a bright, steady "star" that sent a beam to the ground, opened into cones of light, turned into a green cloud and seemed to escort the airliner as a glowing "cloud airplane" all the way to Tallinn. In the account, the Minsk controller first saw nothing, then reported the "northern lights" in the same direction, a second airliner described the cloud as it came closer, and the Tallinn controller spoke of two extra radar blips. N. A. Zheltukhin of a Soviet scientific commission concluded that the crew had seen a UFO.

The only primary record we hold is the newspaper article, in the English translation the CIA later released. It is a journalist's retelling of the crew's story: there is no flight report, radar record or official finding. The Soviet state programme's history (Platov & Sokolov, 2000) does not mention the case.

The leading explanation is a missile launch. James Oberg, a former NASA mission controller, found that the co-pilot's drawings matched those of night-time launches. He traced sightings made the same morning in Sweden and Finland to a source over the Barents Sea, and proposed a submarine-launched missile test. He also said the case cannot be closed until Russian records of that launch are released.

*Sources: Trud, 30 January 1985 (CIA Reading Room 0005516658, English translation); James Oberg, NBC News, 15 May 2008*

Just after midnight on 16 September 1965, two South African Police constables on patrol on the Pretoria–Bronkhorstspruit highway, John Locken (spelled Lockem in the American reports) and Koos de Klerk, said their van's headlights lit a domed, copper-coloured disc about 30 feet across standing on the road, which lifted off within seconds, trailing flames. The next day a British paper reported that police and scientists were investigating.

According to the Associated Press report as summarised by NICAP, the road surface burned after the object left, part of it was found caved in, and the tar was burned over an area about six feet across. The district commandant of Pretoria North, Lt. Col. J.B. Brits, is reported to have called the matter "of a highly secret nature" under inquiry "in top circles", and samples of the road went to a scientific agency.

No police record, analysis result or official conclusion has been found: the case rests on the officers' account as relayed by the 1965 wire reports. The date is the 16th in the contemporary report (17 September in some later retellings).

*Sources: South Wales Echo and Evening Express, Cardiff, 17 September 1965 · NICAP, UFOs: A New Look (1969), citing Associated Press, Johannesburg, 17 September 1965*

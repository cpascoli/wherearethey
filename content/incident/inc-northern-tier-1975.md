For about two weeks from the night of 27–28 October 1975, Strategic Air Command bases along the northern United States reported unidentified aircraft or lights over or near their weapons areas: first Loring AFB in Maine, where intruders were taken to be helicopters coming from Canada, then Wurtsmith AFB in Michigan, Malmstrom AFB and its Minuteman missile field in Montana, Minot AFB in North Dakota, and the Canadian Forces radar station at Falconbridge, Ontario. The National Military Command Center relayed the reports, and they reached the CIA's Operations Center the first morning.

The best documented night is 7–8 November at Malmstrom. NORAD's height-finder radar tracked slow-moving targets over Lewistown while missile-site crews reported lights and jet-like noise, the FAA had no aircraft in the area, and two F-106 interceptors, kept high by the terrain, never made contact. Site crews said the lights went out as the fighters arrived and came back when they left. NORAD ruled out northern lights and carried the track as a fade, "remaining UNKNOWN".

The record leaves the incursions unexplained. The Loring and Wurtsmith intruders were described as helicopters but never identified, and the Air Force's search with helicopters and fighters produced no identification. Records of the incursions were released under the Freedom of Information Act from the late 1970s; our copies are the NMCC memorandum of 8 November, a DDO update and a CIA watch log, and the 11 November message is known here only through the 1979 New York Times Magazine quotation.

On the evening of 5 March 1954, aircraft returning to Nouasseur Air Depot, the U.S. Air Force's supply base near Casablanca in French Morocco, reported lights making passes at them as they flew the landing pattern: two KC-97 tankers of the 301st Air Refueling Squadron from 1918, then a MATS C-54 whose commander, Capt. William M. Pond, said a light turned onto a collision course with him and then seemed to hover. The same night a ground radar site of the 736th AC&W Squadron tracked a stationary target south of the base, and it was still there on the 6th and 7th. The tower operator, searching visually, saw only the reporting aircraft. A week later, on 12 March, an F-86F pilot at 30,000 feet reported a white object, somewhat like an aircraft in shape, flying level with him for about thirty seconds before it vanished.

The Air Force's Office of Special Investigations interviewed the crews and found ordinary candidates for much of it: the crews themselves thought the lights were jets in formation, a lighted weather balloon had been released at 2130, one reported object was another C-47, the GCA targets were buildings and the radar officer put the site's stationary echoes down to atmospheric effects. Project Blue Book's final card accepted ground reflection for the radar, but kept the three earliest aircrew reports as Unidentified.

*Sources: Project Blue Book case files on Nouasseur, March 1954 (NARA IDs 28957771, 28957774, 28957904), including the OSI special inquiry of 15 March 1954*

At 8.20 am on 17 December 1992 two people in the operations room of Heathrow's control tower watched, with the naked eye and binoculars, a black object shaped like an inverted boomerang hanging in the east about seven miles away, over the morning fog with the sun behind it, before it moved off steadily. The report went up the military air traffic chain and RAF West Drayton signalled it to the Ministry of Defence the next day, copying the Defence Intelligence Staff.

The signal, one page in the DI55 file, is all that survives: the names are redacted and no reply or assessment follows. The National Archives' guide to the file pairs it with the Louth triangle of 9 December 1992, reported a week earlier from Lincolnshire.

*Source: MoD file DEFE 31/181, p. 127 (The National Archives, opened March 2009)*

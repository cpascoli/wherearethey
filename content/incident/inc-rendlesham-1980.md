Over three nights in late December 1980, U.S. Air Force personnel at RAF Woodbridge in Suffolk, one of two neighbouring RAF bases (with RAF Bentwaters) then used by the USAF, reported unexplained lights in the adjacent Rendlesham Forest. Around 03:00 on 26 December, security patrolmen including Sgt. Jim Penniston and John Burroughs went into the forest to investigate lights descending among the trees. Penniston later said he approached and touched a small craft; his account is not independently corroborated. Penniston and Burroughs describe health effects in the weeks that followed, and Penniston says AFOSI agents gave him a typed statement to memorise as his official account; Halt says the men told him they were interrogated, drugged and hypnotised.

In the early hours of 28 December the deputy base commander, Lt. Col. Charles Halt, led a second search with several servicemen, recording a running commentary on a micro-cassette and taking radiation readings with an AN/PDR-27 meter at three depressions in the ground. On 13 January 1981 he summarised the events in a one-page memo to the UK Ministry of Defence, "Unexplained Lights" (which dates the first sighting to 27 December). It was released under the U.S. Freedom of Information Act in 1983. The Ministry of Defence judged the report to be of no defence significance and never interviewed Halt; its 1981 papers, as Lord Hill-Norton quoted them to the minister in 2001, record a Defence Intelligence Staff comment that the peak radiation reading seemed "significantly higher than the average background" and that Halt's tape recordings were handed to the visiting commander of U.S. Air Forces in Europe, Gen. Charles Gabriel. The minister replied that no full assessment of the readings had been made and that the Ministry could not answer the questions about Gabriel.

The explanations on the Ministry's own file are the Orford Ness lighthouse and a fireball. A sceptic's summary sent to the MoD in October 1983 matches the first night's lights to a brilliant fireball recorded over southern England at 2.50 a.m. on 26 December, the pulsing light in the trees to the lighthouse, which a local forester found lay on the line from the base's back gate through the site, the depressions to rabbits and Halt's "star-like objects" to stars; the MoD's 1983 note for Parliament records a BBC investigation's lighthouse explanation, and a Suffolk police inspector wrote in 1999 that his three constables remained "unconvinced [the] occurrence was genuine" and that the lighthouse beams "caused strange visual effects" in some conditions. The Ministry adopted none of these: its 1994 note for a television documentary lists the lighthouse and "fireball activity" as theories circulating and says that "in the absence of any hard evidence, the MOD remains open-minded about these sightings". Halt says the lighthouse was visible separately the whole time and rejects the rabbit explanation. The outcome stays open because no official explanation was ever adopted: the MoD found nothing of defence significance, and in 2008 called the case closed, without saying what the witnesses saw.

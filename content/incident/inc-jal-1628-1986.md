On 17 November 1986, the crew of Japan Air Lines cargo flight 1628, a Boeing 747 flying from Paris to Tokyo via Anchorage, reported very large unidentified lights pacing their aircraft at 35,000 feet over eastern Alaska, for about 50 minutes by later accounts (the captain's FAA interview speaks of intervals of five to fifteen minutes). Capt. Kenju Terauchi described two smaller craft and a "mothership" he estimated at twice the size of an aircraft carrier.

Anchorage air traffic control suggested evasive action, and FAA radar data was reviewed afterwards; military radar returns were later dismissed as clutter, and two other nearby aircraft saw nothing. FAA investigators found the crew normal, professional and rational. The FAA's John Callahan later said he briefed the radar data to officials including CIA personnel, who told those present that the meeting "never happened".

The sceptic Philip J. Klass argued the crew had mistaken the planets Jupiter and Mars, which were in the direction of the sighting, for nearby objects.

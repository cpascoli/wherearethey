The Holloman landing is a story, not a documented event: no film, record or named witness to the landing itself has ever surfaced. It comes from the filmmakers Robert Emenegger and Allan Sandler, who in the early 1970s were developing a documentary with the cooperation of officials at Norton Air Force Base, California, and from Paul Shartle, the base's audiovisual security manager. They say they were promised, and Shartle says he saw, film of a craft landing at Holloman Air Force Base, New Mexico, with occupants met by the base commander. Accounts differ on the date, usually given as 1964; in the 1988 broadcast Emenegger put it about three years before his 1973 visit, and other versions say May 1971.

The footage was withdrawn before release, and the 1974 documentary *UFOs: Past, Present, and Future* told the story as a dramatised "what if", narrated by Rod Serling. Shartle repeated his account on live television in 1988, saying he was told it was purchased theatrical footage but did not believe it. The story's circulation through the same period and circles as the documented AFOSI disinformation of the 1980s, including the 1988 broadcast's informant "Falcon" (Richard Doty), leads many researchers to treat it with caution; others say the footage exists: the documentary maker James Fox said so on a 2023 podcast, according to a local Alamogordo report. In 2025 the astrophysicist Eric Davis, a former AATIP adviser, revived the story in *The Age of Disclosure*, saying George H.W. Bush had told him in 2003 of three craft at Holloman, one landing and a non-human entity meeting Air Force and CIA personnel, with film as the main evidence; Bush, he said, was refused it. Davis has given no evidence beyond his account of Bush's words, and no record of the event has been released. It remains an unverified claim.

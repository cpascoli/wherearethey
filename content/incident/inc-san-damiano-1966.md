On an October afternoon in 1966, dated by the researchers who recorded the witnesses to the 17th, pilots of the Italian Air Force's 50° Stormo at San Damiano, near Piacenza, saw what looked like a star shining in the daytime sky. Two F-84 pilots climbed towards it. Dante Golinelli, returning early from a low-level exercise, says he reached about 33–34,000 ft and saw a large, bright, metallic isosceles triangle hanging motionless far above him, which he put at 50–60,000 ft. The air-defence radar would not scramble F-104 interceptors to that height. A second pilot on a test flight went higher, and the object stayed for hours before drifting east.

The tower controller that day, Paolo Gianassi, remembers the chase differently: the young pilot he heard on the radio, Sottotenente Vittorino Bocca, said the bright, silvery object drew away each time he closed in, until his F-84 reached its ceiling, and Gianassi puts the whole episode at ten to fifteen minutes. Neither man says the object was tracked on radar. Both accounts were given more than fifty years later to Alberto Negri of the Piacenza association Spazio Tesla, and they are the only sources the archive has: no Air Force record has been found (the Air Force's published UFO tables begin in 1972). Nor has the contemporary article, which Gianassi places in the Gazzetta di Parma and Spazio Tesla in the weekly Tribuna Illustrata. Text searches of l'Unità's national edition for 18–22 October 1966 and of La Stampa's archive for 17 October–30 November 1966 found no report of it.

The only explanation on record came from the pilots' command, which, Golinelli says, told them it had been a balloon and ordered them not to talk. Golinelli rejects this, since the object stayed still for hours before moving off. A high-altitude balloon has not been ruled out by any investigation, so the case stays open.

*Sources: Spazio Tesla interview with Dante Golinelli (recorded 29 June 2018) · testimony of Col. Paolo Gianassi to Alberto Negri (Spazio Tesla, 2022) · searches of the l'Unità and La Stampa archives*

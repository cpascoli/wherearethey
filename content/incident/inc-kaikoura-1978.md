On 21 December 1978 the crew of a Safe Air cargo flight from Blenheim to Christchurch, pilots Vern Powell and Ian Pirie, saw unexplained lights off the Kaikōura coast of New Zealand's South Island, some reportedly matched by Wellington air traffic control radar.

On the night of 30–31 December a Melbourne television crew, reporter Quentin Fogarty and cameraman David Crockett, flew a newspaper run between Wellington and Christchurch aboard a Safe Air Argosy with Capt. Bill Startup and First Officer Robert Guard (Crockett's wife Ngaire recorded sound) and filmed bright lights on 16 mm colour film for about fifteen minutes, again with radar contacts reported. The footage was broadcast around the world.

The Royal New Zealand Air Force began its investigation when it decided to fly an Orion surveillance of the area on 2/3 January 1979. Its report of 11 January, drawing on interviews with the Argosy crews and radar controllers and on work by the DSIR and the Meteorological Service, found that almost all the sightings could be explained by natural but unusual phenomena: a very bright Venus seen through an atmosphere prone to freak propagation of light and radar, spurious returns from the Wellington radar (which a DSIR watch with observers on the coast confirmed), the lights of more than 50 Japanese squid boats, and possibly cars or trains near the Clarence River. The DSIR's interim report agreed, said Crockett's film was dominated by flaws in the aircraft window, and named two radar-and-visual episodes that did not fit. The file was released in December 2010.

The physicist Bruce Maccabee, whose study is kept in the same file, disputed those explanations; the DSIR scientist Bill Ireland answered him in Applied Optics and in 2000 concluded that the film shows a small group of squid boats about six kilometres away. Startup and Fogarty rejected the squid-boat explanation in their books.

Around midday on 21 October 1982, a young biologist working in a cellular-biology laboratory reported that a small oval object, about 1.5 metres across, had hovered silently a metre above his garden for twenty minutes before leaving straight up at great speed. He went to the gendarmerie that evening. The next morning he noticed that amaranth plants beside the spot had withered, and the gendarmes came back to take samples.

France's official UAP office, then GEPAN, investigated and published the case as Technical Note 17 in March 1983, keeping the witness anonymous and coding the place names; later writers place it in Laxou, near Nancy. GEPAN found no reason to reject his testimony, but he was the only witness, military radar showed nothing notable, and its plant analysts could not distinguish the withered plants from a control, pointing instead to frost the day before and to poor sample keeping. A physicist's estimate that the grass rising beneath the object would need a strong electric field is the note's only physical hypothesis.

The case is often cited as physical evidence of a strong field acting on plants; GEPAN's own report is more cautious. Sceptics argue the withered flowers were normal for the season and the account a perception without an object.

*Sources: GEPAN (CNES), Note technique n° 17, 21 March 1983 · [Rossoni, Maillot & Déguillaume, *Les OVNI du CNES* (2007), ch. 15](http://www.zetetique.fr/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/OvniDuCnes_chapitre15.pdf)*

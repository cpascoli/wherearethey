On the evenings of 6 and 7 August 1952, officers of the U.S. Navy's Air Transport Squadron 24 (VR-24) at the Naval Air Facility Port Lyautey, French Morocco, reported a brilliant white light. On the 6th Lt. C. B. Green saw it from the ground, crossing the sky faster than any jet he knew. On the 7th the crew of an R5D on a night training flight, Lt. D. M. Dobos and Lt. W. Foster, watched it climb away from them, turn across their path, hover and descend, showing a dazzling centre and two blinking red lights, and the airfield tower, given its position, saw it too.

The base intelligence officer sent their statements to the Office of Naval Intelligence on 15 August, rating the report A-1 and calling it the first direct observation made locally of what were called flying saucers; he wrote that another aircraft or a shooting star had been ruled out but reached no conclusion. A watch with cameramen on the 8th saw nothing. Project Blue Book, which received the report, filed it as “Possibly Balloon”, a verdict its own card does not argue for beyond noting that the maneuvers ruled out astronomical causes.

*Sources: Project Blue Book case file Port Lyantey, French Morocco, August 1952 (NARA ID 28947832), with the ONI information report 1-R-52 and the pilots' statements*

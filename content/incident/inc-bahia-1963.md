On the night of 8 November 1963 Radio Tupi in Rio de Janeiro broadcast that a large metal sphere had crashed in the centre of Conde, a town in Bahia, leaving a four-metre crater; through its glass-covered window openings, the report said, a human body could be seen in heavy clothing with undecipherable inscriptions. The CIA's Foreign Broadcast Information Service picked up the broadcast, and the report reached the White House's National Aeronautics and Space Council, which on 13 November asked the State Department to check it.

The check took a week. The embassy in Rio cabled on 14 November to confirm it understood the story — a "large metal manned balloon" with a dead crewman in a uniform or spacesuit — and the consulate in Salvador replied that it was "apparently fabricated in Rio": reporters who rushed to Conde found no one who had seen anything, the state's public-security secretary had only speculated about a weather balloon, and the Ministry of Aeronautics denied any "strange object". A local paper headlined its piece "The Airship that wasn't there".

The case is a clear example of an alarming crash-retrieval story checked through official channels at the time and found unsupported. The three records were released in the PURSUE programme in 2026.

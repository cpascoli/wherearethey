On 11 November 2014 a Chilean Navy helicopter flying a routine training route along the coast west of Santiago filmed a slow-moving, double-lobed object on its infrared camera for nearly ten minutes. At two points the object appeared to release a dense dark trail. Neither the helicopter nor ground stations saw anything on radar, and the crew's radio calls went unanswered. Chile's official UFO committee, CEFAA, part of the civil aviation authority, examined the footage for two years. A French team it consulted proposed an airliner coming in to land, but the committee rejected that and in January 2017 released the video through the journalist Leslie Kean as unidentified; its director, Gen. Ricardo Bermúdez, said: "We do not know what it was, but we do know what it was not."

Within five days amateur analysts working on Metabunk, led by Mick West, used the helicopter's GPS and camera data and public flight tracking to match the object, position by position, to Iberia flight IB6830, an Airbus A340 climbing out of Santiago much farther away and higher than the crew assumed, with its contrail accounting for the trails. The airliner explanation comes from independent analysis rather than an official finding, and CEFAA's conclusion was not revised.

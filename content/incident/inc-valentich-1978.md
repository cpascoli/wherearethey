On the evening of 21 October 1978, Frederick Valentich, a 20-year-old private pilot with about 150 hours, flew a Cessna 182L (VH-DSJ) out of Moorabbin, near Melbourne, for King Island in Bass Strait. Shortly after reporting Cape Otway he asked Melbourne Flight Service whether there was traffic near him and, over six minutes, described an object with bright lights that passed over him at speed, orbited above him, was long, metallic and shiny with a green light, vanished and came back. He then reported his engine rough idling, said the object was hovering above him and was “not an aircraft”, and the transmission ended in 17 seconds of open microphone.

No trace of the aircraft or the pilot was found in a four-day air, sea and land search. The Department of Transport's summary report, approved in April 1982, reproduces the transcript and records that the reason for the disappearance has not been determined. In 1983 a Cessna 182 cowl flap from a serial range including VH-DSJ's washed up on Flinders Island; the file records the department asking whether it could have drifted there, not a finding.

No official explanation has been established. The National Archives of Australia notes the later suggestion that a bright grouping of planets and a star distracted an inexperienced pilot at night. The official report itself offers no explanation.

*Sources: Department of Transport, Aircraft Accident Investigation Summary Report V116/783/1047 (National Archives of Australia, NAA: B1497, V116/783/1047) · National Archives of Australia, “Flying saucers – fact or fiction?” (2018)*

In March 1967, two flights of Minuteman nuclear missiles at Malmstrom Air Force Base, Montana, went into a "no-go" (unlaunchable) condition. On 16 March all ten missiles of Echo Flight shut down within seconds of each other, an event the Air Force's own records document. Former launch officer Robert Salas says that eight days later, on 24 March, at Oscar Flight, where he was deputy crew commander under Fred Meiwald, the topside guards reported a glowing red oval object hovering above the front gate and his missiles then dropped offline one after another: six to eight of ten in his 2001 account, all ten in his 2013 testimony. He dated the event 16 March when he first told it in public, and says he told it for years as having happened at Echo until Meiwald corrected him in 1996.

Others back parts of the story. Salas has played recorded calls with Meiwald, who confirms the guard's reports and missiles going down, and with Walter Figel, Echo's deputy commander, who describes UFO reports as Echo went down. Dwynne Arneson says a message reporting that a UFO had shut down missile silos passed through his communications centre, and that a Boeing engineer later told him the missiles "did not go down by themselves"; Robert Jamison says he was briefed on a UFO sighting before being sent to restart Oscar Flight. Asked about the case in 2022, the Navy's Scott Bray told Congress the task force had no data on it: "I have heard stories."

The 341st Strategic Missile Wing's histories, released under FOIA, document the Echo Flight shutdown but state that rumours of UFOs around Echo Flight "were disproven": a strike team and the base radar squadron reported nothing unusual. They quote the engineering team's opinion that externally generated signals caused the shutdown, while calling that "very remote", and record that tests at Boeing reproduced the fault with a pulse at the logic coupler. The histories contain no record of the Oscar Flight event, and Salas's account of it dates from decades later. In 2025 the Wall Street Journal reported that AARO had traced the shutdowns to a secret Air Force electromagnetic-pulse test the crews were not told about; AARO has not published that finding, Salas calls it a cover-up, and he says the Department of Energy confirmed there were no nuclear tests on either date.

In 2014–2015, Navy F/A-18 crews from the USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group, training off the U.S. East Coast, reported frequent encounters with unidentified objects, including near-midair collisions. Lt. Ryan Graves, an F/A-18F pilot in the group, says the objects appeared when the squadron's jets received the APG-79 radar, were first taken for false tracks, and were then also picked up by the targeting pod and missile seekers; he testified that they held stationary in hurricane-force winds and then accelerated past Mach 1, and that a cube inside a sphere passed within 50 feet of a two-ship, after which a safety report and a survey found four other unreported near misses.

Two infrared videos from these operations, "Gimbal" and "GoFast", were published by *The New York Times* in 2017–2018 and officially released by the Department of Defense on 27 April 2020 as "unidentified". Rear Adm. Tim Gallaudet, then Oceanographer of the Navy, testified that a secure email about the safety concerns vanished from his and the other recipients' inboxes the day after it arrived; Jay Stratton, who says he was the first to receive the videos in the Pentagon, took them to Air Force colleagues to rule out a U.S. system and told the press they were unidentified.

The counter-evidence concerns GoFast and Gimbal separately. NASA's 2023 study and AARO's 2025 case resolution put the GoFast object at about 13,000 feet, moving at wind speed, with no anomalous speed, which Gallaudet concedes while holding that the object itself is unexplained; neither identifies it. AARO has issued no resolution for Gimbal: Marik von Rennenkampff says Sean Kirkpatrick told him privately it was a balloon reflecting sunlight, which he disputes because the video was taken at night and the aircrew described the object moving against 120-knot winds, while the analyst Mick West attributes the rotation to infrared glare and the camera's gimbal mechanism. Graves testified about the encounters to the House Oversight Committee in July 2023.

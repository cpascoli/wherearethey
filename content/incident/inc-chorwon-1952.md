Before dawn on 31 May 1952, during the Korean War, two airmen on guard duty at a radar site just south of Chorwon (Cheorwon) watched a glowing object descend, stop, dart back and forth and climb away, with a pulsing sound like a jet. At the same time an F-94 night fighter of the 319th Fighter-Interceptor Squadron tried to intercept a brilliant white light in the area: by the crew's account it climbed to meet them, traded head-on passes and turned with the fighter up to 30,000 feet, then outclimbed and outran it. Neither the fighter's radar nor the ground radar could paint it.

Project Blue Book filed the main sighting as Unknown, with a faint possibility of a balloon. Far East Air Forces intelligence noted that the crew's and the ground observers' accounts did not match, suggested a light carried by a high-performance aircraft, and recorded that no explanation had been found for the ground radar's failure to detect it. In the same weeks F-94 crews over Korea chased lighted balloons, and shot one down.

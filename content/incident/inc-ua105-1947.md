On the evening of 4 July 1947, ten days after Kenneth Arnold's sighting, the crew of United Air Lines Flight 105 from Boise to Seattle, Captain Emil J. Smith and First Officer Ralph Stevens, with a stewardess they called forward to confirm, watched five objects, and then a second group of four or five, flying in loose formation against the sunset near Emmett, Idaho. They followed them for about 45 miles before the objects disappeared.

As experienced airline pilots, the crew made one of the most cited reports of the 1947 wave; the captain said they were not aircraft, smoke or clouds. The Air Force's opinion was that, at sunset, they could have been aircraft, balloons, birds or an illusion, and that the information was insufficient. The crew's names come from NICAP; they are blacked out in the Blue Book copy.

*Sources: Project Blue Book case file (U.S. National Archives) · Oregon Journal, 5 July 1947 · [NICAP](https://www.nicap.org/470704emmett_dir.htm)*

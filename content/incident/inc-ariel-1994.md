On 16 September 1994, during the mid-morning break at Ariel School, a private primary school in Ruwa near Harare, Zimbabwe, more than 60 children said they saw one or more silver disc-shaped craft land in the brush beyond the playground, and small beings with large eyes. Some said the beings communicated with them telepathically.

Two evenings earlier, on 14 September, a fireball had crossed the night sky of southern Africa; astronomers called it a meteor shower, and Jonathan McDowell's satellite catalogue records the re-entry that day of the second stage of the Zenit rocket that had launched Kosmos-2290. It is context only: it came about 37 hours before the school sighting, and Cynthia Hind's dozens of reports of it are a separate matter from what the children described.

The chronology of the interviews: the BBC's footage from the school is dated 19 September; Hind writes that she came on 20 September with a BBC reporter and interviewed 10 to 12 older children, after suggesting to the headmaster that the pupils draw what they had seen; John Mack, contacted through Tim Leach, arrived in November and interviewed 12 children. Mack's conviction that something extraordinary happened is his professional opinion, not a clinical finding: he published no study of the case. The documentary *Ariel Phenomenon* (2022) follows former pupils revisiting their accounts as adults.

Sceptical explanations include mass suggestion and contamination from group interviews, misidentification of a dust devil or a touring puppet show, and a former pupil's 2023 claim that he started the story, which contradicts his own 2008 account. No official record of the case has been located in the archive.

Shortly after 10 pm on 24 March 1997, dozens of callers around Bolsterstone, on the moors north-west of Sheffield, reported a low-flying aircraft, an orange glow and an explosion. South Yorkshire Police treated it as an air crash: a police helicopter, an RAF Sea King from Leconfield and the Peak District mountain rescue teams searched the Howden Moors through the night and the next morning, with a temporary danger area declared over the reservoir, and found nothing. No aircraft was missing. The British Geological Survey's sensors had recorded a sonic boom from the south at 2206Z.

A year later the Sheffield Star's David Clarke wrote to the MoD, and Helen Jackson MP tabled questions. The MoD's answer admitted that military low flying had been booked over northern England that evening and that it could not say whether aircraft were in the area, but called the occurrence unexplained and said it had no record of RAF or NATO aircraft causing sonic events. The RAF Police inquiry that followed, which Clarke later obtained, concluded that the exercise explained most of the sightings, that the booms could not be tied to any aircraft, and closed the case.

*Sources: MoD file DEFE 24/2018, pp. 232–254 (The National Archives, opened March 2011); DEFE 24/2071, pp. 7–9 (opened August 2011); Hansard, 30 March 1998; David Clarke's account of his investigation, which quotes the 1998 P&SS report (not in the files on hand).*

On the evening of 4 August 1990, two young men working as chefs in Pitlochry went walking on the moors above the hamlet of Calvine in Perthshire. They later said a large, diamond-shaped object hung silently above the glen for several minutes; they sheltered by a fence under some trees and took six photographs, during which a jet — identified by the MoD as an RAF Harrier — made several passes around it. The object then rose vertically at great speed and disappeared.

The men gave their pictures to Scotland's *Daily Record*, which sent a print to Craig Lindsay, the RAF's press officer in Scotland, and the negatives to the Ministry of Defence. Lindsay spoke to one of the witnesses and reported to the MoD's UFO desk, Sec(AS). In September 1990 the images were examined by Defence Intelligence and RAF photo analysts, a briefing was prepared for ministers, and the negatives were returned to the newspaper — which, to the puzzlement of its own news editor, never published the story. The negatives have never resurfaced, the witnesses have never been identified, and the MoD has withheld their names until 2076.

The case was known only through Nick Pope's 1996 book and poor photocopies released by the UK National Archives in 2009 until the researcher David Clarke traced Lindsay, who had kept a print of the best image for 32 years. Published in 2022 and now held by Sheffield Hallam University, it shows a sharp-edged diamond with a small jet beside it. A photographic analysis found it to be a genuine, unmanipulated image of a scene before the camera, but could not say what the object was. Explanations range from a secret military aircraft — the UK government says it authorised no U.S. "Aurora" flights and has no evidence of any — to a hoax with a small model hung from the trees near the camera.

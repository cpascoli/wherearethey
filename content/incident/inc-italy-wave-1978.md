In the second half of October 1978 fishermen of San Benedetto del Tronto, on the central Adriatic coast, began reporting strange things at sea: columns of water, then luminous discs at night and confused radar. On 9 November a Coast Guard patrol boat off Silvi Marina reported a light red, rocket-like object rising from the sea for about four seconds, with nothing on its radar. A deputy, Falco Accame, put a written question to the government on 20 November, and the Defence ministry sent minesweepers and patrol boats; the Navy patrols, by the minister's answer as transcribed by a private group, found nothing unusual and stopped on 21 November.

The reports then moved inland and spread across Italy. The Italian Air Force's own table lists 69 reports for 1978, far more than any other year, and 47 of them fall between 19 November and 31 December, most made by Carabinieri: pre-dawn lights over Sicily and Calabria on 11 December, eight reports on 14 December from Apulia to the Marche. They came from the whole country, Sicily most of all, so "Adriatic wave" fits only its beginning. Every entry is catalogued O.V.N.I., which records that the data in the archive did not identify the object, not what it was. The Air Force says that after this wave the Prime Minister, Giulio Andreotti, made it the body that collects and checks OVNI reports.

No official explanation of the wave as a whole is on file. The harbour-masters said they had detected nothing beyond what the fishermen described, and one put the radar trouble down to more powerful land stations.

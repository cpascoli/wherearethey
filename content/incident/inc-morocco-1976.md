In the early hours of 19 September 1976, people across Morocco, from Agadir in the south to Kenitra, Meknes and the Fez region in the north, reported a silent, luminous object shedding sparks as it crossed the sky, roughly parallel to the Atlantic coast. The Royal Gendarmerie collected the reports and drawings; one of its own officers, Major Mohamed Lissaoui, said he had watched it near Kenitra. King Hassan II sent the Gendarmerie's commander, Col. Housni Benslimane, to ask the U.S. ambassador for any information Washington had, and the embassy cabled the request to the State Department on 25 September.

The Department answered on 5 October that it knew of no U.S. aircraft or satellite activity that could account for it and, while offering no definitive explanation, judged that it was most likely a spectacular meteor or a re-entering piece of a satellite: the timing, the slow flat path, the silence and the burning fragments fit a re-entry, though no re-entry record was found. The same night, Iranian Air Force F-4s were scrambled after a bright object over Tehran.

*Sources: State Department telegrams RABAT 5209 (25 Sep 1976), STATE 244918 (2 Oct 1976) and STATE 247538 (5 Oct 1976), NARA Central Foreign Policy Files (AAD)*

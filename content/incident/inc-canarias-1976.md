On the evening of 19 November 1976 a huge, slowly expanding halo of light appeared over the Atlantic near the Canary Islands. It was watched from the Spanish Navy's training ship *Juan Sebastián Elcano*, from an Air Force T-12 (apparently with the general commanding the Canary Islands Air Zone aboard), from a Spantax airliner and by crowds on Gran Canaria. The Air Force's investigating judge took 14 statements, ruled out balloons, weather and jet exhaust, and concluded that the phenomenon was real and its nature unknown. The general wrote that no flying object had been seen, only an unidentified optical phenomenon, perhaps the work of a foreign power. The released file blanks his name, but Lt. Gen. Carlos Dolz de Espejo described the sighting from his own cockpit on Antena 3 in 1996.

His guess was close. Eastern Test Range launch records, compiled by Jonathan McDowell and checked by V.-J. Ballester Olmos and Ricardo Campo, list Poseidon missiles fired from a US submarine at 19:08 and 19:09 GMT that evening, minutes before the halo was seen. The same authors match the Canary Islands "OVNIs" of November 1974, June 1976, March 1977 and March 1979 to Poseidon launches. A missile's exhaust cloud, lit by the sun high above the darkened sea, produces just such an expanding halo.

*Sources: Ejército del Aire, expediente 761119; Ballester Olmos & Campo, Revista de Aeronáutica y Astronáutica 701 (2001)*

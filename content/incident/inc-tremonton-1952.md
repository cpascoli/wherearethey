On 2 July 1952, U.S. Navy Warrant Officer Delbert C. Newhouse, a Navy photographer, was near Tremonton, Utah, when he saw a group of bright objects and filmed about 30 feet of 16 mm colour film with a handheld camera. He submitted the film to the Air Force, and it became one of the most studied films of the early UFO era: the Navy's Photographic Interpretation Center analysed it, and the CIA-sponsored Robertson Panel reviewed it in January 1953.

The Navy analysts found the objects hard to reconcile with natural phenomena or known aircraft, but the Air Force's later assessments favoured seagulls reflecting sunlight. Without fixed reference points, the film cannot establish distance, size or speed. The film and Project Blue Book's file were published in the 2026 PURSUE releases.

*Sources: PURSUE records DOW-UAP-D098, D102, D103, D105 and PR159 (Department of War/AARO, 2026)*

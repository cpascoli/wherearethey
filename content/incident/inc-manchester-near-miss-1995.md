At 6.48 pm on 6 January 1995 the two pilots of a Boeing 737 descending through 4,000 feet over the Pennines, on radar vectors for runway 24 at Manchester, saw a dark, wedge-shaped object with lights pass down the right-hand side of the aircraft, fast, close and in the opposite direction; the first officer ducked. They reported it at once to Manchester approach, which saw nothing on radar, and filed an airmiss report. The Civil Aviation Authority's joint airmiss working group investigated for a year with the radar and radio recordings, found no traffic, military or civil, that could account for it, and in 1996 recorded both the cause and the risk as “unassessable”.

The MoD's UFO desk received the Manchester controller's occurrence report, noted that the air defence staff had no concerns and treated it as a CAA matter; that is the extent of the papers in its files.

*Sources: MoD file DEFE 24/1961, pp. 381–382 (The National Archives, opened August 2009); Hansard, 18 December 1996; the working group's report as transcribed by BUFORA (a secondary copy; the report itself, Joint Airmiss Working Group report 1/95, is in the House of Commons Library).*

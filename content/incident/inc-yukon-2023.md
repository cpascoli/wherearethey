On 11 February 2023, a week after the Chinese high-altitude balloon was shot down off South Carolina and a day after another object was downed over northern Alaska, a U.S. Air Force F-22 assigned to NORAD shot down a small object flying at about 40,000 feet over central Yukon. NORAD had detected it over Alaska the evening before; Canadian and U.S. fighters were scrambled, and Prime Minister Trudeau and President Biden both ordered it brought down. Defence Minister Anita Anand said it had unlawfully entered Canadian airspace, posed a reasonable threat to civilian flight and appeared to be "a small, cylindrical object", and that this was, to the best of her knowledge, the first time a NORAD operation had downed an aerial object. The Pentagon said an AIM-9X Sidewinder was used.

The Canadian Armed Forces and the RCMP searched the wilderness between Dawson City and Mayo for the wreckage. Within days the Chief of the Defence Staff was calling it a "suspected balloon", and on 17 February the RCMP called off the search: the most likely area had been searched without finding debris, snow had fallen, and the object was not believed to justify extraordinary efforts. Nothing was ever recovered.

On 16 February the President gave the intelligence community's assessment that the Yukon, Alaska and Lake Huron objects were most likely private, recreational or research balloons. A reporter later linked the Yukon object to a missing amateur pico balloon; that link is unconfirmed. The next day's Lake Huron shoot-down is the sister case; unlike it, no Yukon footage is in the Department of War's PURSUE releases.

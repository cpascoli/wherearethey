Late on 5 August 1952, control tower operators at Haneda Air Force Base in Tokyo watched a brilliant light over Tokyo Bay for about an hour, through binoculars a bright disc set in a larger dark round shape with smaller lights along its lower edge. Tachikawa's tower reported it too. The Tokyo GCI radar at Shiroi tracked a target in the area, orbiting, hovering and at one point splitting into three, and an F-94 night fighter vectored onto it held a radar contact for about 90 seconds before the target accelerated away. The fighter crew never saw it.

Far East Air Forces intelligence called it the first sighting in the Far East to combine visual and radar contact over an extended period, and Project Blue Book filed it as Unknown. The Condon Report later argued that the light was most probably a bright star, Capella, low on the horizon and distorted by an unusual optical effect, and that the radar returns were anomalous propagation, unconnected with the light; it conceded that complete weather data were not available.

*Sources: Project Blue Book case file, U.S. National Archives (local copy) · [Condon Report, Section III, Chapter 5 (1969)](https://files.ncas.org/condon/text/s3chap05.htm)*

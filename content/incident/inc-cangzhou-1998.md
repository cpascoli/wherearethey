On the night of 19 October 1998, according to Chinese press accounts, radar at a PLA Air Force airfield in Cangzhou, Hebei, picked up an unidentified object over the field, and a JJ-6 jet trainer crewed by deputy regiment commander Liu Ming and group commander Hu Shaoheng was sent after it. The object is described as a round, hat-shaped craft with rows of lights underneath that kept above the climbing jet, reaching about 20,000 m while the jet was at 12,000 m, until low fuel forced the jet back. About 160 people on the ground are said to have seen it.

The fullest account on file is a 2010 article in the Changjiang Daily, republished by the state broadcaster's news site CNTV, whose reporter interviewed the commander who directed the chase ("Commander Li") in March 1999. It says the crew asked whether to shoot the object down and were told not to rush and first to identify it, rather than flatly refused. The case is often called China's only officially confirmed UFO sighting, but no PLA or government statement has been found, and the original 1998 Hebei Daily report has not been retrieved; the pilots' names and the radar details come from journalists, not released records. No conventional explanation has been established either.

*Sources: CNTV / Changjiang Daily (25 Oct 2010) · Chinanews / Qianjiang Evening News, interview with Wang Sichao (26 Jul 2010)*

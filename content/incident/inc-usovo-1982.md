On the evening of 4 October 1982, servicemen of a Soviet Strategic Rocket Forces division around Usovo and Byelokorovichi, in Ukraine's Zhytomyr region, watched clusters of golden lights flash, move and fade in the northern sky for about two hours. That night a communications major logged a spontaneous illumination of all the indicators on his unit's equipment. Because a warning light on the missile launch system had also come on, the division reported to the General Staff, and a commission from Moscow arrived the next morning.

The case became famous through George Knapp. In the early 1990s he obtained the officers' statements and interviewed the commission's Col. Boris Sokolov, and in 1994 he filmed missile engineer Vladimir Platunov describing a silent disc. In his 2025 congressional testimony Knapp said launch codes had been entered by "something or someone". The officers' statements say less: lights in the sky, and an indicator event their own major put down to a power surge.

Sokolov and physicist Yuli Platov, who ran the Soviet programme, later wrote that the commission identified flares dropped over a bombing range 400 km away and that the panel fault was a coincidence. Their paper gives a different date (1983) and place (Khmelnytskyi), so the identification with Usovo rests on the matching details.

*Sources: Usovo witness statements (KLAS-TV, 2026); Platov & Sokolov, Vestnik RAN 70(6), 2000; Knapp 1994 field footage; Knapp written testimony, 2025*

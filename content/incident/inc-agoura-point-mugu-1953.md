On the evening of 16 December 1953, Lockheed's chief engineer Clarence “Kelly” Johnson, later the founder of the Skunk Works, and his wife watched a black, elliptical object hang motionless over the sea to the west of their ranch near Agoura, California, then climb away fast. At the same time a Lockheed flight-test crew on a Navy WV-2 test flight saw a dark object, like a crescent or a large flying wing, apparently stationary off Point Mugu at about their altitude; it later accelerated due west and vanished. Johnson heard their account the next morning, after giving his own.

Johnson had first taken the object for a lenticular cloud and rejected that when it did not move or change shape; the test pilot's first impression was also a cloud, until the crew judged its edges too sharp and its appearance too constant. Johnson sent the five signed reports to the Air Technical Intelligence Center in January 1954, saying they had not been released to the press. The Air Force's record card concludes “Lenticular Cloud.” The file was released in PURSUE release 08 in November 2026, among the government's unresolved records.

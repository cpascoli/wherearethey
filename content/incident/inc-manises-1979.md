At about 23:00 on 11 November 1979, the crew of TAE flight 297, a Supercaravelle carrying 109 passengers from Salzburg to the Canary Islands, saw red lights that seemed to approach the aircraft at collision speed and then follow it. Radar stations in Madrid and Barcelona could offer no explanation, and Capt. Francisco Javier Lerdo de Tejada diverted to Valencia-Manises, often described as the first emergency landing of a commercial flight because of a UFO.

A Spanish Air Force Mirage F1 was scrambled from Los Llanos Air Base at Albacete. The Air Force file names its pilot only by callsign; later accounts identify him as Capt. Fernando Cámara. The file records that for more than an hour he chased a red light and then a flashing red, green and white one over Valencia without ever closing on them or finding them on his radar, while suffering siren-like radio interference and intermittent locks on his radar-warning receiver; he told the inquiry the interference was like what he had met over the carrier Nimitz during exercise CRISEX-79 nine days earlier. Later accounts that he reached Mach 1.4 and that his systems jammed as he tried to lock a missile on the object do not appear in the file.

The Spanish Air Force file, declassified in 1994, found no confirmed radar contact correlated with the lights: brief echoes on the EVA 5 height-finder were judged probable clutter, and only the airliner's track was recorded. Its investigating general found "no proof to admit the presence of UFOs", and a later note for Parliament called the object possibly "simply an optical illusion". Researchers such as Juan Antonio Fernández Peris have attributed the lights to industrial flare stacks and bright stars, explanations the witnesses reject.

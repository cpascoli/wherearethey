On 5 March 2004 the crew of a Mexican Air Force Merlin C-26 on a routine anti-drug patrol over the state of Campeche tracked objects first seen on radar and recorded them with the aircraft's infrared camera. The video shows up to eleven bright lights that seem to hover in formation, dart about and, at one point, surround the aircraft. The crew could not see them with the naked eye. The Defence Ministry (SEDENA) gave the tape to the UFO journalist Jaime Maussan, who presented it on 11 May 2004 as proof of UFOs, and the ministry confirmed to Reuters that the footage was its own.

The Defence Secretary, Gen. Ricardo Vega García, said the ministry had analysed the video, did not call the objects UFOs, and that the conclusions were Maussan's, not SEDENA's. No official finding was ever published.

The explanation most analysts accept came within weeks: the lights were the gas flares of the Cantarell offshore oil platforms, tens of kilometres behind the aircraft, filmed in hot, humid air near sunset. The match rests on the camera's recorded bearing, the flames that the camera resolves at full zoom, and the Mexican pilot Alejandro Franz's photographs of the flares. It is a private analysis, and the radar contacts were not recorded. Earlier suggestions from UNAM researchers, atmospheric electrical flashes or ball lightning, were not backed by any published analysis.

*Sources: Reuters (NBC News), 11 May 2004 · AP, 12 and 14 May 2004 · R. Sheaffer, Skeptical Inquirer, Sept/Oct 2004*

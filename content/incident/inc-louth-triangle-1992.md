Late on the afternoon of 9 December 1992 drivers on the coast road near Louth, in Lincolnshire, pulled over to watch three lights arranged in a triangle, which many took to be the corners of a single dark craft, hovering silently over the coast to the north-west. After a few minutes it turned on its axis and shot away upwards and to the north-west at a speed the witnesses guessed at 500 mph. Dozens of people phoned the local paper, and the officer commanding the RAF bombing range at Donna Nook was among those who saw it.

The MoD's UFO desk took the report from the paper's reporter a week later; the form, in the file of the Defence Intelligence branch DI55, is the only trace of the case there, and nothing on it records a follow-up with the RAF officer. A week after Louth, two controllers in Heathrow's tower reported a black inverted boomerang; the National Archives' guide links the two.

*Source: MoD file DEFE 31/181, pp. 280–281 (The National Archives, opened March 2009); the form is handwritten and the witnesses' names are redacted.*

On 26 July 2002 two F-16 fighters were scrambled near Andrews Air Force Base, Maryland, after reports of a bright blue light over the Washington area, as the *Los Angeles Times* reported two days later. It is in the archive because the military confirmed the scramble.

A contemporary news report says two F-16s of the D.C. Air National Guard's 113th Wing went up at about 1 a.m. after NORAD picked up a “radar track of concern”, and that NORAD and the Air National Guard confirmed activity near Andrews at about the same time. Residents told local radio of a bright blue ball of light, sometimes orange, with jets right on its tail. When the fighters arrived, they found nothing. Historian Richard Dolan later said the Air Force concluded it could have been “any number of” things.

No identification of the light was announced, and the archive holds no official record of the event beyond the press reports.

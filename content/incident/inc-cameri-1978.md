In September 1978, during a month of UFO reports across Italy, Lt. Col. Giulio Mainini, commander of the 21° Gruppo caccia intercettori at Cameri (Novara), reported an intense light seen from his F-104. La Stampa carried his account on 22 September. He had taken off around 20:00 heading south. Between Cameri and Genoa, after turning towards Piacenza, he saw a very bright point that seemed to grow and approach. It was too bright for an aircraft or a sunlit balloon and too low for a star. He kept to his course, and after three minutes it was gone. He filed a confidential report with the base commander. The Air Force's archive table records a report by Air Force personnel from Novara at about 20:00 on 20 September 1978: a white luminous point moving north to south, catalogued O.V.N.I. The table names no witness.

His later accounts, recorded in 2019, 2020 and 2024, tell a bigger story. In them the light was below him, the radar saw nothing, and he was cleared to chase it, at supersonic speed or Mach 2, for 15 to 30 minutes, as far as abeam Tuscany, until fuel ran short. He also says thousands of people saw it from the ground in Liguria. None of this is in the 1978 report of his words, which says he did not change course. The chase, the radar call and the crowds rest on his own account four decades later, and on what a colleague told him.

No official explanation is on file. In 1978 Cameri officers offered a sodium cloud, the re-entry of Pegasus 1 and a lithium-rocket experiment as possibilities. Mainini himself says it may have been a refraction of light. He does not believe in UFOs, and says Italy's radar archive never confirmed a sighting.

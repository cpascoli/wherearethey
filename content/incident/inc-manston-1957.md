On the night of 20 May 1957, by the account of the pilot, Lt. Milton Torres, two U.S. Air Force F-86D Sabres of the 406th Fighter Interceptor Wing were scrambled from RAF Manston in Kent to intercept a radar target that RAF ground controllers had been tracking over East Anglia. Torres says he was ordered to fire his full load of 24 rockets, had the order authenticated, and locked on to a return “the proportions of a flying aircraft carrier”, which reversed and left his radar seconds before he could fire. He says a civilian debriefed him the next day and swore him to secrecy.

The story reached the Ministry of Defence in 1988, when a British solicitor sent it the narratives of Torres and of the second pilot, written after the two met at a reunion; the MoD found no record of the incident, and neither did the U.S. Air Force. The papers, with the pilots' names redacted, were released at the National Archives on 20 October 2008 (DEFE 24/1931, 24/1942), and Torres then went public. The second pilot's account differs on most details (a training flight, rearming at Bentwaters, several targets, no lock-on). No explanation has been established; the solicitor's suggestion that it was an electronic-warfare exercise that spoofed the radar has never been confirmed.

*Sources: MoD files DEFE 24/1931 and DEFE 24/1942 (The National Archives, released October 2008) · AP via NBC News, 20 October 2008 · Torres's [interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xk0INH_DI1M)*

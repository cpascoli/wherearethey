On the evening of 30 November 1973, a private pilot landing at Turin-Caselle was warned by the tower of an unidentified object over the approach path and was guided towards it. Riccardo Marano, flying a Piper back from Genoa, said he saw a brilliant, colour-changing globe that kept its distance, and followed it as far as Voghera before fuel ran short. The commander of the military airport confirmed that the object had been picked up, describing a light that looked like a dim star drifting west, and an Alitalia captain landing from Paris said he saw a light brighter than a star.

The Italian Air Force's archive confirms the core: a report at 19:00 that day from Caselle Torinese by the civilian pilot of a private aircraft and Air Force personnel, catalogued as unidentified. Its table gives no radar data and names no one.

The contemporary press also carries the counter-evidence: the commander of the Mortara air-defence radar said his radar saw nothing, and the claim that it had tracked the object at Mach 4 was unattributed. The airport commander's own description, a dim star-like light, is much plainer than the newspaper headlines. Later sightings that week were put down to Venus by a Turin astronomer. Lists that name the airliners AZ 345 and AZ 043 with captains "Tranquillo" and "Mezzetti", airborne weather-radar tracks or a vertical climb out of radar cover go beyond what the 1973 reports say.

*Sources: Aeronautica Militare, Archivio OVNI 1972–1990, p. 11 · Stampa Sera, 1 and 3 December 1973; La Stampa, 2 December 1973 (Archivio storico La Stampa)*

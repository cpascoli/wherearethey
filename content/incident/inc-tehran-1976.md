In the early hours of 19 September 1976, residents of Tehran's Shemiran district phoned Mehrabad Airport about a brightly lit object in the sky, and the Imperial Iranian Air Force scrambled two F-4 Phantom II jets. The first, flown by Lt. Yadollah (Yaddi) Nazeri, lost its instruments and UHF and intercom communications as it closed to about 25 nautical miles; they came back as it turned away. The second, flown by Maj. Parviz Jafari with 1st Lt. Jalal Damirian, locked the object on radar at 27 nautical miles. As Jafari prepared to fire an AIM-9 Sidewinder, his weapons control panel and communications failed, and the crew reported smaller objects separating from the main one.

The U.S. Defense Attaché Office in Tehran reported it to the Defense Intelligence Agency in a cable of 23 September 1976, which the Joint Chiefs of Staff message centre distributed to the Secretary of Defense, the Secretary of State, the CIA, the NSA, the White House and the service chiefs. The cable names only Brigadier General Yousefi, the assistant deputy commander of operations who ordered the scramble, and says its information came from a conversation with one of the F-4 pilots; the crew names given above come from later accounts. A DIA evaluation form attached to it rates the information as confirmed by other sources and of high value and calls the case a classic for the study of the phenomenon, citing the witnesses' credibility, radar confirmation and electromagnetic effects on three aircraft. The documents were later released under the Freedom of Information Act, and Jafari described the encounter publicly at the National Press Club in 2007.

The sceptic Philip J. Klass attributed the light to the planet Jupiter and the failures to ordinary equipment faults, noting the first F-4's history of electrical problems; others have pointed to meteors that night.

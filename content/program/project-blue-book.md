Project Blue Book was the U.S. Air Force's study of unidentified flying objects from March 1952 to 17 December 1969, run from Wright-Patterson Air Force Base in Ohio, with the astronomer J. Allen Hynek as its scientific consultant throughout. It followed the earlier Projects Sign and Grudge.

In January 1953 the CIA convened the Robertson Panel, which reviewed Blue Book's case histories and films and concluded that UFOs posed no direct threat to national security. It recommended a public education and "debunking" effort through the mass media, even the Disney corporation, and that civilian UFO groups be monitored for subversive activities; the CIA's own 1997 history notes that the agency kept its sponsorship of the panel secret, which "would later cause the Agency major problems relating to its credibility". Air Force Regulation 200-2, issued that August, gave field investigations to the Air Defense Command's 4602d Air Intelligence Service Squadron and let the press be told only about sightings identified as familiar objects. By 1969 the Air Force stated that UFO reports which could affect national security were handled under JANAP 146 or AFM 55-11 and were not part of the Blue Book system.

Over its lifetime it collected 12,618 reports, of which 701 remained "unidentified". After the Condon Committee concluded that further study was unlikely to yield major discoveries, the Secretary of the Air Force closed the project, stating that no UFO had ever indicated a threat to national security, that there was no evidence of technology beyond contemporary knowledge, and no evidence that the unidentified sightings were extraterrestrial vehicles. Some of its records appear in the PURSUE releases.

The Sky Canada Project was a review by the Office of the Chief Science Advisor of Canada, Mona Nemer, of how reports of unidentified aerial phenomena (UAP) from the public are received and handled in Canada. Launched in the fall of 2022 and announced in early 2023, it was spurred by public interest and by developments in the United States. The office consulted federal departments and agencies (among them National Defence, NORAD, the RCAF, the RCMP, Transport Canada, NAV CANADA and the National Research Council), citizen groups and experts, compared the practice of G7 and Commonwealth countries, and commissioned an online survey of Canadians from Earnscliffe Strategies in 2024.

It deliberately did not investigate sightings or the nature of UAP: it set out neither to prove nor disprove extraterrestrial visitation, and collected no photos, videos or individual reports. Asked about alleged secret reverse-engineering programs, the office said it had neither sought nor encountered evidence of any in Canada.

Its final report (June 2025) found that UAP reports are scattered among many federal and private bodies with no standard system, no public reporting platform and little analysis or scientific involvement, and made fourteen recommendations: a federal lead and a dedicated public service, better reporting by pilots and air-traffic controllers, open data, citizen science, and cooperation with AARO, NASA, France's GEIPAN and Chile's SEFAA.

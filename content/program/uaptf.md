The Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Task Force was formally established by Deputy Secretary of Defense David Norquist in August 2020, approved on 4 August and announced on 14 August, under the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security with the Office of Naval Intelligence. Its job was to detect, analyse and catalogue UAP that could threaten U.S. national security. Scott Bray told the House in 2022 that it was built on the Navy's initial efforts to respond to aviators' reports from the training ranges, and AARO's historical report says the Navy was asked to lead it. Its work produced the ODNI's *Preliminary Assessment* of June 2021: 144 reports, one of them identified.

It is generally described as AATIP's functional successor rather than a formal continuation: Luis Elizondo says the AATIP work continued until he resigned in 2017 and that Jay Stratton took over, while AARO says AATIP was never an official program. Stratton was the task force's first director; George Knapp says it was "casually created" after Stratton's superiors asked for briefings in the wake of the 2017 New York Times story. David Grusch served as its NRO representative before his whistleblower complaint, and Karl Nell as the Army's. Tim Gallaudet calls it the first comprehensive whole-of-government approach to UAP.

In November 2021 its duties passed to the AOIMSG, and the July 2022 memorandum that created AARO ordered the task force disestablished. Colm Kelleher describes the handover as abrupt and under completely different leadership; AARO credits the task force with destigmatizing reporting and with methods that led to the identification of China's high-altitude balloons.

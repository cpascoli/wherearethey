"Legacy program" is the name given since 2023 to the alleged long-running, highly compartmented effort, government and contractor, to retrieve and reverse-engineer craft and biological remains of non-human origin, said to date back to the 1940s and to be hidden from Congress. The term entered proposed law in the UAP Disclosure Act of July 2023, which defined a "legacy program" as all government, industry, academic and private endeavours to collect, exploit or reverse-engineer technologies of unknown origin or examine non-human biological evidence; the enacted FY2024 NDAA kept only a narrower records requirement. The program's existence has not been officially confirmed. This entry describes what is alleged; the people who make the wider allegation, and the official answers to it, are under [crash retrieval](/concept/crash-retrieval).

As its proponents describe it, the program is an unacknowledged, possibly waived special access program or a controlled access program, with the work done inside aerospace contractors. The unverified Wilson–Davis notes of 2002 have a contractor's program manager telling Adm. Thomas Wilson that it was "a special subset of the unacknowledged/carve-outs/waived programs", "buried/covered by conventional SAPs". Eric Davis told the May 2025 congressional briefing that it is hidden under presidential emergency action directives from Eisenhower, outside the Freedom of Information Act, and funded through shell companies and sole-source contracts to defence firms. David Grusch testified under oath in 2023 that he was told of a multi-decade program and denied access, and has since said the material is spread across big contractors, the CIA, the Office of Naval Intelligence and federally funded research centres; Luis Elizondo, in *Imminent* and under oath in 2024, names the contractors; Jay Stratton says the program is "comprised of CIA, the Air Force, the Department of Energy, and the defense contractor community" and keeps its secret from Congress and the president. KONA BLUE, a DHS special access program proposed around 2011 by AAWSAP veterans, is the one documented proposal for such an effort; it was never approved.

The counter-evidence is official and specific. AARO's 2024 historical report, which never uses the phrase, found that interviewees had "mistakenly associated" authentic classified programs with UAP, traced one claimed touch of an alien craft to an F-117, and called the hidden-from-Congress claim "in large part, the result of circular reporting". Chris Mellon, who sat on the Defense Department committee that reviews every special access program and reports them to Congress, "never saw anything related to UAP", though he notes that workarounds exist, and one of his own crash-retrieval sources told him the program had been closed; Davis says he was told the CIA shut its effort down in 1989. AARO's director Jon Kosloski asked the Senate in November 2024 to encourage witnesses on "potential historic or ongoing legacy programs" to come forward, and former Senate staffer Kirk McConnell says the key is first-hand sources, who fear for their clearances, careers and safety. Sean Kirkpatrick, answering Stratton's memoir in October 2026: "There is no legacy program."

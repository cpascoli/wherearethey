The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office is the Pentagon office responsible for UAP reports across the air, sea, space and transmedium domains. Required by section 1683 of the Fiscal Year 2022 NDAA, it was approved in a Deputy Secretary of Defense memorandum of 15 July 2022, published on 20 July, which made it the Department's focal point for UAP, replaced the short-lived AOIMSG and ordered the Navy's UAP Task Force wound down. Its directors have been the physicist Sean Kirkpatrick (2022–2023), Tim Phillips as acting director, and Jon T. Kosloski, who arrived on detail from the NSA on 26 August 2024.

AARO is the hub of the official counter-evidence. Kirkpatrick told the Senate in April 2023 that the office held about 650 cases, that only a very small percentage looked anomalous and that it had found "no credible evidence" of extraterrestrial activity or off-world technology. Its *Historical Record Report, Volume 1* (March 2024) found no empirical evidence of reverse-engineering of extraterrestrial technology by the government or private companies, and identified KONA BLUE as a proposed, never approved, special access program. Kosloski, in November 2024, put the caseload at over 1,600 reports with no verifiable evidence of extraterrestrial technology, while adding that the office holds "some very anomalous objects"; its February 2025 case resolution put the GoFast object at about 13,000 feet moving at roughly wind speed, and its FY2025 annual report counts 1,870 reports, every resolved case prosaic, with a few reports from national-security sites whose performance would exceed the state of the art if validated.

Its critics are filed here too. David Grusch testified in 2023 that Kirkpatrick's "no credible evidence" statement was not accurate and later said the office "ghosted" him; Tim Gallaudet called a meeting with AARO an "influence operation"; James Lacatski says its deputy director described KONA BLUE as a home for "paranormal UFO crap"; Dylan Borland disputes the memorandum AARO wrote of his interview; Rep. Anna Paulina Luna called Kirkpatrick "a documented liar". No evidence presented publicly has settled the dispute, and Gallaudet concedes AARO's GoFast parallax analysis while rejecting its wider reading.

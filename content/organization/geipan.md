GEIPAN is the French space agency CNES's office for unidentified aerospace phenomena. It began in 1977 as GEPAN, was renamed SEPRA in the late 1980s, and became GEIPAN in 2005. It collects reports from the public, the gendarmerie, the Air Force and civil aviation, investigates them, and publishes its case files online with a classification from A (fully identified) to D (unexplained after investigation).

Its files are the source for several cases here: the Trans-en-Provence landing trace of 1981, the Air France 3532 sighting of 1994, the 1951 Orange-Caritat pilots, and smaller D cases such as Port-Gentil and Neuvelle-lès-Lure. Its head, Frédéric Courtade, explains that D means a residual strangeness remains after the plausible hypotheses have been tested, and says Valensole remains a very strange phenomenon in GEIPAN's eyes to this day. Astronomer Jean-Claude Ribes spoke for it at the 2007 National Press Club panel.

A D classification records that no explanation was found, nothing more. GEIPAN's own files also list the weaknesses of its cases, such as late testimony or a single witness.

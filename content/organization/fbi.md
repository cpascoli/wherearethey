The Federal Bureau of Investigation is the U.S. domestic law-enforcement and counter-intelligence agency. It was among the first federal bodies to collect "flying disc" reports: its headquarters case file 62-HQ-83894 runs from June 1947 to July 1968 and holds investigative records, eyewitness testimony, photographs and press coverage, and its message of 8 July 1947 that the disc found near Roswell was being sent to Wright Field is one of only two contemporary records of the crash the GAO could find in 1995. The 1950 Guy Hottel memo, the most-viewed file in the FBI Vault, relays third-hand a story of three recovered saucers; the FBI itself notes that it was never investigated and does not mention Roswell. By 1974 the Bureau was telling inquirers that it did not collect information on UFO sightings.

The Bureau's files also hold the record's clearest counter-evidence on one famous document: when the "Majestic 12" papers circulated in 1988, the Air Force Office of Special Investigations told the FBI that the document was fabricated and "completely bogus", and the case was closed.

Since 2026 the FBI has been the second-largest contributor to the Department of War's PURSUE releases, with 105 records through release 07: the complete 1947–1968 file, a 1957 interview about a vertically rising vehicle in wartime Germany, FD-302 interview reports with service members and federal agents (Colorado Springs 2022, a September 2023 test-site sighting with an FBI Lab composite sketch, the 2026 western triangle), still images from government sensors submitted to AARO, phone videos from a witness the Bureau assessed as highly credible, and digital recreations it prepared for the Department of War. These are unresolved reports, not findings: the FBI assesses its witnesses, and AARO's descriptions say they carry no analytical judgment.

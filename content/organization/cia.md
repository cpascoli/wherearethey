The Central Intelligence Agency is the main U.S. foreign-intelligence agency. Its UAP record begins in its own files: after the summer 1952 wave over Washington, its Office of Scientific Intelligence asked whether "flying saucers" had national-security implications, H. Marshall Chadwell told the DCI in December that "something was going on that must have immediate attention", and in January 1953 the Agency convened the Robertson Panel, which found no direct threat and recommended a public "debunking" effort. The Agency kept its sponsorship of the panel secret, and its 1997 history by Gerald Haines says this later cost it credibility. That history also records that U-2 and OXCART flights accounted for over half of UFO reports from the late 1950s through the 1960s, and that the Air Force made "misleading and deceptive statements" to protect them, while concluding that the CIA itself paid "only limited and peripheral attention" to the subject after the early 1950s. The Agency also funded the remote-viewing research at SRI that grew into Stargate, and its FOIA collection holds material such as a 1990 press note on Soviet air-defence sightings.

Its 23 PURSUE records, through release 07, are mostly of this kind: the Robertson Panel's correspondence, the U-2 and OXCART history, 1955 analyses of "unconventional aircraft", and foreign reports from Budapest, the USSR and Ladakh.

Against that account of a peripheral role stand the allegations of the disclosure witnesses. George Knapp told Congress under oath in September 2025 that the agencies which gave contractors recovered material, "CIA, I think primarily, told them to keep it quiet", and named Glenn Gaffney, a former CIA Director of Science and Technology; Eric Davis says Gaffney blocked a contractor from sharing crash-retrieval material with AAWSAP; Jay Stratton says the Legacy Program is "comprised of CIA, the Air Force, the Department of Energy, and the defense contractor community". These rest on their sources, and no document backing them has been made public. AARO's 2024 report says the former CIA official named by its interviewees denied any knowledge of the allegation on the record, that the named companies also denied it, and that it found no evidence of any such program; Sean Kirkpatrick says flatly that there is no legacy program.

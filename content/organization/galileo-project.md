The Galileo Project is a research programme led by the Harvard astrophysicist Avi Loeb that builds ground-based observatories to look systematically for unidentified objects and possible technological artefacts in the sky, publishing its methods and data openly. Stanford's Garry Nolan joined it in 2022.

Loeb told a congressional briefing in May 2025 that the project's observatories watch the sky in infrared, optical, radio and audio and now triangulate objects, with sites in Pennsylvania and Nevada to follow. At the same briefing he said: “We should invest a billion dollars in figuring out what is in our sky.” Chris Mellon cites its sensors as an example of collection outside government control.

It is a scientific project, not an official one. The UAP Science Advisory Council, which Loeb chairs, has found likely birds in about 8% of the PURSUE sensor videos released so far, in a preprint, not an official finding.

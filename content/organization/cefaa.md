CEFAA (Comité de Estudios de Fenómenos Aéreos Anómalos) is Chile's official committee for studying anomalous aerial phenomena, created in October 1997 within the country's civil aviation authority, the DGAC. Journalist Antonio Huneeus says it was placed there rather than under the air force because UFOs can be a risk to air safety. It collects and analyses reports from pilots, air-traffic controllers and the public, and is one of the longest-running government UAP offices in the world.

Gen. Ricardo Bermúdez directed it from its creation until he retired at the start of 2017, and Capt. Rodrigo Bravo of Chilean Army aviation worked with it. Its best-known case is the 2014 Chilean Navy helicopter video, which it studied for two years before most members agreed to call it a UAP: in Bermúdez's words, “We do not know what it was, but we do know what it was not.”

That verdict is contested. The French analysts CEFAA itself consulted concluded the object was most probably an airliner, and independent analysts later matched it to an Iberia flight leaving a contrail.

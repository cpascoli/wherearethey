NASA is the U.S. civilian space agency. It has never run a UFO investigation of its own; its part in the subject comes from its astronauts' reports, its 2022–23 independent study team on UAP, and the forty records it gave to the Department of War's PURSUE releases in 2026.

Those records are the spaceflight side of the story. They include the air-to-ground audio of Frank Borman's "bogey" on Gemini 7, the "fireflies" and "snowflakes" that John Glenn, Scott Carpenter, Wally Schirra and Gordon Cooper saw around their Mercury capsules, the jagged fragments Apollo 17's crew watched near their spent rocket stage, the light flashes Apollo crews saw with their eyes closed, and photographs from Apollo 12, Apollo 17 and the shuttle flight STS-80. Most of these have conventional explanations in the records themselves: ice and paint shed by the spacecraft, and cosmic rays striking the retina. Borman's bogey is left unidentified, and the Department of War has opened a case on the three dots in an Apollo 17 photograph.

NASA's independent study team, reporting in September 2023, found no conclusive evidence of an extraterrestrial origin for UAP and called for better, curated data. People on file with a NASA connection include the Apollo 14 astronaut Edgar Mitchell, the Mercury astronaut Gordon Cooper, the study team member Michael Gold, the former chief flight surgeon Gregory Rogers and the engineer Larry Lemke.

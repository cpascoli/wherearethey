The National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena (NICAP) was the largest American civilian UFO group of the 1950s and 1960s. Founded in Washington in 1956 and run from 1957 by the retired Marine major and writer Donald Keyhoe, it collected and investigated sighting reports through local subcommittees and campaigned against Air Force secrecy, pressing for the files to be opened and for congressional hearings. Its board included former military officers: the former CIA director Roscoe Hillenkoetter until 1962, and Dewey Fournet, who had monitored Project Blue Book for Air Force headquarters.

Its main publication, *The UFO Evidence* (1964), edited by Richard Hall, argued that the unexplained cases pointed to artificial objects under intelligent control and that the Air Force had played the evidence down. The CIA's own history says Agency officials worried about its pressure to release the Robertson panel report, and that in 1964–65 CIA officers obtained recent sighting reports from it. In April 1968 NICAP broke with the Air Force-funded University of Colorado study, accusing it of bias. The study's report concluded the opposite of NICAP's case: that nothing from two decades of UFO study had “added to scientific knowledge”, and the Air Force closed Blue Book in 1969.

The same year NICAP's board forced Keyhoe out, and the group declined until it was dissolved in 1980. The website nicap.org, cited in several files here for its case directories and document transcriptions, was created in December 1997 by Francis L. Ridge and describes itself as a “fully authorized NICAP site”; it is not the 1956–1980 organization.

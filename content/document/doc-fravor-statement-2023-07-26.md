Cmdr. David Fravor's written statement for the House Oversight hearing of 26 July 2023 gives his first-hand account of the November 2004 Nimitz encounter. Commanding Strike Fighter Squadron 41, he was leading a training sortie when the cruiser USS Princeton, which had tracked objects descending from above 80,000 feet to about 20,000 feet for two weeks, redirected his flight. Over a patch of white water, the four aircrew saw "a small white Tic Tac shaped object" with "no Rotors, No Rotor wash, or any visible flight control surfaces like wings." As he descended towards it, it climbed, aligned with his aircraft, then "rapidly accelerated and disappeared right in front of our aircraft"; the Princeton then picked it up at their combat air patrol point, about 60 miles away.

He writes that the incident "was never investigated, none of my crew were ever questioned, tapes were never taken", until Jay Stratton contacted him in 2009, and credits To The Stars and the December 2017 *New York Times* articles with removing the stigma.

His main argument is oversight: if programmes possess such technology, they must be overseen by Congress, as even unacknowledged waived programmes are. He concludes that the object "was far superior to anything that we had at the time, have today, or are looking to develop in the next 10+ years."

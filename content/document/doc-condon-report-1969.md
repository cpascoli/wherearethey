The final report of the University of Colorado's Air Force-funded UFO study, directed by the physicist Edward U. Condon, transmitted to the Secretary of the Air Force on 31 October 1968 and made available to the National Academy of Sciences' review panel on 15 November 1968. It runs to some 1,400 pages: Condon's conclusions and recommendations, a summary of the study, field studies, photographic, physical, optical and radar analyses, an attitude survey, 59 case studies, a history of UFOs and chapters on perception, optics, radar, balloons and statistics.

Condon concluded that 21 years of UFO study had added nothing to scientific knowledge and that further extensive study probably could not be justified on scientific grounds, though well-defined proposals should still be supported; that UFO reports posed no defense problem; that Blue Book was not needed for the defense function; and that there was no evidence of secrecy. Its case studies leave a few events unexplained, and the Lakenheath–Bentwaters case was judged probably to involve "at least one genuine UFO".

A National Academy of Sciences panel endorsed the report in January 1969, and the Air Force cited both in closing Project Blue Book that December. AARO notes that some scientists, among them J. Allen Hynek, criticised the report; the project itself had been accused of bias before it reported (see the Condon Committee file).

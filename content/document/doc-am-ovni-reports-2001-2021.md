The Italian Air Force's yearly tables of the OVNI reports it received from 2001 to 2021, one file per year (the 2019 file's last report is from November 2019; there is no file for 2020; the 2017 file lists no reports). Each year opens with a month-by-month count, then gives one entry per report: place, date, time, shape, colour, direction, speed, motion, altitude, weather and who sent the report ("pervenuta da"), and the Air Force's finding ("riscontri"). Most reports come from private citizens; a few come from military and civilian pilots, a military radar controller and the Carabinieri. The finding is usually that the data gathered from the Air Force's offices could not link the event to known flight or radiosonde activity or natural phenomena, and from 2018 the entries say outright that the event was therefore catalogued as O.V.N.I.

The tables name no witnesses. "O.V.N.I." records that no explanation was found, not extraordinary performance or origin; several entries name the likely cause themselves (a parhelion, sky lanterns, the Starlink satellites, a Soyuz re-entry). The files were uploaded to the Air Force site in November 2022 (the 2008 table was re-uploaded in October 2024 with the Terrasini photographs added) and are listed on its OVNI page; our copies come from Wayback Machine snapshots of May 2023 (2005: September 2023) and, for 2008, the October 2024 upload.

*Sources: Aeronautica Militare, yearly OVNI tables 2001–2021 (aeronautica.difesa.it/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/, via the Wayback Machine)*

facts:
  - text: A letter from Headquarters, Air Materiel Command (Wright Field) to the Commanding General, Army Air Forces, signed by Lt. Gen. Nathan Twining. It states that “the phenomenon reported is something real and not visionary or fictitious.”
    status: documented
    source: library/docs/doc-twining-memo-1947.pdf
  - text: It describes objects “probably approximating the shape of a disc, of such appreciable size as to appear to be as large as man-made aircraft”, and considers the possibility of a domestic secret project or a foreign nation with propulsion “outside of our domestic knowledge”.
    status: documented
    source: library/docs/doc-twining-memo-1947.pdf
  - text: It recommends that Army Air Forces headquarters issue a directive assigning “a priority, security classification and Code Name for a detailed study”. This became Project Sign.
    status: documented
    source: library/docs/doc-twining-memo-1947.pdf
  - text: It also notes the lack of physical evidence such as “crash recovered exhibits”. That wording is cited both by skeptics and by researchers who argue some recoveries were compartmented.
    status: documented
  - text: According to researcher Jan Aldrich, the opinion was likely drafted by Col. Howard McCoy’s T-2 Intelligence staff in reply to a request from A-2 (Brig. Gen. George Schulgen), and copies exist in several Air Force files, despite later Air Force denials that the letter existed.
    status: claimed
    source: https://www.nicap.org/twining_letter.htm

The earliest high-level official U.S. statement that UFOs were a real physical phenomenon. Classified SECRET at the time; it became public in the late 1960s.

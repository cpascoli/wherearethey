The report of Project Sign, the U.S. Air Force's first formal UFO study, prepared by the Technical Intelligence Division of Air Materiel Command at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base and released in February 1949. It summarises 243 domestic and 30 foreign reports, sorts the objects into four kinds, assesses what aerodynamics and propulsion they would need, and includes appendices by the astronomer J. Allen Hynek's Ohio State team, G. E. Valley of the Scientific Advisory Board and James Lipp of the Rand Project.

Its conclusions are cautious: no definite evidence either way, proof unlikely without the remains of a crashed object, many reports from reliable witnesses but none conclusive, and a recommendation to continue at a minimum level. Its appendices include an early official discussion of the idea that visitors might be drawn by atomic explosions, which the Rand study put at a thousand to one against. Our copy is the scan published on Wikimedia Commons, OCR'd for search; the OCR is imperfect.

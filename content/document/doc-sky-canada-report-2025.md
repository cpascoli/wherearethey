The final report of the Sky Canada Project, published in June 2025 by the Office of the Chief Science Advisor of Canada after a preview in January 2025. It reviews how reports of unidentified aerial phenomena from the public are received in Canada, the history of federal UFO reporting from Project Magnet to the end of the National Research Council's collection in 1995, the practice of other countries, and the results of a 2024 survey of Canadians. It opens with a message from the Chief Science Advisor, Mona Nemer, stressing that it concerns reporting services, not the phenomena themselves.

It finds UAP reports scattered across federal and private bodies, no standard system or public reporting platform, little analysis and little scientific involvement, and recommends fourteen measures: a federal lead and a dedicated service that would investigate cases and publish its analyses, better reporting in civil aviation, a public communications strategy against misinformation, open data and citizen science, and partnerships with AARO, NASA, GEIPAN and Chile's SEFAA.

It draws no conclusion that UAP are extraterrestrial; it notes a strong scientific consensus that they are not, while allowing that new evidence could change that view. Its background section recalls the 2023 shoot-downs, including the Yukon object, and lists Falcon Lake among Canada's best-known cases.

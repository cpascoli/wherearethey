The Sturrock panel was a scientific review of physical evidence in UFO reports, held as a workshop at the Pocantico Conference Center in Tarrytown, New York, from 29 September to 4 October 1997. Laurance S. Rockefeller's LSR Fund paid for it, the Society for Scientific Exploration administered it, and the Stanford physicist Peter Sturrock directed it. Eight investigators presented photographs, radar cases, vehicle and aircraft interference, ground traces, injuries and the Hessdalen measurements to a panel of nine scientists. It was not a government inquiry.

The panel found **no convincing evidence of unknown physical processes or of extraterrestrial intelligence**. A few cases might involve rare electrical phenomena such as sprites, or radar ducting, and a few might come from secret military activity. Further analysis of the evidence presented was unlikely to settle the cause of the reports. It still judged that careful, objective study of new physical data could teach something, and named the French GEPAN/SEPRA office as a model.

The report appeared in the Journal of Scientific Exploration 12(2) in 1998, with the investigators' presentations, the panel's comments and appendices on GEPAN/SEPRA, ducting, sprites and scientific inference. Our copy is a reproduction of the journal's web edition.

*Sources: P. A. Sturrock et al., “Physical Evidence Related to UFO Reports”, Journal of Scientific Exploration 12(2): 179–229 (1998), web edition as reproduced in our copy*

The Royal Canadian Mounted Police file on Stefan Michalak's report of 20 May 1967, held by Library and Archives Canada in the RCMP records (RG 18). Our copy is the selection LAC digitized for its 2005 online exhibition: police reports from the first roadside encounter to the August 1967 summary, the July telexes and memos on the radioactive soil samples, the Department of Health and Welfare's radiation investigation of September 1967 (a copy kept in the RCMP file), and a short National Defence summary. A few names are redacted under the Access to Information Act.

The reports record both sides. The investigators found Michalak's behaviour and parts of his story inconsistent: he could not lead them to the site, a motel employee contradicted his account of the evening before, and he later removed soil, cloth and a steel tape from the site after being asked not to. They also recorded what they could not explain: his illness, burns and weight loss, and a circle on the rock. The health investigator found a small patch of radium-226 contamination at the site, which his first tests suggested might be luminous paint, but left its origin undetermined; National Defence's summary says neither its team nor the RCMP's could disprove the account.

Pages 23 and 31 and paragraph 22 of the August report are faint typescript; their text in our index was transcribed by hand from the scans.

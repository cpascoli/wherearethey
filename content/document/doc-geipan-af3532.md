facts:
  - text: "At 13:14 on 28 January 1994, the crew of an Air France A320 flying Nice–London observed a phenomenon over Coulommiers (Seine-et-Marne). The chief steward, then in the cockpit, thought it looked like a weather balloon; the captain and copilot described a brown or dark red disc that changed shape before suddenly disappearing. Reims air traffic control had no traffic in the sector."
    status: documented
    source: library/docs/doc-geipan-af3532.pdf
  - text: "An unidentified radar track was recorded for 50 seconds by the Cinq-Mars-la-Pile radar. It crossed the flight's path, matched no filed flight plan, and disappeared at the same moment as the visual phenomenon. GEIPAN says the investigators remained cautious about correlating the radar signature with the phenomenon."
    status: documented
    source: library/docs/doc-geipan-af3532.pdf
  - text: "The witnesses made their official reports to the gendarmerie and to SEPRA (CNES) three years later, when the case was cited in the press. A 2012 Météo-France check found that a sounding balloon had crossed the aircraft's path an hour before the sighting, and would have been coming down far to the southeast by then."
    status: documented
    source: library/docs/doc-geipan-af3532.pdf
  - text: "GEIPAN classifies the case D1: unexplained, of medium consistency, with a marked strangeness. It names an unknown atmospheric optical phenomenon or a flexible object such as a balloon envelope drifting on the wind as possible but unprovable hypotheses. It says the late testimony, the absence of the steward's statement and a radar track inconsistent with the object observed weaken the case."
    status: documented
    source: library/docs/doc-geipan-af3532.pdf

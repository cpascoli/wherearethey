The first official British study of UFOs: a six-page report, classified “Secret/Discreet”, by a working party set up in August 1950 under the Ministry of Defence's Directorate of Scientific Intelligence and Joint Technical Intelligence Committee and chaired by G. L. Turney of Admiralty scientific intelligence. Relying heavily on the U.S. Air Force's Project Grudge report, it reviewed the American and Swedish findings and three sightings reported by RAF officers in 1950, explained each, and recommended that no further investigation be made unless material evidence turned up.

Winston Churchill asked the Air Ministry in July 1952 what “all this stuff about flying saucers” amounted to and was told the reports had been the subject of a full intelligence study in 1951. Later attempts to trace the study failed until a surviving copy was found in MoD archives in 2001; it was released the following year. Its sceptical conclusions set the template for British policy until the UFO desk closed in 2009.

The original record of the Tehran F-4 encounter: a four-page priority cable sent on 23 September 1976 by the U.S. Defense Attaché Office in Tehran to the Defense Intelligence Agency, as printed by the Joint Chiefs of Staff message centre with its distribution list, together with a DIA routing slip dated 8 December 1978 (from Louis E. Foster, RDS-3A, DIA) and a DIA evaluation form. The copy is the one released by the National Security Agency and mirrored by The Black Vault; the DIA released the cable alone under the FOIA in 1996.

The cable recounts the calls from the Shemiran district, the decision of Brigadier General Yousefi, assistant deputy commander of operations, to scramble an F-4 from Shahrokhi, the first jet's loss of instruments and communications at 25 NM, the second jet's radar lock at 27 NM, the failure of its weapons control panel as the pilot tried to fire an AIM-9, the smaller objects, and the next day's helicopter visit to the supposed landing site. It says the information came from a source's conversation with a sub-source and one of the F-4 pilots; it names no crew member. The evaluation form calls the case a classic for the study of the phenomenon and gives its reasons; its first line and first reason are overstruck and barely legible in this copy, and the evaluator's name is blank.

The cable reports the crews' account; it is not an investigation. Sceptics, notably Philip J. Klass, attributed the light to the planet Jupiter and the failures to equipment faults (see the incident file).

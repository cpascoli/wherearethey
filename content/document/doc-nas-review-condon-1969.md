The National Academy of Sciences' review of the Condon Report, by an eleven-member panel chaired by Gerald M. Clemence of Yale University. The Academy had agreed in October 1966 to review the University of Colorado study on completion; the panel read the report from 15 November 1968 and concluded its deliberations on 6 January 1969. It was asked only to judge the report's scope, methodology and findings, not to study UFOs itself or hear advocates.

It found the scope adequate and the methodology well chosen, accepted the findings on secrecy and defence, and concurred that Blue Book was not needed and that UFO investigation deserved no high priority. It noted that some sightings remain unexplained but judged extraterrestrial visitation "the least likely explanation". The Bolender memo of October 1969 cites the review, with the report, in recommending the end of Project Blue Book.

The copy on file is a scan released in the Department of Defense FOIA Reading Room, where it appears as an attachment (pages 4–10 of "Attachment 2") to a later compilation; the last page of the file, a directory of UFO organisations, belongs to that compilation, not to the review.

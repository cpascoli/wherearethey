Frederick C. Durant was a rocket engineer and CIA consultant, later a space historian at the Smithsonian. Then at Arthur D. Little, he wrote the report of the Robertson Panel, the CIA-convened panel of scientists chaired by the Caltech physicist H. P. Robertson that met on 14–18 January 1953 to review Air Force UFO cases and films. It is known as the Durant Report.

The report concluded that UFOs posed no direct threat to national security. The panel's concern was indirect: the volume of reports could clog military communications and be exploited psychologically. It recommended a public “debunking” and education effort, which critics see as the origin of official stigma around reporting.

Redacted versions were released from the 1970s; the CIA approved a fuller release in 2013.

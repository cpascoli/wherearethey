Leonard H. Stringfield (1920–1994) was an American UFO researcher from Cincinnati and the first to gather accounts of crashed UFOs and recovered bodies systematically. By his own account he served as an Army Air Forces intelligence officer in the Pacific during the Second World War, ran the early civilian group CRIFO in the 1950s, and screened sighting reports for the Air Force's Air Defense Command; none of this is confirmed by official records in this archive. From his 1978 MUFON paper *Retrievals of the Third Kind* to his seventh *Status Report* in 1994 he published dozens of retrieval accounts, many of them set at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base.

He is filed as a researcher, outside the archive's credibility criteria, because his accounts come almost entirely from informants identified only by initials, often relayed second-hand. He said so himself: he had never seen a retrieved craft or body and held no affidavits, and his cases could be considered hearsay. He matters because his accounts underpin much of today's crash-retrieval narrative and are cited in testimony submitted to Congress; this file lets readers trace those claims to their source.

Edward U. Condon was an American physicist, professor of physics and astrophysics at the University of Colorado and a former director of the National Bureau of Standards. In 1941 he was named to the committee that set up the U.S. atomic bomb program, and he had served as president of the American Physical Society (1946) and of the American Association for the Advancement of Science (1953).

From October 1966 he was scientific director of the University of Colorado's Air Force-funded study of unidentified flying objects, the Condon Committee. He wrote its "Conclusions and Recommendations": that nothing in 21 years of UFO study had added to scientific knowledge, that further extensive study probably could not be justified on scientific grounds, and that the Air Force's defence task could be done without a special unit such as Project Blue Book. The National Academy of Sciences panel endorsed the report, and the Air Force cited it when it closed Blue Book in 1969.

In 1968 Look magazine and the civilian group NICAP accused the project of a negative bias, after two of its scientists were dismissed and a memo attributed to its coordinator, Robert J. Low, was made public; Condon said the Look article contained "falsehoods and misrepresentations" and that the dismissals were "for cause".

*Sources: Scientific Study of Unidentified Flying Objects (1968), Section I and Appendix X (NCAS transcription) · New York Times, 1 May 1968 (transcription) · AARO Historical Record Report, Vol. 1 (2024)*

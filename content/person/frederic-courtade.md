Frédéric Courtade heads GEIPAN, the office of the French space agency CNES that collects reports of unidentified aerospace phenomena from the public, the gendarmerie, the Air Force and civil aviation, investigates them, and publishes its case files and classifications online.

In a 2025 piece by INA, France's national audiovisual archive, he explained why the 1965 Valensole case, in which the farmer Maurice Masse reported a craft shaped like a rugby ball and a figure about a metre tall, is classed D. GEPAN, created twelve years after the events, gave it that class because a residual strangeness remains after the plausible hypotheses have been tested. He said Valensole remains “un phénomène très étrange aux yeux des GPAN encore à ce jour”.

He added that events predating GEPAN call for caution. The same programme gives the explanation offered in 1965: military helicopters on manoeuvres in Provence.

Nick Pope is a former UK Ministry of Defence civil servant who ran the ministry's UFO desk in the Secretariat (Air Staff) from 1991 to 1994, investigating public and military reports for the British government. He has since become the best-known British official voice on the subject, through books, broadcasts, the 2007 National Press Club panel and the Rendlesham Forest panel of the 2013 Citizen Hearing. He says he came to the desk as a sceptic; most sightings were misidentifications, but "around five percent appeared to defy conventional explanation".

Cosford was his turning point. On the morning of 31 March 1993 he came in to find "the phones were ringing off the hook" and pieced together a series of sightings over six hours by police officers, RAF Police and military guards across western England and Wales. He launched a full inquiry, checking aircraft movements, satellite tracks, airships and weather balloons, had the radar tapes impounded and found nothing on them, and was given "cast iron assurances" by the Americans that no secret test aircraft was flying; the head of his secretariat minuted the Assistant Chief of the Air Staff that there seemed to be "some evidence" of an object of unknown origin over the UK, which Pope calls as close as the MoD will ever get to saying "these things are real". He and his superiors had no conventional explanation. The MoD's own file shows the re-entry of the rocket that launched Cosmos 2238 accounted for many of the high-level sightings around 1.10 am, though not, as the desk noted, the low-level ones.

Rendlesham is his other central case. He says the MoD's Defence Intelligence Staff judged the radiation readings at the landing site "significantly higher than the average background", that the U.S. commander in Europe took material from the investigation to Ramstein without telling the British, and that his cold-case review found misidentification, hoax and delusion did not fit the facts. He quotes Lord Hill-Norton's challenge that the witnesses were either telling the truth or "hallucinating or lying". He is also measured. Some intelligence files from the period were destroyed, but he says he has seen no evidence of a deliberate cover-up, and he is "not aware of any cover-up or conspiracy" in the sense theorists mean; what he does describe is an unofficial policy under which the press office slipped "little green men and flying saucers" into its answers to downplay the subject.

He also championed the 1990 Calvine photograph, a print of which hung in his office until a superior removed it, and he describes Project Condign, the Defence Intelligence Staff's late-1990s study that put some sightings down to exotic atmospheric plasmas. The MoD's UFO project, which he says was modelled on Project Blue Book, closed in the 2009 defence cuts. Sceptics attribute Rendlesham to a fireball and the Orford Ness lighthouse, and Calvine possibly to a small model close to the camera.

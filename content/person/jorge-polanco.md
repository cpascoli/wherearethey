Jorge Polanco was a captain with Aerolíneas Argentinas. On the evening of 31 July 1995, flying Boeing 727 flight 674 into San Carlos de Bariloche, he reported a light that rushed towards his aircraft and then paced it as an object with coloured lights, while the airport and city lights failed and he had to go around. The case made national headlines and was cited in the French COMETA report.

In later interviews he describes a 30-metre, plate-shaped craft with a pulsing amber light, flying within metres of his wingtip and racing over the lake. He says it was one of three sightings in his career, and that he went public when the air force declined to investigate. He also cites Gendarmería officers flying nearby as witnesses.

The case is now officially explained. In its 2018 report the Argentine Air Force's CEFAE concluded that the lights were a discotheque sky-tracer searchlight reflecting off ice-crystal cloud during the blackout. The operator confirmed he was testing it that night, and the beam directions matched the Gendarmería commander's sketches. CEFAE noted that Polanco's copilot saw only reflected lights and the Gendarmería pilot saw a light with no structure, so only Polanco described a solid craft.

*Sources: Clarín (2 August 1995) · CHANGO interview · CEFAE report (2018) · Bariloche incident record*

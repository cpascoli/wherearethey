J. Edward Roush, a Democratic U.S. Representative from Indiana, chaired the House Committee on Science and Astronautics' symposium on unidentified flying objects on 29 July 1968, held while the Condon study was still under way.

Opening the session, he said the committee approached the question “as purely a scientific problem, one of unanswered questions”, and that “We take no stand on these matters.” He introduced six scientists: J. Allen Hynek, James E. McDonald, Carl Sagan, Robert L. Hall, James A. Harder and Robert M. L. Baker Jr., with further papers submitted in writing.

When Rep. Ryan asked McDonald to evaluate the University of Colorado project, Roush stepped in: the members had agreed that the symposium was not the place to discuss it, and that its purpose was to explore UFOs as a scientific phenomenon, not the activities of another branch of government. The printed proceedings are the record of the day; no recording is known to survive.

bio: "Jacques Vallée is a French-born computer scientist (born 1939) with a PhD from Northwestern University, a venture capitalist, engineer and author who has studied UFOs since the 1960s. He says he designed the case database of AAWSAP, the Defense Intelligence Agency's program of 2009–2012 run under contract by Bigelow Aerospace, and he has worked with the Stanford immunologist Garry Nolan."
---

His books on file run from *Anatomy of a Phenomenon* (1965) and *Passport to Magonia* (1969) through *The Invisible College* (1975), *Messengers of Deception* (1979), *Dimensions* (1988), *Confrontations* (1990) and *Revelations* (1991) to the journals of *Forbidden Science* (1992); he wrote *The Edge of Reality* (1975) with J. Allen Hynek. Garry Nolan, the Stanford immunologist, says he was introduced to Vallée as part of the "invisible college" when the CIA asked him to study people who had had encounters, and credits him with the idea of "kabuki", that the absurd elements of encounters may be a deliberate show aimed at humanity, and with the rule of talking about data rather than conclusions.

His government work was for AAWSAP. He says he designed its database, built to hold about "240,000 cases" in English so that AI could be applied to them; the subject was dropped when the program was cut after two years and the 40 people of his team were let go. In January 2026 he said that database held "hundreds of reports of creatures live or dead" associated with crashed or landed vehicles, some like those described in Varginha. He argues that the cases show invariants, among them objects that appear suddenly and disappear by "fading in place" without accelerating, and that physical samples need isotope analysis to rule out unusual but mundane aerospace materials, work he now does with Nolan's instruments at Stanford, as Battelle and the Air Force did for Blue Book-era samples in their day.

His most contested position is *Trinity: The Best-Kept Secret* (2021): that in August 1945 a craft crashed near the Trinity test site in New Mexico, two years before Kenneth Arnold, with three small insect-like beings alive inside, a case he puts beside Socorro and Valensole. The story rests on the memories of two men told decades later, with no documentary support; critics call it a hoax, and in 2023 Vallée conceded that a man the witnesses said they saw entering the craft had been serving with the Army in Europe at the time. AARO's 2024 historical report found no empirical evidence that the U.S. has recovered craft of non-human origin.

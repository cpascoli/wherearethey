Carl Sagan (1934–1996) was an American astronomer and science communicator, then at Harvard and later at Cornell. He was a member of the Air Force Scientific Advisory Board committee, chaired by Brian O'Brien, whose review of Project Blue Book was printed in the record of the April 1966 House hearing and recommended contracting university teams to investigate sightings.

On CBS Reports in May 1966 he said “There is not a single verified or checked out report which is at all connectable with the possibility of extraterrestrial life”, though he called rare visits “not implausible”. At the House Science and Astronautics Committee symposium of 29 July 1968 he said he did not think the evidence “is at all persuasive” either way. He co-organised the 1969 symposium of the American Association for the Advancement of Science on UFOs and co-edited its papers as *UFO's: A Scientific Debate* (1972).

He later disputed the claimed "star map" in the Hill case. At a 2023 Senate hearing AARO's Sean Kirkpatrick extended his maxim: “extraordinary claims require not only extraordinary evidence but extraordinary science”.

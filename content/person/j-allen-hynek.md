J. Allen Hynek (1910–1986) was an astronomer at Ohio State University and later Northwestern University, and the U.S. Air Force's scientific consultant on UFO reports for Projects Sign, Grudge and Blue Book from 1948 to 1969. He began as a sceptic whose job was to find the conventional explanation, and kept to that line in public: sent to Michigan in March 1966, he put the sightings there down to marsh gas, lights that appear and go out in different places and give "the illusion of motion". The explanation was ridiculed as "swamp gas", and House Minority Leader Gerald Ford's call for an investigation led to the first open congressional hearing on UFOs, on 5 April 1966.

At that hearing he called the subject "a signal-to-noise ratio problem" and said he had set aside some 20 well-reported cases he could not explain, from scientists, servicemen and police, to show that neither he nor the Air Force hid the fact that unexplained reports existed. To reporters the same day, and on CBS Reports a month later, he said there was no proof "that we have been visited by spaceships", that the charge he and the Air Force were "in cahoots" was "a downright falsehood", and that the burden of proof lay with those sure of spaceships; yet a "residue" of cases, above all from pilots and radar trackers, intrigued him "in the same way that a good mystery story intrigues me", and deserved a quiet scientific look, "without fanfare". Chris Mellon's reading is harsher: Hynek was told to explain cases away. Hynek's biographer Mark O'Connell has said much the same of Blue Book, that its mission became "making the UFOs go away".

He took part in the House science committee's symposium of July 1968, and after Blue Book closed in December 1969 became the subject's best-known scientific advocate: he set out his "close encounters" classification in *The UFO Experience* (1972), founded the Center for UFO Studies (CUFOS) in 1973 and published *The Hynek UFO Report* (1977), his own review of the Blue Book files. The residue he described in 1966 is the question the archive is about.

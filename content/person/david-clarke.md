David Clarke is a historian and journalism academic at Sheffield Hallam University. From 2008 to 2013 he was consultant to The National Archives on the release of the Ministry of Defence's UFO files, bookmarking the key stories in each of the ten releases for the Archives' highlights guides; the Archives published his *The UFO Files* (2009), and he has since written *How UFOs Conquered the World* (2015) and *UFO Drawings from the National Archives* (2017), after *Flying Saucerers* (2007) with Andy Roberts.

He works from the original records rather than later retellings, and his conclusions are usually sceptical: he calls the 1967 student hoax, when six "flying saucers" were found across southern England, "one of the most important stories" in the files, because for a few hours people thought aliens had landed and it shows how impossible keeping such a secret would be. He traced the Calvine photograph to the retired RAF press officer Craig Lindsay, who had kept a print in his garage for 30 years, and has researched Rendlesham, Alderney, Manston, Broad Haven and Howden Moor from the files. A Freedom of Information request of his found that the MoD's unredacted copy of the Condign report had been "accidentally destroyed".

Lakenheath–Bentwaters, 13–14 August 1956, is the case he leaves open. U.S. base radars reported fast objects, RAF Neatishead's chief controller saw a target stop dead and accelerate, and several radars on different frequencies saw the targets over about six hours; the case stayed secret until the Condon study published it in 1969 as the only unexplained British case, with its radar expert Gordon Thayer suggesting "a mechanical device of unknown origin". The night-fighter squadron's diary put it down to a balloon, which Clarke thinks cannot explain the earlier tracks, though the Perseid meteors may have added visual reports; the Venom crew, traced decades later, remembered only a blip, and Philip Klass attributed the returns to anomalous propagation and meteors.

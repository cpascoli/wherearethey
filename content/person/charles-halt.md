Charles Halt is a retired U.S. Air Force colonel who, as a lieutenant colonel and deputy base commander at RAF Bentwaters/Woodbridge in England, became the senior official witness to the Rendlesham Forest incident of December 1980. He had come to Bentwaters from the Pentagon that year, to what he describes as the largest tactical fighter wing in the world.

After security patrolmen Jim Penniston, John Burroughs and Edward Cabansag reported a landed triangular craft in the forest, Halt went out two nights later with a Geiger counter and a dictaphone, intending to debunk the story. His tape and later accounts describe three indentations with elevated radiation, a winking red light moving through the trees that broke into several white objects, and objects sending beams down near the bases' weapons storage area, one at his feet. He insists the Orford Ness lighthouse was visible separately throughout. His one-page memo to the Ministry of Defence, "Unexplained Lights", which he says was deliberately toned down, was released under FOIA in 1983 and became the case's key official document. In the account he gave in 1997, which a correspondent filed with the MoD, he says he never personally saw "a definite shaped craft" but believes the airmen of the first night.

Since retiring he has spoken at the 2007 National Press Club panel and the 2010 "UFOs and Nukes" event, and to many interviewers. He says the objects were intelligently controlled, that his airmen were interrogated, drugged and hypnotised, that there was more than an hour of lost time, and that no one ever debriefed him. The MoD judged his report to be of no defence significance and never interviewed him; the explanations on its file are a fireball and the Orford Ness lighthouse, which a Suffolk police inspector said "caused strange visual effects" in some conditions, and a forester's view that the depressions were rabbit diggings. Halt rejects all of this.

L. Mendel Rivers, a Democratic U.S. Representative from South Carolina, chaired the House Armed Services Committee from 1965 to 1970. On 5 April 1966 he chaired its hearing on UFOs, the first open congressional hearing on the subject, held after the March 1966 Michigan sightings and Minority Leader Gerald Ford's call for an inquiry.

He opened by asking Air Force Secretary Harold Brown to “shed some light on these highly illuminated objects”: “We can't just write them off. There are too many responsible people who are concerned.” Asking whether the session need be closed, he had the doors opened to the press. He pressed Brown on sightings by “Responsible, well-trained people, like pilots”, and asked whether anyone of standing believed the objects came from other planets; Brown said no one in the Air Force did, and that 95 percent of sightings had been explained.

The other witnesses were Blue Book chief Maj. Hector Quintanilla and J. Allen Hynek, who urged a civilian scientific panel. The hearing led to the University of Colorado study directed by Edward Condon.

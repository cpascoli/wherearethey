Robert Bigelow is a Las Vegas billionaire who made his fortune with the Budget Suites of America extended-stay chain and has spent decades and much of that fortune on space and the paranormal. He founded Bigelow Aerospace in 1999, whose BEAM expandable module was attached to the International Space Station in 2016. Earlier he funded the National Institute for Discovery Science, which hired Eric Davis and had Hal Puthoff and Edgar Mitchell on its advisory board, and in 1996 he bought Skinwalker Ranch in Utah, which he owned until 2016.

He was the private engine behind the Pentagon's first modern UAP program. A longtime friend and campaign donor of Senator Harry Reid, he gave to Reid's campaign committee and leadership PAC from 1998 to 2010, according to FEC records and Politico. By Reid's own account to The New York Times, Bigelow first interested him in the subject in 2007, when he told Reid a DIA official wanted to visit his ranch. Reid then steered $22 million to the DIA's AAWSAP, run by James Lacatski, and Bigelow's new subsidiary BAASS won the contract. Puthoff says it was an open competition, while Vice reported Bigelow was the sole bidder. AARO's 2024 report says the contractor went beyond its brief into UFO and paranormal research at Bigelow's ranch, and proposed a PR campaign premised on "E.T. visitations". The DIA ended the program in 2012.

George Knapp and Luis Elizondo say Bigelow negotiated to take custody of recovered material held by Lockheed Martin, a transfer they say was blocked; AARO says the named companies deny holding any such material. Bigelow has said he is "absolutely convinced" aliens have visited Earth. He later funded research on life after death through the Bigelow Institute, and has become one of the largest donors to Republican causes.

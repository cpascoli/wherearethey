Chad Underwood is a former U.S. Navy F/A-18F aircrew member with Strike Fighter Squadron 41, the "Black Aces", a lieutenant in 2004 and later a lieutenant commander. On 14 November 2004, about an hour after David Fravor's encounter, he flew the next sortie from USS Nimitz, found the object on radar and recorded the infrared "FLIR1" video of it on his targeting pod. Which seat he was in is told differently: he described himself to New York Magazine as "a pilot in his squadron" who was not manoeuvring the aircraft aggressively, while Fravor speaks of "Chad and his pilot" and Jeremy Corbell introduces him as a weapon systems officer, the back-seat officer who runs the radar and sensors on a two-seat Super Hornet. He also coined the name "Tic Tac". The video circulated within the carrier group, was published by The New York Times in 2017 and was officially released by the Pentagon in 2020. He later became a flight instructor and worked in the aerospace industry.

He stayed silent for 15 years, wary of being associated with "little green men", before speaking to New York Magazine in 2019 and, in 2020, on camera to Jeremy Corbell. He described an object changing altitude, speed and aspect erratically, dropping from about 50,000 feet to near the sea in seconds, with no visible exhaust. He ruled out birds and balloons, and judged a weather event unlikely given Fravor's sighting and the other sensors. He said he was never debriefed as he would have been about a secret test aircraft.

His two interviews differ in emphasis. In 2019 he stressed the limits of his evidence: he never saw the object with his own eyes and could not confirm that it actually accelerated, a point pressed by sceptics who attribute the dart to the pod's tracking. In 2020 he told Corbell that his jet was flying straight and level, and that the object broke the pod's lock by accelerating away. He also said the radar showed signs of jamming.

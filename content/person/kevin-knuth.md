---

His 2019 paper in *Entropy*, written with Robert Powell and Peter Reali of the Scientific Coalition for UAP Studies, treats the reported motions of craft in the Nimitz, Japan Air Lines 1628 and Bethune encounters as data and estimates lower bounds on their accelerations, from about 70 g to more than 5,000 g. The largest figure rests on Kevin Day's account of the Princeton's radar, not on the radar data, which has never been released.

With the physicist Matthew Szydagis he runs UAPx, which monitors a site in Connecticut with cameras, and in 2026 was preparing to add radar and radio-frequency sensors. In interviews he says AARO had his paper before Sean Kirkpatrick's 2023 Senate testimony, offers a physical hypothesis for the Malmstrom missile shutdowns, and says he has spoken to scientists who worked on recovered craft; those are his claims, not findings of the paper.

*Extraterrestrial* (Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, 2021) is the Harvard astronomer Avi Loeb's popular account of ʻOumuamua, the elongated object seen passing through the inner solar system in October 2017, the first known visitor from another star. Loeb lists what made it odd, including its shape, its brightness and the slight push away from the Sun with no visible gas trail, and argues that the simplest explanation may be that it was artificial, perhaps a thin light sail built by another civilization. The book is also a memoir and a plea for scientists to keep an open mind about extraterrestrial technology.

It became a New York Times bestseller and drew sharp criticism. Reviewers noted that Loeb's best evidence is that the natural explanations seem unconvincing to him, which falls short of the "extraordinary evidence" standard he explicitly rejects; by 2021 most astronomers regarded ʻOumuamua as natural, perhaps a fragment of nitrogen ice or an altered comet-like body. Loeb went on to found the Galileo Project to look for such technology with telescopes.

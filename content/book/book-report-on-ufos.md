*The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects* (Doubleday, 1956) is the first account of the U.S. Air Force's UFO investigations by someone who ran them. Edward Ruppelt, head of Project Blue Book from 1951 to 1953, tells the story from Project Sign's first cases through Grudge, the 1952 wave, the Washington radar sightings, the Tremonton and Montana films and the 1953 Robertson Panel. He describes the Top Secret 1948 "Estimate of the Situation", which concluded the objects were interplanetary and was rejected by the Chief of Staff, and the project's later swing to explaining reports away. He is candid about the Air Force's confusion and about a residue of unexplained cases from credible witnesses, and he coined the term "UFO".

The 1960 edition added three chapters that take a much more sceptical view of the reports since 1953 and end by calling the UFO a "Space Age Myth". Whether that reflected a change of mind, the record he saw or pressure is debated; Ruppelt died of a heart attack months later. The book's copyright was not renewed and it is in the public domain in the United States; the full text is in this archive's search, chapter by chapter.

**Official PURSUE description** (Department of War / AARO, release 08):

This document is an Air Intelligence Information Report (AIIR), a standardized U.S. Air Force reporting form used to record raw information of potential intelligence value for later analysis. The form served as a primary vehicle for U.S. Air Force field intelligence collection throughout the early Cold War, until defense-wide reporting standards were established in the early 1960s.
This AIIR documents an October 19, 1957, observation made from the Larson Air Force Base Operations and Control Tower, Washington, from 0110Z to 0250Z (5:10 p.m. to 6:50 p.m. local). The report describes a bright, silvery, apparently circular object observed south-southwest of Moses Lake and moving westward. Observers reported changing colors, an apparent smaller adjacent object late in the observation, and clear, cloudless weather conditions. Personnel contacted “Igloo Control,” which did not obtain a radar track but reported a visual observation; it assessed the object as “PLUTO,” and deemed that no interception was necessary.
All descriptive and estimative language contained in this report reflects the reporter’s subjective interpretation at the time of the event. Such characterizations should not be interpreted as a conclusive indication of the presence or absence of any intrinsic object features or performance characteristics.
AARO Comment: In this context, the term “PLUTO” almost certainly did not refer to the celestial body Pluto, which cannot be seen with the unaided eye. The report states that “Igloo Control” visually observed the reported object, said it believed the object to be “PLUTO,” and stated that no interception was necessary. This sequence suggests that the term functioned as an operational assessment or identifier indicating that the reported object did not warrant interception. However, the report does not define “PLUTO” or establish its precise operational meaning.

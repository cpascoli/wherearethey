**Official PURSUE description** (Department of War / AARO, release 08):

This document is an Air Intelligence Information Report (AIIR), a standardized U.S. Air Force reporting form used to record raw information of potential intelligence value for later analysis. The form served as a primary vehicle for U.S. Air Force field intelligence collection throughout the early Cold War, until defense-wide reporting standards were established in the early 1960s.
This AIIR documents a November 13, 1957, incident involving two sightings of bright lights in the sky, reported by separate U.S. Army military police patrols near Stallion Site Camp, approximately 90 miles north of White Sands Proving Grounds headquarters. One patrol reported a bright object descending near the bunker area during the early morning; a second patrol reported a pulsating red-and-white light near the Trinity Site at approximately 8 p.m. The preparing officer consulted with Dr. Lincoln La Paz, Head of the Department of Meteoretics at the University of New Mexico; Dr. La Paz stated that no unusual meteorological or astronomical phenomena had been recorded in the area during the incident. The preparing officer assessed that the first patrol had likely observed a moonset and that the second had likely observed the planet Venus.
All descriptive and estimative language contained in this report reflects the reporter’s subjective interpretation at the time of the event. Such characterizations should not be interpreted as a conclusive indication of the presence or absence of any intrinsic object features or performance characteristics.
AARO Comment: The “Trinity Site” is the location of the first successful nuclear weapons test during World War II. “White Sands Proving Range” is the contemporary name for the modern U.S. Army installation White Sands Missile Range.

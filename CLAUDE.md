# Where Are They — UAP / NHI case archive

A knowledge map of UAP/NHI testimony, programs, hearings, incidents, official records and video evidence.
Plain files in `content/` are the source of truth; `scripts/build.mjs` compiles them into `site/data/` for the browser app in `site/`.

## The sourcing rule (most important)
Only facts and testimony from **credible, on-record people**: current/former government officials, members of Congress and staff,
military personnel, pilots, radar/sensor operators, intelligence officers, academics, astronauts, aerospace-industry insiders.
- Every person needs `credentials` (category + role). Categories: `congress government intelligence military aviator sensor-operator academia astronaut aerospace law-enforcement` (credible), `journalist`, `policy-expert` (reporter tier — relays or analyses, not a witness), `civilian disputed unknown` (outside criteria → hidden by default).
- Incidents count as credible when linked to a credible person or a documented fact. **Mass-sighting rule:** an incident with `witnesses: <n>` ≥ 50 *and* at least one contemporary `coverage:` entry (outlet + date, from the time of the event) is included with the “Mass sighting” badge. Only cite coverage that actually dates from the event, and never mark Wikipedia-sourced facts as `documented`.
- Incidents carry `outcome:` `open` (default) | `disputed` (shown as “Partly explained”: an official/credible explanation covers part of the core of the case — the sightings or evidence that make it notable — or is contested; an explanation of side reports only, e.g. the Cosmos 2238 re-entry for Cosford's high-altitude lights, leaves the case `open` and goes in its facts) | `explained` (a credible conventional explanation is established, e.g. an AARO or air-force resolution) | `hoax`, with `outcome_note:` (one sentence) and `outcome_source:` (required unless open). Explained and hoax cases stay in the archive (counter-evidence matters) but are hidden by the “Hide explained cases” filter, on by default. Don't mark a case explained on a private sceptic's theory alone.
- Never upgrade a status without a source. Prefer the counter-evidence too (e.g. Kirkpatrick, AARO resolutions) — balance is part of credibility.

## Entry format — `content/<type>/<id>.md`
Types: person, incident, hearing, program, organization, document, film, book, record (PURSUE, generated), video, event, concept, place, source.
PURSUE `record`s are not graph nodes and not in the type filter: they appear as an “Official records” list in the dossier of whatever they link to, in the Index (own group), in search and in full-text search. Link a record to the entity it documents (`rel`), and it will surface there. Records with a DVIDS video (`pursue.dvids`) play it in place: on the record's page, and as an “Official footage” carousel (one slide per video) in the dossier of every file they link to, except organizations with more than 20 (the Department of War). The poster frames come from `content/dvids.json` (commit it), which `npm run dvids:thumbs` fills for new records after `import:pursue`; it also records DVIDS's MP4 for video entries whose `url:` is a DVIDS page, so those play in our own player, synced with transcript and moments (DVIDS's embed player if the file fails); a record's footage can get its own video entry (`[ recording of, <record> ]`, `media: dvids-<id>`, `url:` its DVIDS page) with moments: for silent sensor video, take them from AARO's timecoded description and from what the frames show (check them; e.g. find colour frames by saturation), say which is which, and never imply a link the records don't make. The carousel slide then offers “▶ N moments”, the record page the same button, and the video stays out of the file's Watch list. Don't give such entries an `about` link to the organization or incident (the carousel already shows them there); instead tag only the few strongest moments with `refs: [ <that file> ]` so they appear in its Video moments section; DVIDS's embed player answers 403 to headless browsers, so check it in a normal Chrome.
`document` is for official records and primary documents only; documentaries go in `film/` and books in `book/` (shown on the “Documentaries & books” page, under More). Give them `credits:` (free text), `runtime:` (minutes) or `publisher:`/`isbn:`/`pages:`, link people with `features`/`authored by`/`narrated by`, and attach an official trailer as a video with `[ trailer of, film-… ]`.  `shelf_featured: true` shows one first, as a large “featured” card, on its page. Books can carry `glimpses:` (`text`, `where`, `via`, `url`): short verbatim passages from the book, taken only from an authorized excerpt (publisher, author's own site) or a review that quotes it, shown as “Glimpses” under the summary. Save a copy of each source page in `sources/excerpts/<book-id>/` (git-ignored: publishers forbid reprinting) and check every glimpse word for word against it; never quote unofficial full-text uploads. Their contents are testimony: `claimed` unless an official record backs it; link out, never host.
`news` items (`content/news/<id>.md`) are short dated updates too slight for a full entry: a statement, a post on X, a press article, a bill. Fields: `name` (headline), `date` (when it happened), `kind` (`statement post article interview podcast hearing legislation release research incident event`), `url` (the original source, required), optional `outlet`, a 1–2 sentence body, and `rel` to the people and cases involved (`[ features, person ]`, `[ about, inc-… ]`). Like records they are not graph nodes; they show on Latest and as “In the news” in linked dossiers. When the person tells it on video, file the video with `[ recording of, <news-id> ]` and moments: the Latest card then plays it, and the news page lists its moments. What someone says is `claimed`; anything significant (a hearing, an interview) becomes a full entry with moments instead, and the news item, if any, links to it.
`claim`s (`content/claim/<id>.md`, page `/claim/<id>`) are the few dozen key propositions of the debate (“The U.S. holds recovered craft of non-human origin…”), stated neutrally in one sentence as `name`, with `status:` `open` | `disputed` | `supported` | `refuted`, `status_note:` and `status_source:` (required unless open; never upgrade without a source), a short body, and `rel` to what they are about and to the evidence (`[ evidence for | evidence against, <doc/record/news>, "note" ]`, `[ narrower than | depends on | contradicts, <claim> ]`). They are not graph nodes. A claim gathers its **assertions**: facts and video moments tagged `claims: [ <claim-id> ]`, with `stance:` `supports` (default) | `disputes` | `context`, `basis:` `first-hand` | `relayed` | `documented` | `opinion` (how the speaker knows), `claim_by:` when the position is someone else's than the speaker's (e.g. Gallaudet reporting Kirkpatrick), and on facts `said:` (when it was said, if not the date of its source; `unknown` for an undated re-upload). Status rule: an official counter-finding on the proposition *as stated* → `disputed`; a primary record that establishes it as stated → `supported`; no official finding on this sentence → `open` (don't use Blue Book's verdict on a light to dispute an abduction, or AARO's GoFast paper to close Gimbal). A moment or fact has one stance for all its claims: where it supports one claim and disputes another, tag one and cite the source on the other claim as `evidence for`/`evidence against`. The claim page shows its evidence (`evidence for/against`, comments) and related claims, then lists the assertions oldest first (“who said it first”), with how the people making it are connected; a person's page lists their positions on claims; `/claims` shows the claims (strongest first: most first-hand and documented assertions, then volume; `claimOrder` in app.js and pages.mjs); the statements themselves are on each claim's page. Moments in a hearing's recording are dated by the hearing.
The id is the filename. Frontmatter (YAML) + optional Markdown body (bio/summary).
```yaml
name: David Fravor
aka: [Fravor]                  # used for search AND transcript name-matching
match: [Fraver]                # extra transcript spellings (auto-captions misspell names)
credentials: [{ category: aviator, role: "Commander, U.S. Navy (ret.)" }]
date: 2004-11-14               # YYYY | YYYY-MM | YYYY-MM-DD ; `end:` for ranges
place: { name: "…", lat: 31.5, lng: -117.8 }
tags: [aviator, navy]
rel:                           # [label, target-id] — read as “this —label→ target”
  - [ witness, inc-nimitz-2004 ]
  - [ based at, wright-patterson-afb, "Run by Air Materiel Command at Wright Field from 1948" ]  # optional note: how
facts:
  - text: "…"
    status: documented | claimed | unverified
    by: david-fravor           # who said it
    source: v-2023-07-26-hearing-a@34:29   # entity id, entity@timestamp (videos), or URL
links: [{ label: "…", url: "…" }]
files: [library/…pdf]          # local copies
```
Links: the label is 1–3 words (it is drawn on the graph) and must be in `scripts/relations.mjs`, which also gives its reading from the other end (“based at” → “home of”), so it reads correctly from either file; add a pair there for a new label. Avoid the generic `related to`: say how the two are related, and add a one-line note (third item) taken from what the files say, shown on hover in the graph and under the link in the dossier; quote only words that are in one of the two files. Where the files don't say how, `related to` stays (drawn dashed and faded); `npm run check -- --related` lists them. Don't add a link that only repeats one in the other file. Quote and moment links (“statement about”, “discusses”) are generated, and step aside where an explicit link exists.
Videos add `youtube:` and curated `moments:` (`t`, `text`, `speaker`, `refs`). `top_pick: N` puts a video in “Start here” on the Videos page, in that order (the recording of the latest congressional hearing is added automatically; at most eight). Videos page: filters in the left side panel, like Explore (Category, Decade, Speakers, “only with moments”; `vidKind` in app.js decides the category): Congress hearings (hearing recordings, plus events with lawmakers listed in `CONGRESS_EVENTS`), Citizen Hearing (the 2013 sessions), Conferences (any other event recording: press conferences, forums), Interviews & podcasts, News (news-channel segments under 20 minutes; longer ones count as interviews), Archive & official footage (anything before 2005, the official Navy videos, and recordings of official records). Lists are newest first, sessions of one series by `part`, last first. The side panel's one search box filters by title, person and case at once and searches the transcripts (results under the grid); “Start here” shows only with no filter or search on; while a video plays the side panel becomes the playlist of what the filters let through. Latest has the same kind of side panel (Type, When, the RSS link). Sessions of one long recording link to each other with `series: <hearing/event id>` and `part: N`; the hearing's `parts:` gives the total (e.g. the 20 Citizen Hearing sessions). Use `npm run find -- "phrase" [video-id]` to get exact timestamps.
Search results: each page's title and meta description are generated (title + what the page offers, e.g. “David Fravor: testimony & sources”; snippet = whole lead sentences + counts of sourced claims and video moments, ≤160 chars). For pages that matter in Search Console, set `seo_title:` (≤65 chars) and `seo_description:` (≤155 chars) in the frontmatter, using the words people search with; never claim anything the page doesn't support.
Bios: 2–3 short factual paragraphs, then an italic `*Sources: …*` line. Verify Wikipedia links point to the right person (many names redirect to topic pages).

## Commands
- `npm run dev` — build + serve http://localhost:4321 with rebuild/live-reload on changes (re-imports the build script each time, so build changes need no restart)
- Explore opens on the **key files** map, not the whole network: the files in `content/explore.json` (`core`, the well-sourced backbone of the story, about 25) and the starting points above it (`starts`: label + id, each opens a trail). Ids are checked by `npm run check`. Above the map, **stories** (`stories` in the same file: `id`, `label` for the button, `title`, `blurb`, and ordered `steps` of `title`, `ids`, `text`) replace the single-file starting points (`starts`, kept as a fallback): a story opens as a trail with all its files pinned, the current step outlined with its links to the earlier steps and later steps faded, told with a pager (‹ numbered dots ›; on the first step the left arrow is a ✕ back to the key files, and there is no › on the last), the ← → keys, and Esc to close; `/explore?story=<id>` opens one. Captions say only what the files say, and include the counter-evidence; the build warns when a step has no link to the steps before it. It is a trail with no steps whose files are pinned (`state.trail.core`): following a file starts a trail from it, “Back to the key files” returns, `/explore?full` still shows everything, but there is no button for it (the whole network is too big to read). Loose files are pulled in beside the main cluster and the map turns 90° to fit the screen. Trails leave out links generated from video moments (“discusses”), like transcript mentions, except when following a video; “speaks in” stays.
- Search results: picking a file opens it full width on desktop (the reading view, as its own address does), with an Explore trail already started from it behind; collapsing it (⤡ or R) shows that file in the middle of the graph, selected (ring and halo). Records, news and claims are not graph nodes: the trail starts from the graph file they belong to (`graphAnchor` in app.js: the incident or person first, then a document or the video that records it, the agency, the release event last), which is highlighted while they are open. Phones keep the graph alone.
- World map: on `/world` the top search lists only files that are on the map (“On the map”), and picking one flies to its dot, highlights it and opens the file, instead of starting an Explore trail. `#map` uses `isolation: isolate` so Leaflet's panes stay below the header and its dropdown.
- The graph layout is precomputed at build time (`scripts/layout.mjs`, cytoscape + fcose headless) for the default view and the full network, cached in `.cache/layout.json`; keep its options in sync with `layoutGraph()` in `app.js`. PURSUE record summaries ship separately in `site/data/records.js`, loaded on demand.
- Real URLs, no `#/`: every view has a path (`/explore`, `/timeline`, `/world`, `/videos`, `/documentaries`, `/books`, `/a-z`, `/claims`) and every file its own page (`/person/david-fravor`, `/incident/…`, `/video/<title>`; `?t=SECONDS` seeks a video; on the Videos page the address also records the file open beside the player, `/video/<title>?t=SECONDS&file=person/<name>`, so a copied link reopens the same moment and file; phones ignore `file`). On desktop a file's own address opens it full width (reading view); `?panel=side` opens it in the side panel beside the network instead. `?explore` (or `?explore=1`) opens it as “Explore from here” would: a trail from the file, with the file in the side panel on desktop and the graph alone on phones (ignored for claims, records and news). Shareable link for a file seen in context: `/concept/government-ufo-disinformation?explore`. `scripts/pages.mjs` writes one HTML page per view and per file into `site/` (gitignored): the app shell from `templates/app.html` (edit that, not `site/index.html`) plus a server-rendered article with title, description, canonical, Open Graph, JSON-LD and the full content (facts with sources, links; video pages carry moments and the full transcript), plus `sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt`. The sitemap's `<lastmod>` is when each page's visible content (title, description, body) last changed, frozen in `content/lastmod.json` (commit it; hash-based, seeded from git, never written on Netlify). The app removes the article on boot. PURSUE records get pages with `noindex` and stay out of the sitemap. Slugs are frozen in `content/slugs.json` (commit it) so renaming an entry never breaks a link; to change one on purpose, edit that file. View paths live in both `VIEW_PATH` (pages.mjs) and app.js. Use absolute paths (`/assets/…`, `/data/…`) in the app — pages live at nested URLs.
- Latest (`/latest`, homepage strip, `site/feed.xml` RSS): entries of the `LATEST.types` whose event `date` falls in the last 90 days (or up to 60 days ahead, marked upcoming), sorted by that date; then older material newly filed, sorted by `added`. `date` is always when the thing happened (hearing day, first broadcast or release, statement, document release, incident), never the upload or filing date. `added` (when the entry entered the archive) is frozen in `content/added.json` (commit it; back-filled from git, new entries get the build date; frontmatter `added:` overrides; the initial import of 2026-10-17 is the baseline and never counts as recent). Any file can carry a picture: `image: /assets/images/<file>.jpg` (16:9, about 1600×900, committed under `site/assets/images/`) with `image_caption:` saying what it is and where it comes from (say so when it is an illustration); it shows on the Latest card, at the top of the file's page and as its share image. To pin an entry at the top of Latest (and first in the homepage strip), add `featured: YYYY-MM-DD` (the date it stops being pinned); it then drops back into its dated place. To keep an entry off Latest, the homepage strip and the feed (e.g. a hearing announced but not yet scheduled), add `latest: false`; remove it once confirmed. Keep `LATEST` in sync between `scripts/pages.mjs` and `app.js`.
- `npm run check` — validate (dangling ids, bad statuses, missing files)
- Caching and updates: `pages.mjs` stamps the app's code and data with a content hash (`/assets/app.js?v=…`, `/data/kb.js?v=…`; lazy files like `records.js`, transcripts and doctext get `?v=<BUILD>`), so a deploy reaches every visitor on their next page load; `/data/*` is cached for a year, pages are never cached. Add a new script or stylesheet of our own through that versioning, never as a bare URL. The boot loader in `templates/app.html` fetches the data with a progress bar (shown after 400 ms) and runs it in order, falling back to plain script tags. Open tabs compare `window.BUILD` with `/version.json` (no-store) when they come back into view and every 10 minutes: a new deploy shows an “updated · Reload” notice, and the next move to another section reloads by itself.
- Latest bot: a daily Claude Code routine (07:00 Bangkok) follows `bot/latest.md`: finds news from the last 3 days about entries on file (`npm run watchlist`), files the best 2–4 as `content/news/` items with their source texts in `sources/news/<id>.txt`, and opens a `latest/<date>` pull request (Netlify deploy preview; merging publishes). `npm run check:news -- <files>|--changed` verifies every quote word for word against the saved source text, the anchor rule and duplicates; `bot/seen.json` remembers what was considered. Edit `bot/latest.md` and `bot/sources.json` to tune it.
- `npm run fetch -- <youtube url|id> [--download]` — metadata + transcript into `media/<id>/`, creates a stub `content/video/` entry. YouTube rate-limits (HTTP 429): fetch in small batches.
- `npm run import:pursue [-- --download "regex"]` — refresh the war.gov/UFO PURSUE catalog into `content/record/` (drives local Chrome; importer-owned fields refresh, user notes below the marker are kept)
- `npm run add:doc -- <url> <id>` — download a document into `library/docs/` (falls back to Chrome when sites block scripts), extract text, OCR scans; then write `content/document/<id>.md` with `files:` and facts citing `source: library/docs/<id>.pdf`
- Non-YouTube videos (X, news sites): download with `yt-dlp -o media/<id>/video.mp4 <url>` (e.g. id `x-<postid>`), write `media/<id>/meta.json` (title, channel, date, duration, url), transcribe with `node scripts/transcribe.mjs <id> [--translate] [--lang xx]` (Whisper large-v3-turbo, model in ~/.cache/whisper-cpp), and create `content/video/<slug>.md` with `media: <id>` and `url: <post>`. Local copies play only on the dev server; by default the public site embeds or links the original post. Re-hosting a third-party video publicly is the owner's decision, case by case: if approved, make it faststart (`ffmpeg -c copy -movflags +faststart`), add a `thumb.jpg`, set `stream:`/`thumb:` on the video entry, and run `npm run r2:push`, which uploads exactly the files named by every video's `stream:`/`thumb:` lines (the rest of `media/` stays local).
- Web sources get an “archived” link beside them (the latest Wayback Machine snapshot, `archived()` in app.js and `sourceHtml` in pages.mjs). `npm run archive:sources` (after a build) checks every cited page and asks the Internet Archive to save those with no snapshot yet, slowly; results in `.cache/archived.json`. Our own copies in `sources/web/` stay local (copyright): they are for checking quotes word for word.
- `node scripts/ocr.mjs <pdf>` — OCR a scanned PDF into a sibling `.txt`
- Full-text search: every `files:` entry with a sibling `.txt` is split into pages (on form feeds) into `site/data/doctext/<id>.js` and searched from the top search bar (“Search inside documents”, results in the dropdown; video transcripts are searched on the Videos page); hits open the PDF at `#page=N`. Keep the `.txt` committed — it is the search index. Bulk collections carry `search: optin` and are left out of it: the MoD UFO files (`library/docs/mod-ufo/`, ~36,000 OCR'd pages, one document per National Archives release; files that back a case get their own document without the flag) are searched on their own page, `/mod-files` (`site/mod-files.html`, hand-maintained, in the More menu; `?q=` and `?release=Aug 2009`), from a Pagefind index of one record per PDF page built by `scripts/mod-index.mjs`. The index lives on R2, not in the Netlify build: after changing the files or release documents run `npm run r2:index` (builds it with PDF links to FILES_BASE and syncs `site/pagefind-mod/` to R2; Netlify proxies `/pagefind-mod/*` there). The main document search shows only a line “N pages in the MoD UFO files mention …” linking to that page. `npm run index:mod` builds it locally for the dev server.
- Documents: verify every supplied link by reading the downloaded text before filing (links from lists are often wrong). **Verify every quoted passage word-for-word against the downloaded text** before using it — pasted summaries have repeatedly contained altered or fabricated “exact quotes” (e.g. a misquoted UAPDA legacy-program definition; an invented AARO sentence). Quote only from our own copies. Record provenance (who released it, when, record ID). Mark compilations and mirrors as secondary sources and trace their claims to primary documents.
- Analytics: Google Analytics loads only after consent in the EEA/UK/Switzerland (banner + `site/privacy.html`); elsewhere it runs by default with opt-out via “Cookie settings”. The visitor's country is injected by the Netlify edge function `netlify/edge-functions/geo.js` (replaces `<!--GEO-->` in `index.html`); unknown country → banner. Disabled on localhost. Besides page views (including Back/Forward), app.js sends events after consent: `search` (search_term, search_type), `select_content` (content_type, item_id, method = where it was opened from), `moment_click` / `video_play_click`, `video_start` / `video_progress` (25/50/75%) / `video_complete`, `reading_view` (expand/collapse of the reader panel, by button or the R key), `explore_start` (a trail started from the key files: method `starting_point` or `key_files`), `explore_full` (Show the full network), `file_open` (PDFs), `link_out` (external links, by domain), `social_click` (our X and GitHub links: network = x | github, placement = header | more-menu | home-footer | side-footer; marked with `data-social`/`data-placement`) and `filter_change`.
- Heavy binaries (library PDFs/images, media video) are gitignored and synced to Cloudflare R2 with `npm run r2:push` / `r2:pull` / `r2:check` (rclone remote `r2`, bucket `wherearethey`): all of `library/`, plus the self-hosted video files named by `stream:`/`thumb:`. Commit the `.txt` extractions, never the binaries.
- Original seed notes: `notes/original-notes.md`. Raw PURSUE catalog snapshot: `sources/pursue/`.

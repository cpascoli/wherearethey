NUISANCE CORRESPONDENCE - MOD (AIR) BLACKLIST PROCEDURE

Ref A: D/S10(Air)/45/9/6 dated 8 June 84

1. It is five years since the MOD(Air) nuisance correspondence
procedure was ou#ined at Ref A. As an aid to new staff and to
update branch titles, this revised version has been produced.

2. Correspondence with people outside the Department can reach a
stage where they persist in writing about a particular subject
even though the fullest possible answer has been given on several
eccasions. Where this occurs, it is usual for the person to be
declared and listed as a nuisance correspondent so that any
further correspondence from them, on the subject in question, is
intentionally not answered, so long as no new points relevant to
the case are raised.

3. When a correspondent has reached the nuisance stage, a
summary of the case with a recommendation for blacklisting should
be prepared and referred to the Head of Division concerned, with
all the correspondence, for a decision on blacklisting. This
action should, however, be regarded only as a last resort and
should be taken, in the case of Ministerial correspondence, only
after agreement with the relevant Private Office. If blacklisting
is agreed, it will normally be on the understanding that a final
letter is then sent to the correspondent informing him or her that

can, however, be requested to agree to the termination of
correspondence. Assuming such agreement is reached, any new MP
involved thereafter should receive background information and be
asked to agree to the correspondence remaining closed.

Thank you for your letter to my predecessor, Section 40, | on the subject of .
Unidentified Flying Objects (UFO's). - . * ™

I think it would be helpful if I begin by explaining that the Ministry of Defence
was content that the Rendlesham incident was of no defence significance because
whatever was witnessed was not apparently hostile. As you know, our interest in
the UFO phenomenom is limited to the question of defence. We are satisfied that
no threat to the UK's defences has yet occurred.

With regard to your request for information, as you are already aware, because of
the provisions of the Public Records Act we are unable to release details of
reports ‘ten bloc’. Therefore it will not be possible to release’a ‘small

sample' of reports. As we have stated previously, we may be able to help with
specific enquiries about particular incidents, but to answer an enquiry as general
as yours is not permissible under current legislation.

Finally, I regret that we do not have figures for the numbers of reports received
in the years prior to 1980. The files in question are now in MOD archives, and

we have neither the staff nor the resources to facilitate the research this
question entails. However, I can tell you that during 1989 we received 258 reports.

I hope that you will find this helpful.

Thank you for your report to Humberside Police of an unusual sighting you witnessed
on 11 December, which has been passed to the Ministry of Defence.

You may find it useful if I begin by explaining that while the Ministry of Defence_.
does receive and co-ordinate some reports of UFO sightings, our sole concern is :
to determine whether or not they present a threat to the security of the United
Kingdom. Unless we judge that they do, and this has not been the case so far,

we do not attempt to investigate further or te identify whatever might have been
seen.

Given this limited involvement with the subject, the MOD does not have. any
department which is appointed solely for the purpose of studying UFO reports,

nor do we have any staff employed on the subject full time. I am sure that

you will appreciate that defence funds are already fully committed to the
continuing security of the United Kingdom against, primarily, aggression from

the Warsaw Pact countries. This being so, we could not justify the use of.
defence funds on scientific investigations unless a clear threat to the security
of the UK had been identified.

In this context, I regret that I am unable to offer you a definitive explanation
for what you witnessed. I hope you will understand that because no threat to
the UK's defences has been established, we do not intend to investigate further.
Nevertheless, we are grateful to you for taking the time to make a report.

I hope that you will find this helpful.

I refer to your letter of 20 November addressed to Sec(AS)2. As this office is
responsible for the Department's records under the provisions of the
Public Records Acts we have been asked to reply on their behalf.

Under section 5(1) of the Public Record Act of 1958 public records are not made
available for public inspection for 50 years from their creation. This period
was subsequently reduced to 30 years under section 1 of the Public Records Act of
1967 and this 30 year period before public access is granted to official records
is what is commonly referred to as "the 30 year rule". This restriction applies
to all official records, irrespective of whether they are classified or not.

Our enquiries have indicated that there are no Ministry of Defence records
relating to UFOs in the Public Record Office at Kew. A House of Lords debate on
the topic on 4 March 1982 revealed that all UFO records prior to 1967 has not
been preserved. Those created after that date are subject to the 30 year
retention rule.

It seems, therefore, that there are no official papers on this subject presently
available in the PRO and in their absence I can only refer you to commercially
available books and publications which may indicate other primary source material.

I am sorry we cannot be of more assistance to you in this matter.

Yours faithfully

Deputy Departmental

Thank you for your letter of 11 November concerning the subject of
Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs).

Although you are indeed correct to assume that the Air Staff :
Secretariat acts as the Ministry of Defence's focal point for those
who wish to write on this particular topic, I must correct the,
perhaps generally held,misconception that the MOD knows more about
UFOS than it is prepared to reveal. This is not the case. The
department's involvement is, in fact, very limited and our sole
concern with sighting reports of UFOs is to determine whether or not
they present a threat to the security of the United Kingdom. Unless

we judge that they do, and this has not been the case so far, we do

not attempt to investigate further or to identify whatever might have
been seen. Similarly, we do not undertake studies into the UFO
phenomenon, nor do we have the resources to carry out general surveys
or indepth investigations. I am sure that you will appreciate that
defence funds are already fully committed to the continuing security

of the UK against, primarily, aggression from the Warsaw Pact countries.
This being so, we could not justify the use of defence funds on
scientific investigations unless a clear threat to the security of the
UK had been identified.

As such, I regret that I am unable to offer you a definitive explanation
for what you witnessed, and because no threat to the UK's defence has
been established, you will understand that we do not intend to
investigate the incidents. Nevertheless, we would like to thank you

for taking the time to pass on the reports.

I hope that you will find this helpful.

UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTS

In writing on this topic one must first establish one's
eredibility. I am an architect, retired from PSA at Grade 6 on
reaching 60 in 1985, but remain in the Civil Service employed
by MoD as an AO in the Map Research and Library Group, Mapping
and Charting Establishment RE, at Tolworth. I was a Captain in
the Royal Engineers in WW2 and afterwards in the TA.

My family and I have experienced a number of UFO sightings over
the years but have never been able to discover the reporting
centre until a recent event prompted enquires among colleagues
and led to yourself as the focal point.

The two sightings I first wish to report have features in
common, were both made from this address and seen by both my
wife and myself:-

1. On Saturday, 6 September 1980, at about 04.25 hrs

My wife was awake and, on looking out of the window, (the
full width of the room and facing due east), noticed a red
bright darting light, flashing at regular intervals, making
enormous leaps across the sky at high altitude at very high
speed. She immediately woke me and we watched the object
together for some time before it became fainter as it moved
towards the horizon. It made dramatic changes of course at
what appeared to be each flash and appeared to cover the
whole field of visible sky in its erratic movements.
2.

2. On Tuesday, 31 October 1989, at approx 18.30 hrs

Having just returned from work, I was in the garden looking
for our cat when I saw a similar bright red light, flashing
at regular intervals, moving fast at high altitude from the
east directly overhead. The object was not however on a
straight course although its general direction was westwards
over the house. I called my wife from the kitchen and we
observed the phenomenon together, remembering what we had
seen in 1980. This object made sudden minor changes of
direction, of not more than perhaps 20 degrees, but more
remarkably, its speed was seen to vary at random, sometimes
slowing down and then accelerating. We had to move into
the middle of our lawn to watch it move over the roof when,
just before it got to the horizon, there appeared to be a
bright white flash to its south, after which it vanished.

Neither of these objects moved with the characteristics to be
expected of aircraft, satellites or ballistic missiles,
although, being relatively near Heathrow, I am aware that
aircraft display various forms of flashing navigation lights
after dark. The great height and speed of the objects
suggested another order of space technology?

One other sighting I would mention, although not documented at

the time, was seen by various members of_the famil by friends
glice. We then lived at Section 0
when in August 1979 at about ° hrs, my

daughter and a friend noticed a large bright light, stationary
most of the time but occasionally moving slowly, fairly low in
the sky to the west. It appeared to have red and green glows
rotating about an axis and its relative position and movement
were easily fixed against trees. In due course the whole
family and friends were alerted and as the object moved in this
way for over an hour, we called the police and had the
satisfaction of hearing a police sergeant say to control over
his car radio "I see what they see" before it disappeared.

We logged the sighting with a UFO society someone recommended
and were visited next day by a bunch of nutters who showed us
pictures of spaceships and aliens and whom we got rid of as
quickly as possible.

It is generally said that MoD knows more than it is prepared to
reveal and this becomes more acceptable in view of the advent
of Star Wars weapon systems. We only ask to be told as much as
we need to know!

Thank you for your letter of 30 October, in which you raised a number of points
relating to the subject of Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs).

I think it would be helpful to reiterate that the vast majority of the sighting
reports the Ministry of Defence receives come from members of the public, and
that since 1968 we have kept a running tally of the reports received because of
the interest the general public has in the subject. Similarly, I would like to
repeat that unless we consider UFOs are a threat to the security of the United
Kingdom, and this has not been the case so far, no attempt is made by the MOD
to investigate or identify whatever might have been seen.

You also asked for details of the unusual incident witnessed at Rendlesham Forest
in December 1988. The only information that we have on this event is a copy of
the report submitted by Lt Col Charles Halt USAF. I enclose a copy of this
report for your information. Again, the Ministry of Defence did not consider it
necessary to investigate the incident, as it was felt that no threat to the UK's
defences and security had occurred. We are not aware of the existence of an
audio tape recording of the incident.

Finally, with regard to your follow-up letter which we received on 6 November,
we do not have any information on the sighting at Lakenheath in August 1965,
since all our records prior to 1968 were destroyed in accordance with the
appropriate procedures at the time. I should also point out that we are only
able to answer specific queries about events which have occurred recently, ie,
within the last three years, as we have neither the staff nor the resources to
facilitate searches through our archives.

I hope that you will find this helpful.

Thank you for your report to RAF St Athan of an unusual sighting which
you witnessed on 25 August, a copy of which has been passed to the
Ministry of Defence.

You may find it helpful if I begin by explaining that while the
Ministry of Defence does receive and co-ordinate some reports of UFO
sightings, our sole concern is to determine whether or not they
present a threat to the security of the United Kingdom. Unless we
judge that they do - and this has not been the case so far - we do
not attempt to investigate further or to identify whatever might have
been seen.

Given our limited involvement with this subject, the MOD does not have
any department which is appointed solely for the purpose of studying

UFO reports, nor do we have any staff employed on the subject full time.
I am sure that you will appreciate that defence funds are already

fully committed to the continuing security of the United Kingdom against,
primarily, aggression from the Warsaw Pact countries. This being so,

we could not justify the use of defence funds on scientific investiga-
tions, unless a clear threat to the security of the UK had been
identified.

In this context, I regret that I am unable to offer a definitive
explanation for what you witnessed, although it is possible that the
light you saw was reflected, rather than being emitted by the object.

Thank you again for taking the time to make a report. I hope you will
find this reply helpful.

Thank you for your letter to the Secretary of State for Defence on the subject
of Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs). I have been asked to reply.

I think it would be helpful if I begin by explaining that whilst we do receive
and co-ordinate some information about UFO sightings, the sole concern of the
Ministry of Defence is to determine whether or not UFOs present a threat to
the security and defence of the United Kingdom. Unless we judge that they

do, and this has not been the case so far, no attempt is made by MOD to
investigate or identify the objects.

The Ministry of Defence has no department which is appointed solely for the
purpose of studying reports of UFOs and we have no staff who are employed on
the subject full time. The sighting reports we receive, which are usually
limited to the brief details of the incident passed on to us by those who
witnessed them, are referred to our staff in the departments which are
responsible for the air defence of the United Kingdom, and they examine the
reports as part of their normal duties.

Since our interest in UFOs is limited to possible defence implications we do

not carry out studies into the UFO phenomenon nor do we have the resources to
undertake any in depth investigations or general surveys. I hope that you will
appreciate that defence funds are already fully committed to the continuing
security of the UK against, primarily, aggression from the Warsaw Pact countries.
This being so we could not justify the use of defence funds on scientific
investigations, unless a clear threat to the security of the UK had been
identified.

More specifically, you asked for information on the number of sighting reports
we receive. Although I regret that because of our limited resources it has
not been possible to establish the exact origin of the reports I can confirm
that we have received the following number since 1980.

1980 ~ 350 1984 ~ 215
1981 - 600 1985 ~ 175
1982 ~ 250 1986 - 120
1983 - 390 1987 ~ 150

1988 ~— 400
Nevertheless, from past experience, I would say that only a very small proportion
of our reports originate from defence related sources.

You also enquired if any reports have been backed up by physical evidence. As

far as we are aware, no reported sighting has been accompanied by a definitive
photograph or similar and we have no record of any radar traces caused by

alleged UFO activity. I should point out, however, that the UKs Air Defence radar
(UKADGE) acts as part of the overall defensive umbrella protecting our country
and, as such, is targetted to scan the approaches to the UK rather than our
homeland.

With regard to the further release of information on this subject, I regret
that all MOD files, like those of other government departments, are subject to
the Public Records Act. As you may know, this Act states that official files
(be they classified or unclassified) remain closed from public viewing for

30 years after the last action has been taken. There is no UK equivalent to
the American Freedom of Information Act. Nevertheless, we have been given
dispensation to answer specific queries about particular incidents which have
occurred recently, and if we have a report on it, to let you know what it
contains.

I hope that you will find this helpful.

Thank you for your letter to the Prime Minister on the subject of Unidentified
Flying Objects (UFOs). This has been passed to the Ministry of Defence because
of our interest in the subject.

I think it would be useful if I begin by explaining that while the Ministry of
Defence does receive and co-ordinate some reports of UFO sightings, our sole
concern is to determine whether or not they present a threat to the security
of the United Kingdom. Unless we judge that they do, and this has not been
the case so far, we do not attempt to investigate further or identify whatever

might have been seen.

Given this limited involvement with the subject, the MOD does not have any
department’ which is appointed solely for the purpose of studying UFO reports,
nor do we have any staff employed on the subject full time. As you will expect,
therefore, we do not carry out studies, investigations or surveys into the
phenomena nor do we have the resources to do so. I hope you will appreciate
that defence funds are already fully committed to the continuing security of
the UK against, primarily, the threat of aggression from the Warsaw Pact
countries. This being the case, we could not justify the use of defence funds
onscientific investigations, unless a clear threat to the security of the UK
has been identified.

Nevertheless, we do, of course, recognise that there are many strange things
to be seen in the sky, by we believe that rational explanations can be found
for most of them. Indeed, we have yet to receive conclusive evidence that
alien spacecraft have landed on the planet.

I hope that you will find this information helpful.

Thank you for your recent letters to the Secretary of State for Defence and the

Chief of the Defence Staff which enclosed your article on "Irregularities in
Radio Communication". I have been asked to reply.

As I mentioned in my letter of 16 December 1988, we are grateful to you for
bringing this matter to our attention. Nevertheless, I would like to reiterate
that the Ministry of Defence has no knowledge whatsoever of the matter you
raise. I would also like to reassure you that we do not believe that what you
describe constitutes a breach of the security of the United Kingdom. I hope
you will understand, therefore, that we do not consider that an investigation
into the matter is warranted.

Given our very limited interest in this, I hope you will accept that no further
purpose will be served in continuing with this correspondence and that we should
now consider the matter closed. I am sorry I can be no further assistance.

Thank you for your letter of 14 September which described an unusual
sighting witnessed by a signalman at Blackpool North railway station
on June 10th.

You may find it helpful if I begin by explaining that while the
Ministry of Defence does receive and co-ordinate some reports of UFO
sightings, our sole concern is to determine whether or not they
present a threat to the security and defence of the United Kingdom.
If we do not feel that this is so, and this has been the case so far,
we do not attempt to investigate further, or identify whatever might
have been seen.

Given this limited involvement with the subject the MOD does not have
any department which is appointed solely for the purpose of studying
UFO reports, nor do we have any staff employed on the subject full
time. As you will expect, therefore, we do not carry out studies,
investigations or surveys into the phenomena, nor do we have the
resources to do so.

In this context, I regret that I am unable to offer a definitive
explanation for what was witnessed. However, I can confirm that

RAF Air to Air refuelling aircraft were not in the area at the time
in question and that, in general, RAF flights are uncommon at
weekends. It is therefore unlikely that RAF aircraft would have been
seen.

I hope you will find this helpful.

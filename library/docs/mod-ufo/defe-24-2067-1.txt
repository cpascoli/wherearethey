Please find enclosed a copy of the report entitled Unidentified Aerial Phenomena in the UK Air
Defence Region written in December 2000 as you requested on 11 November 2005, in accordance
with the Freedom of Information Act 2000. I apologise for the length of time this request has
taken, but the meticulous scrutiny this document has received has enabled us to release the vast
majority of the report and I hope you will be pleased with the final result.

The report consists of 465 pages divided into four parts, three Volumes and an Executive
Summary. While we have endeavoured to release as much information as possible, it has been
necessary to remove some information and where this is the case the appropriate Section of the
Freedom of Information Act has been indicated beside the redaction. The Sections and the reasons
for their use are given below. Sections 26, and 27 are qualified exemptions and in accordance
with the provisions of the Freedom of Information Act we have looked at the balance of public
interest for and against disclosure for each Section. Details of the public interest test and our
conclusions are also detailed below.

Section 26 (Defence) ~ This information consists of details of the operation and performance of
UK Air Defence radar. In favour of disclosing this information is the public interest in promoting
understanding of the RAF’s ability to detect and effectively respond to potential threats in UK.
airspace. It is important to recognise public anxiety with regard to possible attacks on the UK,
particularly from acts of terrorism and the need to reassure the public that everything possible is
being done to protect them. However, this public interest has already been served to a
considerable extent by the publication of the Sixth Report of the House of Commons Defence
Committee in 2002. In the report the Committee outline the measures the Government takes to
protect the public from terrorist activity including threats from the air.

Against the limited public interest in disclosure, is a countervailing public interest which favours
withholding the information in order to preserve the effectiveness of the UK’s air defences. The
release of this information could be of significant value to the planning of an attack on the UK,
including from terrorism. There is therefore a strong public interest in preserving the RAF’s
ability to defend the British mainland through the effectiveness of its air defences. and we
therefore conclude that the balance of the public interest is firmly in favour of withholding the
information in accordance with Section 26(1)(a) & (b).

information from our allies, concerning issues of mutual benefit, with a degree of confidentiality.

test.

Further to your e-mail message of 8" November, I can confirm that I have received your request
for information. I apologise for not replying sooner.

I can advise you that this Department (DAS) currently have two electronic databases containing
information about the UFO sighting reports we have received. These databases do not have names
and are not classified. The first of these was started in 2000 purely in order for us to track
incoming reports and correspondence. It contains only details of the date and time of a reported
sighting, name, address and telephone number of the individual making the report, our file
reference and the date we responded to the individual. It contains no details of the actual reports
and is not used in order to investigate or analyse any sightings, but for administration purposes
only. It is held electronically on an internal MOD system that can only be accessed by myself and
my colleague who also deals with UFO correspondence. Personal data about those who made
UFO reports or corresponded with the MOD will not be released in accordance with the Data
Protection Act 1998. The only meaningful information that could therefore be released is the date
and times of sightings and the town / village and county. These details are contained in the second
database as detailed below.

The second database is still under construction and is being developed in order to assist with
public enquiries. Following the introduction of the FOI Act in January 2005, this department
received a large number of requests for information about UFO sightings in particular areas of the
UK or on particular dates. Our paper records are not constructed in this way and we therefore
decided to create a database containing brief details of all the UFO sightings for which we
currently hold records, divided into the years in which the sightings occurred. As we hold records
from 1985 to date, Iam sure you will appreciate this is a large project which is still on going. We
have, however, completed the database for the years 2002 up to 2005 and I enclose details from
those years. Extracts of the database have also been released in response to specific Freedom of
Information requests and these can be viewed in the Reading Room of the MOD FOI Publication
Scheme at www.foi.mod.uk. Once completed it is our intention to put the whole database into the
MOD FOI Publication Scheme.

Finally, you may be aware that in the past a few other Departments within the MOD received
copies of ‘sighting reports’ of unexplained phenomena and one of these was

Defence Intelligence 55 (DI5S5). DISS felt there was nothing of Defence Intelligence value in
these reports, so during a policy review in 1997, in order to back up this view, they conducted a
small study of historical records from 1987 to 1997 to determine if there was any reason for them

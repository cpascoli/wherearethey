1. You will have seen that US of S(AF) has agreed that the
MoD should not participate in the proposed programme on UFOs
currently being considered by the BBC as a topic for a future

Wogan Show.

2. You will wish to go back to Section 40] the BBC

researcher who raised this proposal, advising him of the
department’s decision and offering him a statement of MoD policy
on UFOs (attached).

3. We would of course be very interested to hear if the BBC
intend to go ahead with the programme without any MoD
involvement.

Section 40
MOD STATEMENT ON UFOs

1. The sole interest of the Ministry of Defence in reported
sightings of Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs) is to establish
whether or not they have any bearing on the defence of the
country. Information on UFOs is evaluated only as one part of the
Department’s overall interest in the air defence of the United

Kingdom.

2. The MoD has no section dedicated to the study of UFOs,
nor any staff employed on the subject full time. Sighting reports
that are received are referred to air defence staffs who check
that they do not provide evidence of a threat to the continuing
security and defence of the UK or an unauthorised incursion into
UK airspace. Unless it is judged that there is a threat, and this
has not been the case so far, the MoD does not attempt to
investigate or identify whatever might have been seen.

3. Because MoD’s interest in UFOs is limited to possible
defence implications the Department does not undertake general
surveys or studies into the scientific significance of the
phenomenon, nor does the MoD have the resources to do so. Defence
funds are already fully committed to the continuing security of
the UK against, primarily, the threat of aggression from the
Warsaw Pact countries. As such the use of finite resources on
scientific investigations could not be justified, unless a clear
threat had been identified.

4, Most reputable UFO organisations in the UK are fully
aware of the MoD’s limited involvement with this subject .and, as
far as can be ascertained, accept that the MoD in no way attempts
to conceal activity from the general public.

Thank you for your letter to the Prime Minister on the subject of Unidentified Flying
Objects (UFOs) which, as the appropriate Department, has been passed to the Ministry
of Defence for reply.

AS you will be aware from our previous correspondence, although the MOD does receive
and co-ordinate some reports of UFO sightings, our sole concern is to determine
whether or not they present a threat to the security of the United Kingdom. If we do
not feel that this is so, and this has been the case so far, we do not attempt to
investigate the incident further, or identify what might have been seen. Additionally,
the MOD does not have any department which is appointed solely for the purpose of
studying the phenomena, nor do we have any staff employed on the subject full time.

As you will expect, therefore, we do not carry out investigations into the phenomena,
nor do we have the resources to do so.

With regard to your second, as far as we are aware, there has been no further debate
on the subject of UFOs in the House of Lords. And, as I have said in the past, I
would like to assure you that there is no attempt to conceal the "truth" about the
phenomena, or cover up incidents reported to us. Indeed, it would be a nonsense to
attach such significance to a simple lack of staff, resources and direct interest in
the subject.

I hope you will find this reply helpful.

THE WOGAN SHOW — POSSIBLE FEATURE ON UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTS

1. US of S(AF) will wish to be aware that the Department has
been approached by a researcher from the BBC Wogan Show regarding
a feature they are considering on Unidentified Flying Objects
(UFOs).

2. If the feature is to go ahead, and this is still far from
clear, the programme will focus on the allegations of a
Government cover up of the ‘true’ facts of the UFO phenomenon.
The researcher claims to have an expert who has seen classified
MoD decuments which confirm that the Department has a section
dedicated to the study of UFO activity and seeks to ensure that
this knowledge is concealed from the public. As the Minister will
be aware, although Sec(AS) receives and coordinates information
that the MoD receives on reported sightings, the Department does
not attempt to carry out scientific investigations or general
surveys into the subject.

3. To balance the programme, which we understand would be
broadcast either at the end of the month or in the autumn, the
BBC have asked whether the appropriate Minister, in this case US
of S(AF), would be willing to appear on the programme and answer
the "expert". .

4. The MoD’s Line on UFOs, which has been deployed over many
years, emphasises that the Department’s interest is strictly
limited. There is little that can be added to this, and MoD
representation is unlikely to affect the outcome of the programme
one way or another. Indeed participation might reinforce the
misconception that the MoD has a significant interest in the UFO
phenomenon. On balance, therefore, we would advise against
participation by a Minister, or indeed any other MoD
representative, offering to the BBC instead a statement of Mob
policy on UFOs along the lines of the attached draft. It is
possible that this approach may also cause the programmers to
reconsider the value of the feature since without someone to
counter the allegations of a "cover up", it will be difficult for
the lively debate, which they are seeking to generate, to take
place.
5. I should be grateful to know if US of S(AF) is content
for the suggestion of MoD participation to be politely declined
and for the attached statement of MoD policy, which is in keeping
with that adopted in all Parliamentary and public correspondence
on this subject, to be forwarded to the BBC via the Defence Press

Office.

Thank you for your letter of 2 June which enclosed an
account of an alleged Unidentified Flying Object (UFO) being
studied by the MoD on Foulness Island. You asked for my comments
on this particular case.

I think it would be helpful if I explain that the Proof
and Experimental Establishment (P&EE) at Shoeburyness is
responsible for the firing, and hence testing, of batches of
ammunition coming in to service with the United Kingdom Armed
Forces. They, like all other MoD establishments, do not involve
themselves with the study of unidentifiable ‘alien’ craft. As
such, I can only say that the story you have obtained from your
informant is ridiculous in the extreme. Certainly there has never
been any form of UFO on Foulness Island and similarly the MoD has
not made any contact with alien lifeforms.

As requested I have endeavoured to treat Mr Cain's
statement seriously but fear that I have found it to be the best
fiction I have read in a long time. Nevertheless I have the
following observations to make. Firstly, if the object was such
an important find, why was it towed to the UK for investigation?
Surely the Americans would wish to examine it in house.
Similarly, do you not find it strange that nobody saw the object,
which Mr Cain states as having a circumference of nearly a mile,
when it was being towed across the Atlantic behind a Battle
Cruiser. This is notwithstanding the fact that the Royal Navy
does not operate such ships nowadays. In addition there are a
number of contradictions in the article, for instance at one ;
stage he says that the object was encased in concrete, at another
he mentions that it was taken to Australia. Apparently
overnight! There are numerous other incredible statements but I
do not feel that I need mention these to prove my point.

Although I doubt that this was the answer you would have
wished for, I hope you will find some food for thought in the

above.

closure they remain closed from public viewing for 30 years. However, we
are able to answer any specific queries you may have about particular
ineidents which have occurred recently and can let you know whether we
have a report on the incident and if so what it contained.

Thank you for your letter of 4 May which asked about our
policy on the subject of Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs).

I think it would be helpful to say that we do not have a
"policy statement" as such, indeed, our involvement with the
subject is minimal. Nevertheless, we do receive and co~ordinate
reports of UFO sightings that have been sent in to us by members
of the public, although our sole concern is to determine whether
or not the sightings present a threat to the security and defence
of the United Kingdom. Unless we judge that they do, and this has
not been the case so far, the Ministry of Defence does not
attempt to investigate or identify whatever might have been
witnessed.

Because of our limited involvement with the phenomenon,
the Ministry of Defence has no department which is appointed
solely for the purpose of studying reports of UFOs and we have no
staff who are employed on the subject full time. The sighting
reports we receive, which usually are limited to the brief
details of the incident passed onto us by those who witnessed
them, are referred to our staff in the departments which are
responsible for the air defence of the United Kingdom, and they
examine the reports as part of their normal duties. Additionally,
we do not carry out full time studies into the phenomenon nor do
we have the resources to undertake any indepth investigations or
general surveys. I hope that you will appreciate that defence
funds are already fully committed to the continuing security of
the UK against, primarily, the threat from the Warsaw Pact
countries. This being so we could not justify the use of defence
funds on scientific investigations, unless a clear threat to the
security of the UK had been identified.
Nevertheless it is clear from the reports that we
receive that many strange things have been observed in the sky,
although we believe that rational explanations could probably be
found for most of them. Possibilities that spring to mind include
satellite debris re-entering the earth’s atmosphere, ball
lighting, unusual cloud formations, meteorological balloons,
aircraft lights, aircraft at unusual angles or a host of other
things. Certainly, we have yet to receive any conclusive evidence
to suggest that alien spacecraft or beings have landed on the
planet.

I hope that you will find this helpful.

HIGH G AND HIGH G PROTECTION — AEROMEDICAL AND
OPERATIONAL ASPECTS

WEDNESDAY 21st OCTOBER 1987

The Aviation Medicine and Test Pilots Group of the Royal Aeronautical Society will hold a one day Symposium on
High G and High G Protection - Aeromedical and Operational Aspects.

The agility of air combat aircraft has increased to the extent that the limited G tolerance of their crews is now
restricting the overall system performance. Of particular relevance are the sustained turn rates achievable — 9G or
more — and the rate at which this acceleration level can be applied — within 1 sec or less. This latter factor can
lead to a potentially disastrous G-induced loss of consciousness (G-LOC).

The opening papers will address current operational aspects, the physiological mechanisms which determine G
tolerance and the reported incidence of G-LOC in the Royal Air Force. Papers in session two will describe equipment
for G protection, both current and developmental, including anti-G sutts and valves, regulators to give positive
pressure breathing related to G and their in-flight evaluation, and the potential for detecting early G-LOC so as to
initiate autorecovery. Papers in the final session will be concerned with possible methods for enhancing G tolerance
and their influence on future aircraft designs.

Thank you for your letter of 2 March 1989 which asked for
the Ministry of Defence’s comments on the subject of Unidentified
Flying Objects (UFOs). As the MoD’s point of contact with those
who wish to correspond on this particular topic, your letter has
been passed to me for a reply.

Before I attempt to answer your questions I feel it would

be helpful if i explain that, contrary to the advice given in
Secton 40) book, the MoD has a very limited interest in the
phenomenon. Indeed, our sole concern is to determine whether

or not reported sightings of UFOs present a threat to the
security and defence of the United Kingdom and unless we judge
that they do, and this is not normally the case, no attempt is
made by the MoD to investigate or identify whatever might have
been seen. The vast majority of information that we have on the
subject are sighting reports, usually limited to the brief
details of the incident, passed on to us by those who witnessed
them. These are referred to our air defence staffs who examine
them as part of their normal duties.

Since our interest in UFOs is limited to possible defence
implications we do not carry out studies into the UFO phenomenon
nor do we have the resources to undertake any indepth
investigations or surveys. Moreover, we have no department which
is appointed solely for the purpose of studying reports of UFOs
and we have no staff who are employed on the subject full time.

Moving now to your particular questions, you will
understand that because of our limited involvement with the
subject we have little information on it. However, I can
categorically confirm that the MoD does not possess any
conclusive evidence which suggests that alien spacecraft or
beings have either landed in or have communicated with this
planet. As far as sightings within this country are concerned, I
think it would be useful to say that from the reports that we
have received there appears to be no evidence to suggest any
connection between military installations and the location of
sightings; I would suggest further that we have received more
from areas which are not connected with the military.

On a more general note, we would not disagree that
strange things have been (and are being) observed in the sky,
certainly the reports we receive bear this out. Nevertheless, we
believe that rational explanations can probably be found for
many sightings. Possibilities that spring to mind include
aircraft lights, Meteorological balloons, satellite and meteorite
debris and ball lighting to mention but a few.

I hope that you will find this helpful.

Thank you for your letters of 3 and 4 March to the Ministry of Defence on the subject
of Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs). As the MODs point of contact for those who

MOD with this subject is to determine whether or not UFOs present a threat to the
security and defence of the United Kingdom and, unless we judge that they do, and
this is not usually the case, we do not attempt to investigate or identify whatever
might have been seen.

Given our limited involvement with the subject, the MOD does not have any department
which is solely appointed/studying or collecting data on UFO reports nor do we

have any staff employed on the subject fulltime. The vast majority of information
that we have are sighting reports, usually limited to the brief details of the
incident passed on to us by those who witnessed them, which are referred to staff
in the departments responsible for the air defence of the United Kingdom and they
examine them during the course of their day to day routine.

Nevertheless it is quite clear from the reports that we receive that many strange
things have been and are being observed in the sky, although we believe that

rational explanations could probably be found for most of them. Possibilities that
spring to mind include satellite debris re-entering the earth's atmosphere, ball
lighting, unusual cloud formations, meteorological balloons, aircraft lights, aircraft
at unusual angles to mention just a few.

You also asked about viewing any information on UFO sightings that we might have
received. Although I regret that the release of any material on the subject

"en bloc" is net sossible because of the provisions of the Public Records Act, which
states that official files should generally remain closed from public viewing for
30. years after the last action has been taken, we are able to answer specific
queries about varticular incidents which have occurredrecently and can let you
know whether we have a report on the incident and if so what it contains, However,
due to our limited resources, I regret that I can not instigate general searches
through our files.

Thank you for your letter of 23 March and indeed your previous of the Autumn
of last year. I am sorry for the delay in replying.

Before looking at your latest letter in detail I think it would be helpful if I
explain our involvement with the subject of Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs), as

it appears that there might be some misunderstanding. Whilst I regret that there

is some delay in replying to individuals corresponding on the subject, I should say
that this is caused, primarily, by our Ministerial and Parliamentary work which we
carry out on behalf of the Air Force Department. I hope you will understand,
therefore, that this work, which is not insubstantial, must take priority over
requests for information such as yours, however regrettable that may be. Additionally
because of our heavy workload on RAF operational matters we do not have the resources
to spend a large amount of time on each UFO enquiry that we receive.

As you may already know, the only information that we have on UFOs are the sighting
reports passed on to us by those who witnessed them. These are usually limited to
the brief details of the incident and are referred to our staff in the departments
responsible for the air defence of the United kingdom who examine them during the
course of their normal duties. But unless we judge that a sighting report presents
a threat to the security and defence of the United Kingdom, and let me assure you
that this is not normally the case, we do not attempt to investigate or identify
whatever might have been seen. From this background I hope you will understand that
for the most part we do not possess any information, other than any reports that

we might have received, on incidents which involve unusual aerial activity. Moreover,
I would expect that more often than not, because of your specific interest in the
subject, UFO societies would have more information than we do.

Moving to the detail in your letters I would, first of all, like to say that for
the reasons above it is extremely unlikely that MOD personnel would have been
investigating the incident witnessed at Little Hayward, Staffordshire, and would
therefore like to rule out this possibility. Nevertheless, we did receive a report
which is similar to the one that you describe and I attach a copy of the report

for your information but in line with our policy to maintain the privacy of the
originator, the identifying details have been obscured.

around the airport.

Thank you for your letter of 26 February to the Secretary of State for
Defence on the subject of Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs). I have
been asked to reply.

I think it would be helpful if I first of all explain that whilst we do
receive and co-ordinate some information about UFO sightings, the sole
concern of the Ministry of Defence is to determine whether or not UFOs
present a threat to the security and defence of the United Kingdom.
Unless we judge that they do, and this is not normally the case, we do
not attempt to investigate or identify whatever might have been seen.

Given our limited involvement with the subject, the MOD does not have
any department which is appointed solely for the purpose of studying UFO
reports nor do we have any staff employed on the subject full time. As
you will expect, therefore, we do not carry out studies,in-depth
investigations or surveys into the UFO phenomenon, nor do we possess the
resources to do so. Nevertheless, we do, of course, recognise that many
strange things are to be seen in the sky, although we believe that
rational explanations could probably be found for most of them.
Possibilities that spring to mind include satellite debris re-entering
the earth's atmosphere, ball lightning, unusual cloud formations,
aircraft lights to mention just a few.

Moving to your specific request, I regret that given our very limited
involvement with this particular subject, we in the MOD do not possess
the necessary information to give you the advice that you require.
However, on a more positive note, I believe that one of the many
organisations set up by enthusiasts specifically to study the UFO
phenomenon are more likely to be in a position to give you the

Thank you for your letter of 9 February which gave details of an unusual sighting
that you witnessed on Tuesday 31 January 1989.

Before looking at this sighting in particular, I think it would be helpful if I
explain our involvement with the subject.of Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs)
generally. Whilst we do receive and co-ordinate some information about UFO sightings,
the sole concern of the Ministry of Defence is to determine whether or not they
present a threat to the security and defence of the United Kingdom. Unless we

judge that they do, and this is not normally the case, no attempt is made by MOD

to investigate or identify whatever might have been seen.

The vast majority of information that we have on the subject are sighting reports,
usually limited to the brief details of the incident passed on to us by those who
witnessed them, such as yourself, and these are referred to our staff in the departments
which are responsible for the air defence of the United Kingdom and they examine

them during the course of their day to day routine.

We have no department or agency which is appointed solely for the purpose of

studying UFO reports and we have no staff who are employed on the subject full time.

This being the case we do not have the resources to undertake any further investigations,
general surveys or studies into the UFO phenomenon.

Furthermore, I'm sure that you will appreciate that defence funds are already fully
committed to the continuing security of the UK against, primarily, the threat of
aggression from the Warsaw Pact countries. As such we could not justify the use of
defence funds on scientific investigations, unless a clear threat to the security of
th UK had been identified.

Having said all that it is clear from the reports that we receive that many strange
things have been and are being observed in the sky, although we believe that

rational explanations could probably be found for most of them. Possibilities that
spring to mind include satellite debris re-entering the earth's atomsphere, ball
lighting, unusual cloud formations, meteorological ballons, aircraft lights, aircraft
at unusual angles to mention but a few.

Turning to your particular sighting, I would like to confirm that we received two

other reports which corresponded to the details that you gave, but regrettably

these are not as informative as yours. Nevertheless, I attach copies of the reports

but in line with our policy to maintain the privacy of the originators, their
identifying details have been observed.

I hope you will find this helpful. However, because we have not investigated the
incident I regret that I can not offer you a definitive explanation as to what the
object might have been, although it seems to me that it could have been a fireball

of some sort.

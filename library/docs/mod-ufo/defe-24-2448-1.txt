Thank you for your Freedom of Information request of 4 June 2008 asking for a copy
of the ministerial submission on the transfer of UFO files dated 18 September 2007 and the response
dated 25 September 2007. Additionally, you asked for copies of any dialogue directly related to the
two documents which took place between those dates. Finally, you asked for details of any formal,
minuted, meetings regarding the transfer of the UFO files.

The ministerial submission and response are withheld under s.35(Formulation of Government
Policy). Section 35(1)(a) of the Act provides that information may be exempt from disclosure if it
would, or would be likely to, prejudice the formulation or development of government policy. The
decision to proactively release such a large number of files to The National Archives (TNA), was
quite clearly a matter of government policy.

This is not the end of the matter, of course; section 35 is a qualified exemption, meaning that the
MOD must assess the balance of public interest in release of the information. We have done this at
several levels and although the arguments are the same as outlined in my e-mailed dated 11
December 2007 in response to your FOI request 08-10-2007-090830-001, I reproduce them below:

i. There is an undoubted public interest in the public being able to assess the quality of advice
being given to ministers and subsequent decision making based on that advice. Given the level of
interest in the matter amongst a small but vociferous section of the public, and the probability that ill
informed speculation and conspiracy theories are likely to be rife, it is reasonable that the public
have a right to know why the Ministry of Defence has made its decision to release the UFO/UAP
files. .

ii. Greater transparency makes government more accountable to the electorate and increases
trust; if by releasing these discussion papers we were to demonstrate that the decision to proceed was
one taken on good advice and following a sensible debate, this may have a positive effect in
increasing public confidence in government decision-making. However, if the advice and debate
behind policy-making were routinely disclosed, officials may be less willing to offer frank advice or
ministers may be less willing to explore the full range of policy options. There is a risk that officials
could come under pressure not to challenge ideas in the formulation of policy, thus leading to poorer
decision making. Ministers and officials also need to be able to conduct rigorous and candid risk
assessments of their policies in a free space without constant scrutiny. Discussions surrounding the
release of these files are still on going. This is a major exercise, which I believe is unique in MoD
history. The MoD will be proactively placing a large number of files less that 30 years old in TNA in
both paper and importantly, electronic format. The practical difficulties of this are still under
discussion and may well continue to be for some time. As we work on the transfer of these files to
TNA, we are coming across a number of challenges and need to be able to discuss and seek solutions
without the concern that our discussions will be placed in the public domain while this process is
ongoing.

Taking the above arguments into account, in this case, we believe that it would not be in the public
interest to disclose the specific information you have asked for surrounding the decision to place the

Thank you for your Freedom of Information request of 26 April 2008. You asked a

In addition to volume of requests, what other factors contributed to the eventual decision to
release the UFO files?

The overriding reason to release the files to the public through The National Archives was to reduce
the level of work taken up in answering UFO related FOI requests. Additionally, it was considered
that the Freedom of Information Act had made eventual, if piecemeal, full release inevitable. By
establishing our own programme of release, the files could be placed in the public domain in an
orderly fashion avoiding duplication of effort. There were also considered to be presentational
advantages to the MoD in releasing this information voluntarily. Additionally, it was considered that
the files themselves contained minimal amounts of information of direct defence relevance.

To what extent did these other factors influence the decision to release?

This question is difficult to answer satisfactorily as all the above factors needed to be considered in
the decision making process. However, if you are looking for external influences other than volume
of work, then I am afraid that there are none. I am aware that there has been a considerable degree of
speculation that the French release of their own files played a part in our decision to release our own
files. Other than noting it increased media and internet speculation on the subject of UFOs, this is
simply not the case. There has also been speculation that the decision of the MoD to release its UFO
files is part of an international agreement, allegedly to prepare the worlds population for the news
that aliens are amongst us. I am afraid any such speculation is pure fantasy.

When was the idea of a wholesale release first suggested?

The notion of a wholesale release has been discussed within DAS for some years and is certainly not
new. It was considered by my predecessor but not taken up primarily due to the heavy workload
created by answering FOI requests. I had discussed the matter with her informally (it was not within
my remit at the time) whilst she was still in post in 2006 and held informal discussions with my line
managers and my opposite number in DIS, when I first covered the post on a temporary basis in late
2006 and early 2007.

What specifically triggered the idea?

The DIS decision to review their files for release caused DAS to revisit the question of release
themselves.

Details of meetings to discuss release?

The MoD held, and continues to hold, a number of meetings to discuss the release. These have
involved, at various times, staff from DAS, DIS, Corporate Memory, MoD Press Office and The

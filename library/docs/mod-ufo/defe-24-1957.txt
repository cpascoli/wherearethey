Thank you for your letter dated 10 November, in which you asked about the '
Government's view on UFOs, and the position of the Ministry of Defence. In
fact, I have already been contacted by one of your associates. I explained that
T would not be able to conduct an interview, but sent a letter setting out our
policy. I explained that this could be used to illustrate our policy and views.
The following paragraphs - which I have already included in a letter to your
colleague ~ set out this information.

Although the Ministry of Defence does receive and co-ordinate reports of UFO
sightings, our only concern is to establish whether or not they pose a threat to
the security of the United Kingdom. Unless we judge that they do ~ and this has
not been the case so far ~ we do not attempt to investigate further, or to
identify whatever might have been seen.

Given this limited involvement with the subject, the Ministry of Defence has no
department which is appointed solely for the purpose of studying UFO reports,
nor are there any staff employed on the subject full time. The reports we
receive, which are usually limited to very brief details of what was seen, are
passed to staff in departments concerned with the air defence of the United
Kingdom, who examine them as part of their normal duties.

Since our interest in UFOs is limited to possible defence implications, we do
not carry out studies into the UFO phenomenon, nor do we have the resources to
undertake any in-depth investigations. Defence funds are fully committed to
maintaining the security of the UK and meeting our responsibilities to allies
and dependents. This being so, we could not justify the use of defence funds on
scientific investigations, unless a threat to the security of the UK had been
identified.

It is clear from the reports we receive that there are many strange things to
be seen in the sky. However, we believe that explanations could be found for
most of them. Some of the possibile explanations include aircraft lights or
aircraft seen from unusual angles, satellites in orbit or satellite debris
re-entering the atmosphere, high altitude kites, helium balloons, weather
balloons, searchlights or lasers reflecting off clouds, ball lightning,
fireballs and meteorites. As you are also doubtless aware, Some reports turn
out to be hoaxes. Having said this, we accept that there will always be some
sightings that defy explanation, and we have an open mind on these.

Despite some of the stories that are sometimes told, there is no question of our

when we spoke about the crash of a Lightning F6 aircraft, on 8 September 1970.

Following a number of approaches from researchers, who had heard stories that
this aircraft had crashed during an encounter with a UFO, I tracked down the
Aircraft Accident Report (AAR) for this particular crash. This document is
classified Restricted, as is the case for all AARs, and cannot therefore be
released. From my reading of this document I can tell you that the Lightning
was taking part in a Tactical Evaluation Exercise designed to practise the night

into the sea while attempting to intercept one of the Shackletons. There is no
indication of any unidentified target having been encountered, and no reason to
suggest that there is any sort of UFO incident in any way connected with this
tragic crash.

As I mentioned, I am trying to track down further papers relating to this
accident, in the hope that there will be an Unclassified Military Aircraft
Accident Summary (MAAS), that I can release. Given the time that has elapsed
since the accident, there are no guarantees that I will be successful. I will
let you know what, if anything, I track down.

Thank you for your letter dated 26 October to the Secretary of State for
Defence, in which you asked about UFOs and extraterrestrial life. I have been
asked to reply.

Although the Ministry of Defence does receive and co-ordinate reports of UFO
sightings, our only concern is to establish whether or not they pose a threat to
the security of the United Kingdom. Unless we judge that they do - and this has
not been the case so far - we do not attempt to investigate further, or to
identify whatever might have been seen.

Given this limited involvement with the subject, the Ministry of Defence has no
department which is appointed solely for the purpose of studying UFO reports,
nor are there any staff employed on the subject full time. The reports we
receive, which are usually limited to very brief details of what was seen, are
passed to staff in departments concerned with the air defence of the United
Kingdom, who examine them as part of their normal duties.

Since our interest in UFOs is limited to possible defence implications, we do
not carry out studies into the UFO phenomenon, nor do we have the resources to
undertake any in-depth investigations. Defence funds are fully committed to
maintaining the security of the UK and meeting our responsibilities to allies
and dependents. This being so, we could not justify the use of defence funds on
scientific investigations, unless a threat to the security of the UK had been
identified.

It is clear from the reports we receive that there are many strange things to
be seen in the sky. However, we believe that explanations could be found for
most of them. Some of the possibile explanations include aircraft lights or
aircraft seen from unusual angles, satellites in orbit or satellite debris
re-entering the atmosphere, high altitude kites, helium balloons, weather
balloons, searchlights or lasers reflecting off clouds, ball lightning,
fireballs and meteorites. As you are also doubtless aware, some reports turn
out to be hoaxes. Having said this, we accept that there will always be some
sightings that defy explanation, and we have an open mind on these.

Despite some of the stories that are sometimes told, there is no question of our
attempting to cover up any incidents or keep any information about UFOs from the
public. The Ministry of Defence does not possess any evidence which supports
the existence of alien life, or suggests that extraterrestrial spacecraft have
ever visited the earth. Again, we are open-minded about the possibilities.

The Ministry of Defence does not release material on the subject "en bloc",
because, as is the case with all government departments, all Ministry of Defence
files are subject to the public records act, and generally remain closed from
public viewing for 30 years after the last action has been taken. Prior to 1967
all UFO files were routinely destroyed, although a small number of files
survived, and may be viewed at the Public Record Office. The files we hold in
this office go back to about 1985, so we are able to answer specific queries
that people have about particular sightings which have occurred in recent

years, and can let them know whether we have a report on the incident, and if
so, what it contains. Most of the reports that we have are relatively
unexciting, and refer to little more than a vague shape or light in the sky.

When we spoke last Friday I promised to let you have a letter setting out the
Ministry of Defence’s policy and views on UFOs, together with some other related
information that you may find useful. Hopefully your daughter will be able to
draw upon the information in this letter when writing her essay.

Although the Ministry of Defence does receive and co-ordinate reports of UFO
sightings, our only concern is to establish whether or not they pose a threat to
the security of the United Kingdom. Unless we judge that they do, and this has
not been the case so far, we do not attempt to investigate further, or to
identify whatever might have been seen.

Since our interest in UFOs is limited to possible defence implications, ve do
not carry out studies into the UFO phenomenon, nor do we have the resources to
undertake any in-depth investigations. Defence funds are fully committed to
maintaining the security of the UK and meeting our responsibilities to allies
and dependents. This being so, we could not justify the use of defence funds on
scientific investigations, unless a threat to the security of the UK had been
identified.

It is clear from the reports we receive that there are many strange things to
be seen in the sky. However, we believe that explanations could be found for
most of them. Some of the possibile explanations include aircraft lights or
aircraft seen from unusual angles, satellites in orbit or satellite debris
re-entering the atmosphere, high altitude kites, helium balloons, weather
balloons, searchlights or lasers reflecting off clouds, ball lightning,
fireballs and meteorites. As you are also doubtless aware, some reports turn
out to be hoaxes. Having said this, we accept that there will always be some
sightings that defy explanation, and we have an open mind on these.

Despite some of the accusations that are sometimes made, there is no question of
our attempting to cover up any incidents or keep any information about UFOs from
the public. The Ministry of Defence does not possess any evidence which
supports the existence of alien life, or suggests that extraterrestrial
spacecraft have ever visited the earth.

The Ministry of Defence does not release material on the subject "en bloc",
because, as is the case with all government departments, all Ministry of Defence
files are subject to the public records act, and generally remain closed from

Thank you for your letter dated 9 October in which you asked a number of
questions about the MOD’s policy and role with regard to UFO sightings.

You asked whether our UFO statistics included sightings reported by military
personnel such as RAF pilots. The figures that I give are always total figures,
and include all sightings reported to us, irrespective of the occupation of the
witness. Having said this, we do not break sightings down by category, and I am
not able to give you any information on how many sightings have been reported by
the military. Any sightings by military witnesses should be reported to us in
the usual way. I would probably have noticed a report from a military witness,
but do not recall having seen one in my time here. The only cases that I am
familiar with that involve military witnesses are the RAF Topcliffe sightings in
1952, and Lt Col Halt’s report.

You also asked about our conclusion that, to date, UFOs have not posed any
threat to national security. We look at all reports of UFO sightings - as do
staff in those departments responsible for the air defence of the UK - to
determine whether there seems to be a threat to the UK’s security. The fact is
that to date we have not found any evidence of such a threat. While sightings
are often unidentified, it does not follow that this means that there isa
threat to security. The key consideration in determining any threat is
evidence, and the simple fact is that we do not possess any. Given our scrutiny
of UFO reports, I do not agree that the information we hold is "unevaluated".
O£ course, as you know from previous correspondence, the reports we hold are
almost all limited to fairly vague details of lights or shapes in the sky.

Finally, you asked about UK studies into the UFO phenomenon. I am not aware of
any such studies, although our views do mirror the general findings of the US
Project Blue Book study, which concluded that there was no evidence that UFOs
are extraterrestrial, or that alien life exists. However, in common with the
Americans, we are open-minded about the possibilities. Like you, I will be
watching the current NASA SETI project with interest!

When we met yesterday I promised to let you have a written note setting out the
Ministry of Defence’s position on UFOs. ’

While the Ministry of Defence does receive and co-ordinate some reports of UFO
sightings, our only concern is to establish whether or not they pose a threat to
the security of the United Kingdom. Unless we judge that they do, and this has
not been the case so far, we do not attempt to investigate further, or to
identify whatever might have been seen.

Given this limited involvement with the subject, the Ministry of Defence has no
department which is appointed solely for the purpose of studying UFO reports,
nor are there any staff employed on the subject full time. The reports we
receive, which are usually Limited to very brief details of what was seen, are
passed to staff in departments concerned with the air defence of the United
Kingdom, who examine them as part of their normal duties. I have attached a
copy of the form that ve use to record details of a UFO sighting.

Since our interest in UFOs is limited to possible defence implications, we do
not carry out studies into the UFO phenomenon, nor do we have the resources to
undertake any in-depth investigations. I am sure you will appreciate that
defence funds are fully committed to maintaining the security of the UK and
meeting our responsibilities to allies and dependents. Consequently, we could
not justify the use of defence funds on scientific investigations, unless a
threat to the security of the UK had been identified.

It is clear from the reports we receive that there are Many strange things to be
seen in the sky. However, we believe that explanations could be found for most
of them. Possibilities that spring to mind include aircraft lights or aircraft
seen from unusual angles, kites, helium balloons, weather balloons, unusual
cloud formations, satellites in orbit or satellite debris entering the
atmosphere, ball lightning, fireballs and meteorites. We accept, however, that

there will always be some sightings that defy explanation, and we have an open
mind on these.

I regret that the release of material from our files "en bloc” is not possible
because, as is the case with other government departments, all Ministry of
Defence files are subject to the public records act. AS you are aware, this act
states that official files generally remain closed from public viewing for 30

sightings, and can release examples of reports, although as is the case with any
report that we release, we will delete the name and address of any witnesses.
We will always do our best to answer any questions that people’have about the
UFO phenomenon, and will endeavour to keep the national groups such as BUFORA
informed of-any interesting developments.

views and policy on UFOs, and secondly, the actual UFO reports themselves.

These are far less exciting than most people would suppose; nearly all of the
sightings that have been reported to us relate to little more than a description
of a light or a shape in the sky. Of all the reports on the files we hold -

ufologists might classify as a close encounter of the third or fourth kind.

copies for you. I make no judgements on these reports, and leave researchers to

When we spoke earlier today I promised to write to you, setting out the position
of the Ministry of Defence with regard to UFOs, and detailing other related
information that you may find useful. Hopefully you will be able to draw upon
the information in this letter when making your documentary.

Although the Ministry of Defence does receive and co-ordinate reports of UFO
sightings, our sole concern is to establish whether or not they pose a threat to
the security of the United Kingdom. Unless we judge that they do, and this has
not been the case so far, we do not attempt to investigate further, or to
identify whatever might have been seen.

Since our interest in UFOs is limited to possible defence implications, we do
not carry out studies into the UFO phenomenon, nor do we have the resources to
undertake any in-depth investigations. Defence funds are fully committed to
maintaining the security of the UK and meeting our responsibilities to allies
and dependents. This being so, we could not justify the use of defence funds on
sclentific investigations, unless a threat to the security of the UK had been
identified.

It is clear from the reports we receive that there are many strange things to

be seen in the sky. However, we believe that explanations could be

found for most of them. Some of the possibile explanations include aircraft
lights or aircraft seen from unusual angles, satellites in orbit or satellite
debris re-entering the atmosphere, high altitude kites, helium balloons, weather
balloons, searchlights or lasers reflecting off clouds, ball lightning,
fireballs and meteorites. As you are also doubtless aware, some reports turn
out to be hoaxes. Having said this, we accept that there will always be some
sightings that defy explanation, and we have an open mind on these.

Despite some of the stories that circulate among ufologists, there is no
question of attempting to cover up any incidents, nor are we trying in any way
to obscure the truth about UFOs from the public. The Ministry of Defence does
not possess any evidence yhich supports the existance of alien life, or suggests
that extraterrestrial spacecraft have ever visited the earth.

The Ministry of Defence does not release material on the subject "en bloc",
because, as is the case with all government departments, all Ministry of Defence
files are subject to the public records act, and generally remain closed from

year's research is to see whether these initial findings can be replicated by
more exacting tests and placed on a basis which will justify publication to
the wider scientific community in peer-reviewed journals.Most of the current
work has had to be funded by CCCS, its American counterpart, and private donors;

help has, however, been offered on an informal basis by the Radiation Spectroscopy
Unit at Harwell Laboratories, the Physics Department at Glasgow University, and
several well qualified individuals in this country. In the United States the
University of Nevada and some other institutions and individuals are undertaking
a range of scientific tests of crop and soil samples and controls.

its agencies is taking a scientific interest in the phenomenon, although earlier
approaches made by us to MAFF had suggested otherwise. We shall shortly be
informing them again of our research programme.

be confidently assessed; and we shall not be rushing to publish them until
suitable peer-reviewing proves possible, It will, however, be CCCS*s continuing
policy to inform British government departments and agencies of any indications

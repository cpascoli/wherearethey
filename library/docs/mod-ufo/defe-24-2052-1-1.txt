I am writing concerning your request for documents about UFO landings and
abductions in the UK from 1940 to date. Your message has been passed to me
because this department is the focal point within the Ministry of Defence for
correspondence about UFOs.

I should explain that the MOD does not have any expertise or role in respect of
'UFO/flying saucer' matters or to the question of the existence or otherwise of
extraterrestrial lifeforms. We remain open-minded, but to date the MOD knows of no
evidence which substantiates the existence of these alleged phenomena and there is
therefore no official documents about UFO landings or abductions. Members of the
public occasionally write to the MOD alleging they have seen UFOs land or been
abducted by alien beings, but these letters are not held separately, but filed with all
other correspondence about UFOs in the order in which they were received. We
currently hold correspondence files covering a 25 year period and to search all of
them for any relevant letters would exceed the permitted £600 limit set for
compliance with the Freedom of Information Act 2000 and as provided by Section 12
of the Act, the Ministry of Defence is not obliged to conduct such a task.

You may wish to be aware that there is already some information about UFOs
available for public viewing. MOD files on UFOs were routinely destroyed after 5
years until 1967 when they were generally preserved for The National Archives. A
few have survived before 1967 and these together with records up to 1977 are now
available for viewing. The National Archives can be contacted at Ruskin Avenue,
Kew, Richmond, Surrey TW9 4DU or by telephone on 020 8876 3444. The National
Archives also have a website giving information about the records they hold and how
to access them. This can be found on the internet at
http://www.nationalarchives.go.uk. The Ministry of Defence Freedom of Information
Publication Scheme also contains documents relating to the well known events in
Rendlesham Forest, Suffolk in 1980 and the final report of the Flying Saucer Working
Party in 1951. These can be accessed via the internet at http://www.foi.mod.uk. A
search in the Scheme under ‘UFO’ will take you to this information. We are looking
to see if further information can be made available to the public and we are currently
compiling a database of reports which we intend to publish in the MOD Freedom of
Information Publication Scheme in the near future.

I am writing concerning your e-mail messages requesting information about UFO sighting reports.
Both of your messages have been passed to this Department as we are the focal point within the
Ministry of Defence for correspondence about UFOs. I apologise for not replying sooner.

As both of your requests refer to UFO sightings reported to the MOD and objects seen on radar, it
may be helpful if I explain the differences in procedures between the handling of these two
scenarios. Most people make ‘UFO’ sighting reports to the MOD because they have seen
something in the sky that they are unable to identify or because they believe they have seen
something of an extraterrestrial nature. The MOD does not have any expertise or role in respect of
'UFO/flying saucer’ matters or to the question of the existence or otherwise of extraterrestrial
lifeforms. Although we remain open minded, to date the MOD knows of no evidence as to the
existence of this alleged phenomena. The UFO sighting reports we received are examined solely
to establish whether they provide any evidence that UK airspace has been compromised by hostile
or unauthorised air activity. Unless there is evidence of a potential threat to the United Kingdom
from an external source, we do not attempt to identify the precise nature of each reported sighting.
We believe it is possible that rational explanations, such as aircraft lights or natural phenomena,
could be found for them, but it is not the function of the MOD to provide this kind of aerial
identification service. We could not justify expenditure of public funds on investigations which go
beyond our specific defence remit. Any UFO reports which we consider may be worthy of further
investigation are referred to staff within the MOD who have responsibility for air defence matters
for further examination. I should add that none of the UFO sighting reports received in recent
years have produced anything of defence concern. This procedure is followed for all reports
considered worthy of further investigation regardless of whether the report was made by members
of the public, military or civil pilots, or police officers.

The procedures for defending UK airspace are not connected to, or reliant on, people reporting
sightings of objects they are unable to identify. The integrity of the UK's airspace in peacetime is
maintained through continuous surveillance of the UK Air Policing Area by the Royal Air Force
and the overarching NATO air defence system. This is achieved by using a combination of civil
and military radar installations, which provide a continuous real-time “picture” of UK airspace.
Any threat to the UK would be handled in the light of the particular circumstances at the time
which might, if deemed appropriate, involve the scrambling or diversion of air defence aircraft.

Oy. also enquired about the reporting procedures between the Civil Aviation Authority (CAA),
the Home Office and the MOD. If civil airline pilots or police officers make a ‘UFO’ report,
these may be sent to this department and would be handled as described above. If a civil pilot
believes that his/her aircraft may have been endangered by the proximity of another aircraft or
object (or, in regulated airspace, where an Air Traffic Controller believes there has been a risk of
collision) they may file an airmiss report and this will be investigated by the UK Airprox Board
which is a joint military and civilian organisation. If there was no risk of collision, but a pilot
wishes to report an unusual occurrence during a flight, they might file a Mandatory Occurrence
Report and this would be investigated by the CAA. MOD may be contacted as part of these
investigations, if it is possible that military aircraft or activity was involved.

With regard to your request for information about the number of UFO sighting reports in the past
10 years from military and civilian sources, I should inform you that our records are not held
electronically but filed on paper files in the order in which they were received. The reports are not
segregated according to source and this means to identify the records you have requested would
involve a manual search of 72 paper files. It has been assessed that the cost of such an exercise
would exceed the permitted £600 limit set for compliance with the Freedom of Information Act
and as provided by Section 12 of the Act, the MOD is not obliged to comply with your request.
You may wish to be aware, however, that in order to assist members of the public we are
currently compiling a database of the sighting reports we have received which we hope to publish
in the near future. Personal data about the individuals who made the reports will not be shown in
accordance with the Data Protection Act 1998, but the database will include details of occupations
where known. This means it will be possible to see which reports were received from military or
civilian aircrew, Air Traffic Controllers etc. While the database will take some time to complete
we have completed the records for 2002 to April 2005 and I am able to supply the following
figures which are relevant to your requests.

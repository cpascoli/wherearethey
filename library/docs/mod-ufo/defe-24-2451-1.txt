Thank you for your recent letter asking for an explanation of the timetable for the
release of UFO information to the public.

Generally, Ministry of Defence (MoD) files are released under what is known as the 30 year
rule. This means that 30 years after they are finally closed, and if the MoD thinks they are
worth keeping, we will transfer them to The National Archives.

In the case of the UFO files, a commitment was made by the MoD to keep its files since they
might be of interest to the general public at a future date.

Following the introduction of the Freedom of Information Act 2000, the MoD was receiving a
large number of requests for information about UFOs and in 2007 it was decided in principle,
to transfer the existing files from the two branches interested in UFO matters to The National
Archives. The hope was that this would reduce the time taken up in answering FOI requests
on this topic and, at the same time, give the public a more accurate understanding of the very
limited MoD role in UFO matters.

The files must be prepared for release by redacting or blacking out individual’s personal data,
militarily sensitive information etc and this is a very time consuming task. It was estimated that
this would take about three years but we decided that rather than complete the whole task
and then release the files, we would do it is smaller chunks. | am sure you are aware of the
publicity surrounding the files we have already released.

You may also wish to note that the MoD places details of UFO sightings reported to it on its
website on an annual basis.

Dear ERIN

Thank you for your e-mail of 25 January 2009 asking how the MoD investigates UFO reports and
whether the MoD has to investigate over extended periods of time.

The MoD examines any reports of ‘unidentified flying objects’ it receives solely to establish whether
what was seen might have some defence significance; namely, whether there is any evidence that the
United Kingdom's airspace might have been compromised by hostile or unauthorized foreign
military activity.

Unless there is evidence of a potential threat to the United Kingdom from an external military
source, and to date no 'UFO' report has revealed such evidence, we do not attempt to identify the
precise nature of each sighting reported to us. We believe that rational explanations, such as aircraft
lights or natural phenomena, could be found for them if resources were diverted for this purpose, but
it is not the function of the MoD to provide this kind of aerial identification service. It would be an
inappropriate use of defence resources if we were to do so.

That having been said, we do occasionally investigate sightings reported to the MoD. This usually
involves liaising with air defence specialists (e.g. radar experts) on perhaps half a dozen sightings
per year. These will generally be sightings made by aircrew, members of the armed forces or the
police. Each case is viewed on an individual basis and it is not possible to predict how long an
investigation would take or what course it would follow. However, I can say that investigations are
limited in their nature and likely to be concluded within weeks, rather than months. I am not aware
of any investigation in recent years lasting for more than a few weeks.

Thank you for your e-mail of 8 January 2009 asking whether the Ministry of Defence
(MoD) was going to comment on recent reports of a UFO hitting a wind turbine in Lincolnshire.

The MoD examines any reports of ‘unidentified flying objects’ it receives solely to establish whether
what was seen might have some defence significance; namely, whether there is any evidence that the
United Kingdom's airspace might have been compromised by hostile or unauthorized foreign
military activity.

Unless there is.evidence of a potential threat to the United Kingdom, from an external military
source, and to date no 'UFO' report has revealed such evidence, we do not attempt to identify the
precise nature of each sighting reported to us. We believe that rational explanations, such as aircraft
lights or natural phenomena, could be found for them if resources were diverted for this purpose, but
it is not the function of the MOD to provide this kind of aerial identification service. It would be an
inappropriate use of defence resources if we were to do so.

The MoD is not aware of any substantive evidence to suggest the turbine was hit by a UFO and as
we have not received any physical evidence of an aircraft or other object flying into the turbine, we
do not intend to investigate further.

I hope this helps explain the position.

Thank you for your e-mail of 1 January 2008 to Secretary of State for
Defence, Des Browne, it has been passed to this office to answer as we are the branch with
responsibility for UFO matters within the MoD.

When the Ministry of Defence was informed of the events which are alleged to have
occurred at Rendlesham Forest/RAF Woodbridge in December 1980, all available
substantiated evidence was looked at in the usual manner by those within the MOD/RAF
with responsibility for air defence matters. The judgement was that there was no indication
that a breach of the United Kingdom's air defences had occurred on the nights in question.
As there was no evidence to substantiate an event of defence concern no further
investigation into the matter was necessary. Although a number of allegations have
subsequently been made about these reported events, nothing has emerged over the last
quarter of a century which has given us reason to believe that the original assessment made
by this Department was incorrect. Other than an obligation to respond to questions from the
public, the MoD has no further interest in the subject and considers the matter closed.

The MoD file on the Rendlesham Incident is already in the public domain via our website
and will be included in the general release of 160 UFO files that you mention in your e-mail,
which will also include policy files, correspondence files, sighting report files, Freedom of
Information request files and a small number of files regarding specific incidents or subjects
such as alien abduction.

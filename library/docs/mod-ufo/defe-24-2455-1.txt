Firstly, it may be helpful if | explain that the Ministry of Defence (MoD) examines any
reports. of ‘unidentified flying objects’ it receives solely to establish whether what was seen
might have some defence significance; namely, whether there is any evidence that the
United Kingdom’s airspace might have been compromised by hostile or unauthorised air
activity.

Unless there is evidence of a potential threat to the United Kingdom from an external
source, and to date no UFO report has revealed such evidence, we do not attempt to
identify the precise nature of each sighting reported to us. We believe that rational
explanations, such as aircraft lights or natural phenomena, could be found for them if
resources were diverted for this purpose, but it is not the function of the MoD to provide
this kind of aerial identification service. It would be an inappropriate use of defence
resources if we were to do so.

The MoD does not have any expertise or role in respect of ‘UFO/flying saucer’ matters or
the question of the existence or otherwise of extraterrestrial life forms, about which it
remains totally open-minded. | should add that to date, the MoD knows of no evidence
which substantiates the existence of these alleged phenomena.

Details of UFO sightings for the period 1997-2008 can be found on the MoD website
www.mod.uk by searching under “UFO Reports’. For the current year until the 30 June,
the MoD has recorded 240 reports relating to 2009, a further 8 relating to 2008 which were
not received in time to appear on the website and 1 for 2007. Details of sightings for 2009

Thank you for your Freedom of Information request of 3 August 2009 asking for details of UFO
sightings for York and North Yorkshire for the period April 1999 to April 2009 and asking what the
Ministry of Defence (MoD) policy on UFOs was.

Firstly, it may be helpful if I explain that the MoD examines any reports of ‘unidentified flying
objects’ it receives solely to establish whether what was seen might have some defence significance;
namely, whether there is any evidence that the United Kingdom’s airspace might have been
compromised by hostile or unauthorised air activity.

Unless there is evidence of a potential threat to the United Kingdom from an external source, and to
date no UFO report has revealed such evidence, we do not attempt to identify the precise nature of
each sighting reported to us. We believe that rational explanations, such as aircraft lights or natural
phenomena, could be found for them if resources were diverted for this purpose, but it is not the
function of the MoD to provide this kind of aerial identification service. It would be an inappropriate
use of defence resources if we were to do so. :

The MoD does not have any expertise or role in respect of ‘UFO/flying saucer’ matters or the
question of the existence or otherwise of extraterrestrial life forms, about which it remains totally
open-minded. The MoD investigates only a handful of UFO sightings per year, (usually from
aircrew, air traffic controllers, the police or military personnel) and to date, the MoD knows of no
evidence which substantiates the existence of these alleged phenomena.

To state with certainty which sightings had been the subject of some form of MoD investigation
would far exceed the £600 limit set for compliance with the Freedom of Information Act. This
represents the estimated cost of one person spending 3 and a half working days in determining
whether the Department holds the information, and locating, retrieving and extracting the
information. Under the terms of Section 12 of the FOI Act, this means that we are not obliged to
comply with this element of your request at this time

Details of UFO sightings reported to the MoD for the period 1997-2008 are available for viewing on
the MoD website www.mod.uk by searching under “UFO Reports“. I attach a spreadsheet containing
details of sightings received during the current year for Yorkshire until 30 July 2009. You will note
that for two of this years sightings we only have the month, not the date, of the sighting.

You might also be interested to know that the MOD has already released a great deal of information
about UFOs which is available for public viewing. MOD files were routinely destroyed after being
held for five years until 1967, when they were generally preserved for The National Archives. A few
have survived before 1967 and these together with records up to 1992 are now available for public
viewing. They can be contacted at Ruskin Avenue, Richmond, Kew, Surrey TW9 4DU or telephone,
020 8876 3444. The National Archives also have a website giving information about the records they
hold and how to access them. This can be found on the internet at www.nationalarchives.gov.uk.

Thank you for your e-mail of 26 June 2009 asking how much money has been spent by
the Ministry of Defence (MoD) investigating UFO sightings for each of the years 2008 and 2009.
Additionally, you asked how many UFO reports there had been for each of those years.

Firstly, it may be helpful if I explain that the Ministry of Defence (MoD) examines any reports of
‘unidentified flying objects’ it receives solely to establish whether what was seen might have some
defence significance; namely, whether there is any evidence that the United Kingdom’s airspace
might have been compromised by hostile or unauthorised air activity.

Unless there is evidence of a potential threat to the United Kingdom from an external source, and to
date no UFO report has revealed such evidence, we do not attempt to identify the precise nature of
each sighting reported to us. We believe that rational explanations, such as aircraft lights or natural
phenomena, could be found for them if resources were diverted for this purpose, but it is not the
function of the MoD to provide this kind of aerial identification service. It would be an inappropriate
use of defence resources if we were to do so.

The MoD does not have any expertise or role. in respect of ‘UFO/flying saucer’ matters or the
question of the existence or otherwise of extraterrestrial life forms, about which it remains totally
open-minded. I should add that to date, the MoD knows of no evidence which substantiates the
existence of these alleged phenomena.

The Ministry of Defence does not record the costs of investigating UFO sightings, (of which it
carries out a handful per year) and I am therefore unable to provide you with this element of the
information you requested. However, you may be interested to know that the MoD, which has the
lead across government in dealing with UFO matters, employs one fulltime civil servant (myself)
dedicated to dealing with the subject of UFOs. The post is at D grade, which is the most junior
management level within the Ministry. Whilst it would be inappropriate to provide individual salary
details, the pay range for D grade posts is £20,815-£27,159 per year.

In addition to my post, I receive some administrative support from an El Grade civil servant and my
civil service line manager has oversight of my work. Approximately 20% of the E1’s and C1’s time
is spent on UFO related matters. The annual pay range for an El is £15,847-£20,074 and for the C1
it is £32,623-£41,325. All three posts attract a standard local recruitment and retention allowance for
RAF High Wycombe of £2000 per year. As a rough guide therefore, based on the highest annual
salary, total costs are in the order-of £40,000 per year.

Details of UFO sightings for the period 1997-2008 can be found on the MoD website *. sv sodas
by searching under “UFO Reports”. For the current year until the 30 June, the MoD has recorded
240 reports relating to 2009, a further 8 relating to 2008 which were not received in time to appear
on the website and | for 2007. Details of sightings for 2009 will be placed on the MoD website in

of UFO sightings and reports and any follow up investigations, even if they were not undertaken by
the Ministry of Defence (MoD). Additionally, you asked for any files that have never been classified

The MoD has over 170 UFO files and the costs of searching for and preparing the information you
request for release, would far exceed the £600 limit set for compliance with the Freedom of
Information Act. This represents the estimated cost of one person spending 3 and a half working
days in determining whether the Department holds the information, and locating, retrieving and
extracting the information. Under the terms of Section 12 of the FOI Act, this means that we are not
obliged to comply with your request.

viewing. They can be contacted at Ruskin Avenue, Richmond, Kew, Surrey TW9 4DU or telephone.
020 8876 3444. The National Archives also have a website giving information about the records they
hold and how to access them. This can be found on the internet at www.nationalarchives.gov.uk.

1970’s up to 2007 to The National Archives by the close of 2010. It is hoped that files for the period
1992-2000 will be transferred by the end of this year and they will be made available for public
viewing on the internet.

searching under “UFO Reports”. UFO sightings for 2009 will also be placed on the website in
January 2010.

be found on our website by searching in the publication scheme under UAP. This report was
commissioned by the Defence Intelligence Service and produced by a civilian contractor. An earlier

Thank you for your Freedom of Information request of 12 May 2009 asking for any

Firstly, it may be helpful if I explain that the Ministry of Defence (MoD) examines any reports of
‘unidentified flying objects’ it receives solely to establish whether what was seen might have some
defence significance; namely, whether there is any evidence that the United Kingdom’s airspace
might have been compromised by hostile or unauthorised air activity.

Unless there is evidence of a potential threat to the United Kingdom from an external source, and to
date no UFO report has revealed such evidence, we do not attempt to identify the precise nature of
each sighting reported to us. We believe that rational explanations, such as aircraft lights or natural
phenomena, could be found for them if resources were diverted for this purpose, but it is not the
function of the MoD to provide this kind of aerial identification service. It would be an inappropriate
use of defence resources if we were to do so.

The MoD does not have any expertise or role in respect of ‘UFO/flying saucer’ matters or the
question of the existence or otherwise of extraterrestrial life forms, about which it remains totally
open-minded. I should add that to date, the MoD knows of no evidence which substantiates the
existence of these alleged phenomena.

communication, discussion or sharing of information on UFOs. You might wish to consider
contacting The National Archives at www.nationalarchives.gov.uk in case they have any such old
files. However, should this be the case, other UFO researchers would probably have mentioned them
by now. You may also wish to know that the MoD currently has a programme to release some 160
UFO files from the late 1970’s up to 2007 to The National Archives by the close of 2010. Files for
the period 1979-1992 have already been transferred and it is hoped those for 1992-2000 will be
transferred by the end of the year and they will be made available for public viewing.

they have any such files.

‘unidentified flying objects’ it receives solely to establish whether what was seen might have some
defence significance; namely, whether there is any evidence that the United Kingdom’s airspace
might have been compromised by hostile or unauthorised air activity.

Unless there is evidence of a potential threat to the United Kingdom from an external source, and to
date no UFO report has revealed such evidence, we do not attempt to identify the precise nature of
each sighting reported to us. We believe that rational explanations, such as aircraft lights or natural
phenomena, could be found for them if resources were diverted for this purpose, but it is not the
function of the MoD to provide this kind of aerial identification service. It would be an inappropriate
use of defence resources if we were to do so.

The MoD does not have any expertise or role in respect of ‘UFO/flying saucer’ matters or the
question of the existence or otherwise of extraterrestrial life forms, about which it remains totally
open-minded. I should add that to date, the MoD knows of no evidence which substantiates the
existence of these alleged phenomena.

Thank you for your Freedom of Information request of 4 May 2009 asking for any
Top Secret or “conspiracy” information into UFO research carried out by the Ministry of Defence
(MoD).

Firstly, it may be helpful if I explain that the MoD does not have any expertise or role in respect of
‘UFO/flying saucer’ matters or the question of the existence or otherwise of extraterrestrial life
forms, about which it remains totally open-minded. I should add that to date, the MoD knows of no
evidence which substantiates the existence of these alleged phenomena.

The Ministry of Defence (MoD) examines any reports of ‘unidentified flying objects’ it receives
solely to establish whether what was seen might have some defence significance; namely, whether
there is any evidence that the United Kingdom’s airspace might have been compromised by hostile
or unauthorised air activity.

Unless there is evidence of a potential threat to the United Kingdom from an external source, and to
date no UFO report has revealed such evidence, we do not attempt to identify the precise nature of
each sighting reported to us. We believe that rational explanations, such as aircraft lights or natural
phenomena, could be found for them if resources were diverted for this purpose, but it is not the
function of the MoD to provide this kind of aerial identification service. It would be an inappropriate
use of defence resources if we were to do so.

Turning to the specifics of your request, in light of the above, you will appreciate that the MoD does
not have any information into UFO research classified as Top Secret.

to date no ‘UFO’ report has revealed such evidence, we do not attempt to identify the precise

‘unidentified flying objects’ it receives solely to establish whether what was seen might have some
defence significance; namely, whether there is any evidence that the United Kingdom’s airspace
might have been compromised by hostile or unauthorised air activity.

Unless there is evidence of a potential threat to the United Kingdom from an external source, and
to date no ‘UFO’ report has revealed such evidence, we do not attempt to identify the precise
nature of each sighting reported to us. We believe that rational explanations, such as aircraft lights
or natural phenomena, could be found for them if resources were diverted for this purpose, but it
is not the function of the MOD to provide this kind of aerial identification service. It would be an
inappropriate use of defence resources if we were to do so.

The MOD does not have any expertise or role in respect of ‘UFO/flying saucer’ matters to the
question of the existence or otherwise of extraterrestrial life forms, about which it remains totally
open-minded. I should add that to date, the MOD knows of no evidence which substantiates the
existence of these alleged phenomena.

‘UFO’ sightings from Thetford in Norfolk in December 2007. We are satisfied that there is no

We have now reviewed the radar data for the date, time and location given in your email dated
27 October.

There were several airliners in the vicinity, at high altitude, at around 12:10pm that day. There
were also numerous light aircraft, not under air traffic control, flying at much lower altitudes in
the same area. The light aircraft pilots were providing their own separation from other aircraft
by keeping a look out and using the ‘see and avoid’ principle, which is standard practice in
uncontrolled airspace.

Having viewed the radar data, the most likely explanation is that there was an airliner tracking
South to North at 25,000ft at 12:10pm. A light aircraft can be seen tracking West to East at
this time, close to the lateral path of the airliner but at a much lower altitude. It is not possible
to identify the light aircraft or its actual height using the radar information. However, it is
possible that these aircraft tracks may have appeared to cross each other, when viewing the
aircraft from the ground. It is notoriously difficult to judge horizontal and vertical separation
from the ground, as aircraft may be separated by thousands of feet vertically but they appear
to be at the same level depending on aircraft size and trajectory.

In the UK it is a legal requirement that, in respect of commercial air transport operations, an
Airprox report is filed, by a pilot and/or an air traffic controller, within four days. This time has

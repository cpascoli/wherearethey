I am writing concerning your Freedom of Information request for details of UFO sightings in
Bromley and Bexley. Your request has been passed to this Department as we are the focal point
within the Ministry of Defence for correspondence regarding UFO matters. Several attempts to
send this information by e-mail have failed, so I am replying by post. If you have now received
my e-mails, please ignore this letter.

You have not specified a time period which interests you, so it might be helpful if I explain what
information we hold and what is already available. The MOD files containing UFO reports
received between 1967 and 1978 are fully open for viewing at The National Archives in
Richmond, Surrey. A few records have survived from the 1950s and early 1960s and these are
also available. The National Archives on line catalogue and details of how to access these records
can be viewed on their website at www.nationalarchives.gov.uk. Records from 1979 to date are
still held by the MOD. These consist of records of UFO sightings reported to the MOD and public
correspondence about UFOs (some of which also contain sighting reports). These records are not
held electronically, but are filed on paper files in the order in which they were received. They are
not segregated by geographical area.

With regard to your particular request, it is not possible for us to search all of our records for
sighting reports specifically from Bromley and Bexley without exceeding the permitted £600 limit
set for compliance with Section 12 of the Freedom of Information Act 2000. However, we are in
the process of compiling a database of sightings which has now been completed for the years
2002 to date. A search of these records has revealed only one sighting from Bromley during this
period. There were none from Bexley. Details of the Bromley sighting are as follows;

At 02.25 on 17 November 2003 two adults and a child in Bromley saw 20-30 red flashing
lights in the sky accompanied by a whirring noise. The family called the Police who
confirmed the sighting. The Policeman using a lamppost as a marker concluded that the
lights were zigzagging/ turning across the sky at speeds faster than any man-made
aircraft. The crew of a Police helicopter which was airborne at the time also confirmed
they saw the lights, but thought they might be lights of aircraft on the approach to London
Heathrow Airport.

We do not hold details of the exact location of the witnesses, nor their age or sex. As for your
questions about the MOD’s response, I should inform you that the MOD examines reports of
UFO sightings solely to establish whether there is any evidence that the United Kingdom's
airspace might have been compromised by hostile or unauthorised air activity. Unless there is
evidence of a potential threat to the United Kingdom from an external source we do not attempt to
identify the precise nature of each reported sighting. We believe it is possible that rational
explanations, such as aircraft lights or natural phenomena, could be found for them, but it is not
the function of the MOD to provide this kind of aerial identification service and we could not
justify expenditure of public funds on investigations which go beyond our specific defence remit.
On this occasion the MOD was satisfied that this report did not represent anything of air defence
interest.

information is added whenever possible.

I am writing concerning your request for information about military aircraft activity in the area
around Sanquhar and Carronbridge Village at 10.30pm on 9 February 2006.

As explained in my previous correspondence, military aircraft do not fly fixed flight paths, but
plan their sorties individually. Fixed wing aircraft travel hundreds of miles on an individual sortie,
so aircraft seen in any area, do not necessarily originate from a RAF Station in the immediate
vicinity. The flight plans for these sorties are held by each Squadron and with the exception of
aircraft conducting low flying training (below 2000 feet) no records are kept centrally. It is,
therefore, not possible to identify whether there were any aircraft in the Sanquhar / Carronbridge
area at the time you have specified without checking the individual flight plans of any aircraft that
could have been flying. The cost of conducting such a task would exceed the permitted £600 limit
set for compliance with the Freedom of Information Act 2000 and as provided by Section 12 of
the Act, the Ministry of Defence is not obliged to comply with your request. I can, however,
inform you that military aircraft conducting low flying training are required to make a booking to
fly in individual low flying areas. I have checked the night low flying area which covers this area
and there were no fixed wing (jet or transport aircraft) or rotary wing (helicopters) booked to low
fly at the time you have specified.

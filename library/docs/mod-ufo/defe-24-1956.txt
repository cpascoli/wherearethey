Thank you for your letter dated 15 May to the Prime Minister on the subject of
UFOs. I have been asked to reply. You will also recall that when we spoke
recently on this subject, I explained that I vould be unable to accept your
offer to appear in your documentary. I shall attempt to set out for you our
position on UFO’s, together with other information that you may find useful. In
this way although I am unable to appear in your film, you will be able to draw
upon the material in this letter in representing the position of the Ministry of
Defence.

You may find it helpful if I begin by explaining that although the Ministry of
Defence does receive and co-ordinate some reports of UFO sightings, our sole
concern is to establish whether or not they pose a threat to the security of the
United Kingdom. Unless we judge that they do, and this has not been the case so
far, we do not attempt to investigate further, or to identify whatever might
have been seen.

Since our interest in UFOs is limited to possible defence implications, we do
not carry out studies into the UFO phenomenon, nor do we have the resources to
undertake any in-depth investigations. I am sure that you will appreciate that
defence funds are fully committed to maintainifig the security of the UK and
meeting our responsibilities to allies and dependents. This being so, we could
not justify the use of defence funds on scientific investigations, unless a
threat to the security of the UK had been identified.

It is clear from the reports we receive that there are Many strange things to

be seen in the sky. However, we believe that rational explanations could be
found for most of them. Some of the possibile explanations include aircraft
lights or aircraft seen from unusual angles, satellites in orbit or satellite
debris re-entering the atmosphere, high altitude kites, helium balloons, weather
balloons, searchlights or lasers reflecting off clouds, ball lightning,
fireballs and meteorites. As you are also doubtless aware, some reports turn
out to be hoaxes. Having said this, we accept that there will always be some
sightings that defy explanation, and ve have an open mind on these.

like to assure you that there is no question of attempting to cover up any
incidents, nor are we trying in any way to obscure the truth about UFOs from the

Thank you for your recent letter to the Secretary of State for Defence on the
subject of UFOs. I have been asked to reply.

I should begin by explaining that while the Ministry of Defence does receive and
co-ordinate some reports of UFO sightings, our sole concern is to establish
whether or not they pose a threat to the security of the United Kingdom. Unless
we judge that they do, and this has not been the case so far, we do not attempt
to investigate further, or to identify whatever might have been seen.

Given this limited involvement with the subject, the Ministry of Defence has no
department which is appointed solely for the purpose of studying UFO reports,
nor are there any staff employed on the subject full time. With regard to the
procedure for UFO sightings, any report submitted to the Ministry of Defence is
automatically passed to this office. The reports we receive, which are usually
limited to very brief details of what was witnessed, are passed to staff in
departments concerned with air defence of the United Kingdom, who examine them
as part of their normal duties. The Ministry of Defence does not carry out
studies, investigations or surveys into the UFO phenomenon.

It is clear from the reports we receive that there are many strange things to
be seen in the sky. However, we believe that rational explanations could be
found for most of them. The possibilities that spring to mind include aircraft
lights, aircraft seen from unusual angles, kites, helium balloons, weather
balloons, unusual cloud formations, satellites in orbit or satellite debris
re-entering the earth’s atmosphere, ball lightning, fireballs and meteorites.
As you may be aware from your reading, there is also a suspicion that some
reports are hoaxes.

I would like to assure you that there is no question of attempting to cover up
any incident, nor are we trying in any way to obscure the truth about UFOs from
the public. The Ministry of Defence does not possess any positive evidence to
support the existance of alien life, or to suggest that alien spacecraft have
ever visited the Earth. .

Tf you are interested in finding out more about UFOs, then you might like to
contact one of the many civilian organisations currently engaged in study of the
UFO phenomenon. These organisations, needless to say, would be very interested
to hear about any UFO sightings. May I suggest the following societies:

The procedure with regard to sightings of any unusual lights or objects in the

or British) should be forwarded to this department, as the central point for all
such business. However, our only role is to ensure that there is no threat to
the security and defence of the UK. There is no policy document setting out any
eriteria for this. :

Information Act, and the release of American documents is a matter for the

is that we,are bound by the provisions of the Public Records Act, and official
files generally remain closed from public viewing for 30 years after the last
action has been taken. With regard to our files on UFOs, therefore, we are not
able to release material en bloc, although we are able to answer any specific
queries you may have about particular incidents that have occurred recently and
can let you know whether we have a.report on the incident and if so, what it
contains.

forwarded to us, and therefore any queries on any UFO incidents that have
occurred in the UK should be addressed to us.

Thank you for your letter of the 5th of August on the subject of Unidentified
Flying Objects (UFOs).

The Ministry of Defence does not have a "Policy Statement" as such, but you may
find it helpful if I begin by explaining that while the Ministry of Defence does
receive and co-ordinate some reports of UFO sightings, our sole concern is to
establish vhether or not they pose a threat to the security of the United
Kingdom. Unless we judge that they do, and this has not been the case so far, we
do not attempt to investigate further, or to identify whatever might have been
seen.

Given this limited invelvement with the subject, the Ministry of Defence has no
department which is appointed solely for the purpose of studying UFO reports,
nor are there any staff employed on the subject full time. The reports we
receive, which are usually limited to very brief details of what was witnessed,
are passed to staff in departments concerned with air defence of the United
Kingdom, who examine them as part of their normal duties.

It is clear from the reports we receive that there are many strange things to be
seen in the sky. However, we believe that rational explanations could be found
for most of them. Fossibilities that spring to mind include aircraft seen from
unusual angles, weather’ balloons, satellites, or unusual cloud formations to
name but a fev. Certainly, we have yet to receive conclusive evidence that alien
spacecraft have ever landed on the planet.

I hope that this is helpful.

Yours Sincerely,

I am writing concerning your emai! message of 16 May in which you requested information regarding the
“Condign Report” and MOD procedures for handling UFO reports. This Department is the focal point within
the Ministry of Defence for correspondence regarding UFOs and the Department jointly responsible with the
Defence Intelligence Staff (DIS) for the release of this report. I will answer your questions in the same order as
your letter.

(1) How does procedure vary from public reports, opposed to one of an official nature?

All UFO sighting reports received by the MOD (from any source) are examined solely to establish whether
what was seen might have some defence significance; namely, whether there is any evidence that the United
Kingdom's airspace might have been compromised by hostile or unauthorised air activity. Any report
considered to be worthy of further examination is sent to relevant department experts.

(2) Are witnesses interviewed in person?
No.

(3) Do witnesses fill out a report form without assistance and mail them in, or is this done by personnel
over the phone and the blanks filled in?

The MOD has a UFO report form which we use to record details of UFO sightings reported on our
answerphone and RAF Stations tend to follow a similar list of details. However, the general public are not
asked to fill in a form and the MOD receives details of UFO sightings in a number of ways. These include
letters, emails, telephone messages to our answerphone, and via contacts with military establishments and the
Police.

(4) What form is used, and can you e-mail a copy?
Please see attached a copy of the report form this department uses to record UFO sightings.

(5) Aside from filling out a “report form”, what investigation is done, if any?

The MOD does not have any expertise or role in respect of 'UFO/flying saucer' matters or to the question of the
existence or otherwise of extraterrestrial lifeforms. We remain totally open-minded, but to date the MOD
knows of no evidence which substantiates the existence of these alleged phenomena. Unless there is evidence
of a potential threat to the United Kingdom from an external source, we do not attempt to identify the precise
nature of each sighting reported to us. We believe that rational explanations, such as aircraft lights or natural
phenomena, could be found for them if resources were diverted for this purpose, but it is not the function of the
MOD to provide this kind of aerial identification service. Jt would be an inappropriate use of defence resources
if we were to do so.

With regard to the release of the “Condign Report” I can advise you that this study was not conducted to
establish the existence of extraterrestrial lifeforms. It was conducted purely to establish whether the UFO
sighting reports received by the MOD were of any value to the Defence Intelligence Staff (DIS) and whether
there was a requirement for the DIS to see them in the future. Given the conclusion it was decided that there
was no such requirement and, since December 2000, UFO reports have not been forwarded to the DIS. The
Ministry of Defence understands the public interest in information about UFOs and has embraced the spirit of
open government. All requests for information are answered as fully as possible and, despite the size and
original classification of this report, staff have taken great care to ensure that the majority of this large report is
made available. We are pleased to be able to make the report available to a wide audience via the MOD website
and if you have not yet had an opportunity to see the full report, you may wish to view it at

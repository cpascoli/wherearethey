Section 40

Thank you for your letter of 14 March 1991 on the subject of
Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs).

I have checked our files for any sighting reports which

correspond to the date, time and location mentioned in your
letter and have found two such reports. I enclose copies of
these reports, which have had the details of the originators
removed in order to ensure that their privacy is maintained.

As you will be aware, the Ministry of Defence has only a limited
involvement in this subject and we do not have the resources to

undertake any in-depth investigations, I can, however, confirm

that we have no reason to link these sightings with a satellite

re-entry. I-regret that I cannot be more positive in offering a
definitive explanation for what was witnessed.

I hope that this is helpful.

Yeu bincerell,

Section 40

I wonder if you could assist with regard to an incident on Wednesday 20
February 1991; approximately 19.30 GMT ‘or within 30 minutes either side).

On that night a large number of reports of a green and white mass of light
/ in the sky were received by myself for the British UFO Research Association
and via other bodies.I worked with Jodrell Bunk and the BBC the next day
collating calls and demystifyting the stories they were flooded with.
I personally fielded calls from as far south as Stoke,as far north as
Preston and to the Wirral in the west and Oldham in the east, describing the
same effect.In my view, and this opinion was shared by those I spoke with at
the institutes named, the phenomenon was probably the re-entry of parts from
a Soviet satellite, or just possibly a rocketry experiment releasing a cloud
of vapour for scientific research on the upper atmosphere.

I wondered if you had any specific information on this matter, which
presumably must have come to your notice as media sources refered to MoD
interest and we had so many calls some surely must have been directed
towards Air staff 2A I would anticipate.

Secondly,I was informed by staff at Manchester Airport that a Manx Airline
crew inbound from Ronaidsway to Ringway observed the spectacle and that an
official report would be filed with you,since the crew expected it
regardiess of possible explanations.A copy of this,minus any data you need
remove for security and witness protection purposes, would be very useful.

Even though reasonably certain this is not a UFO except by definition (if
there be any UFOs in any other sense, of course!) we are interested in the
data accumulated on this case because it offers us useful information about
witness perception of anomalous stimulii. These account for 95% of ail our
Cases.When a booster from Cosmos 1068 reentered on 31 December 1978
(approximately 19.06 GMT) we received nearly 200 reports. We still get them
today. Of course,we can positively indentify what that re-entry was Cand
maybe you can help us do the same with this 1991 case).However,the main
value of the 1978 data was what it taught us about witness observation.

. As many fire brigades and coast guards were called out back in 1978 to
‘phantom’ air crashes and as these space debris events are likely to become
more frequent with tgime, collating this information not only assists our
work but could be of potential value to other sources in the future, maybe
even to yourselves.I would be pleased to submit a copy of our report on the
latest incident for your records, should it be of any practical use there. -

PHOTOGRAPHS OF AN UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECT - SCOTTISH DAILY RECORD

1. US of S(AF) may wish to be aware that the Scottish Daily Record may run
a story regarding an alleged sighting of a UFO near Pitlochrie in early August.
Such stories are not normally drawn to the attention of Ministers and the MoD
press office invariably responds to questions along well-established lines
emphasising our limited interest in the UFO phenomenon and explaining that ve
therefore do not have the resources to undertake any in-depth investigations
into particular sightings. On this occasion however, the MoD has been provided
with six photographic negatives of an alleged UFO by the Scottish Daily Record
and has been asked for comments almost certainly for inclusion in a forthcoming
story. For this reason it is felt that US of S(AF) should be made aware of the
background and the line adopted by the DDPR(RAF) in responding to the newspaper.

2. The photographs, which were received on 10 Sept, are alleged to have
been taken near the A9 road at Clavine, north of Pitlochrie on the evening of 4
August. They show a large stationary, diamond-shaped object past which, it
appears, a small jet aircraft is flying. The negatives have been considered by
the relevant staffs who have established that the jet aircraft is a Harrier (and
also identified a barely visible second aircraft, again probably a Harrier) but
have reached no definite conclusion regarding the large object. It has also
been confirmed that there is no record of Harriers operating in the area at the
time at which the photographs are alleged to have been taken. The negatives
have now been returned to the Scottish Daily Record.

Bs In consultation with DDPR(RAF) it has been agreed that the attached
lines to take should be used in responding to the Scottish Daily Record. These
are consistent with the position adopted in the replies to the many public and
occasional pariiamentary enquiries on the subject of UFOs.

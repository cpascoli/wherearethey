When we spoke on Friday I promised to let you have a note setting out the
Ministry of Defence’s policy and views on the UFO phenomenon.

Although the Ministry of Defence does receive and co-ordinate reports of UFO
sightings, our only concern is to establish whether or not they pose a threat to
the security of the United Kingdom. Unless we judge that they do - and this has
not been the case with any sighting to date - we do not attempt to investigate
further, or to identify whatever might have been seen. :

Given this limited involvement with the subject, the Ministry of Defence has no
department which is appointed solely for the purpose of studying UFO reports,
nor are there any staff employed on the subject full time. The reports we
receive, which are usually limited to very brief details of what was seen, are
passed to staff in departments concerned with the air defence of the United
Kingdom, who examine them as part of their normal duties.

Tt is clear from the reports we receive that there are many strange things to
be sean in the sky. However, we believe that explanations could be found for
most of them. Some of the possible explanations include aircraft lights or
aircraft seen from unusual angles, satellites in orbit or satellite debris
re-entering the atmosphere, high altitude kites, helium balloons, weather
balloons, searchlights or lasers reflecting off clouds, ball lightning,
fireballs and meteorites. As you are also doubtless aware, some reports turn
out to be hoaxes. Having said this, we accept that there will always be some
sightings that appear to defy explanation, and we remain open-minded about .
these.

The Ministry of Defence does not release material on the subject "en bloc"
because, as is the case with all government departments, all Ministry of Defence
files are subject to the Public Records Act, and generally remain closed from
public viewing for 30 years after the last action has been taken. Prior to 1967
all UFO files were routinely destroyed after five years, although a small number
of files survived, and may be viewed at the Public Record Office, Ruskin Avenue,
Kew, Richmond, Surrey, TW9 4DV. The reference numbers of these files are as
follows:

You asked for a breakdown of numbers of UFO sightings reported to the MOD. We
have had 95 sightings so far this-year. We do not usually break the figures
down any further, but having had a quick look through the file, I can tell you
that we have had 5 sightings reported to us during May, 18 during June, and 17
so far this month. Many of these recent_sightings have almost certainly been
generated by people having seen (he SET 35 ship, which has
been operating over London and surrounding areas recently. There has also been
a recent wave of sightings in the Bristol area, although I understand that
lasers or searchlights reflecting off clouds might explain these. The largest
wave of sightings so far this year was on 30/31 March; these sightings occurred
mainly over the South-West, with most of the reports being from Devon, Cornwall
and South Wales. The most common feature of these reports was that people
reported having seen two bright white lights. It was not clear whether these
were two separate objects flying in close formation, or whether they were lights
from one objéct. There were conflicting reports about the speed, height and
heading; the upshot was that no firm conclusions could be drawn, and the
sightings remain unexplained. You may wish to be aware that the largest Perseid
meteorite shower for many years is expected to occur on 11/12 August, with the
best time for viewing likely to be 2am on 12 August; meteorites should be
visible low in the North-Eastern sky. You will also wish to be aware that on
Saturday 28 August there will be a Jean Michel Jarre concert at Wembley Stadium;
this will involve one of the largest displays of lasers and searchlights ever
seen in this country. You might like to put these dates in your diary and bear
these events in mind if you receive any sightings.

You also asked about the structure and duties of this division; broadly
speaking, Sec(AS) deals with secretariat aspects of RAF operations world-wide,
with Sec(AS)2a having responsibility for operations on the NATO Southern Flank
and the rest of the world. There is no special reason why the post that I hold
also has responsibility for UFOs.

The following paragraphs set out the Ministry of Defence’s policy and views on
the UFO and crop circle phenomena.

/

Although the Ministry of Defence does receive and co-ordinate reports of UFO
sightings, our only concern is to establish whether or not they pose a threat to
the security of the United Kingdom. Unless we judge that they do ~- and this has
not been the case with any sighting to date - we do not attempt to investigate
further, or to identify whatever might have been seen.

Given this limited involvement with the subject, the Ministry of Defence has no
department which is appointed solely for the purpose of studying UFO reports,
nor are there any staff employed on the subject full time. The reports we
receive, which are usually limited to very brief details of what was seen, are
passed to staff in departments concerned with the air defence of the United
Kingdom, who examine them as part of their normal duties.

It is clear from the reports we receive that there are many strange things to
be seen in the sky. However, we believe that explanations could be found for
most of them. Some of the possible explanations include aircraft lights or
aircraft seen from unusual angles, satellites in orbit or satellite debris
re-entering the atmosphere, high altitude kites, helium balloons, weather
balloons, searchlights or lasers reflecting off clouds, ball lightning,
fireballs and meteorites. As you are also doubtless aware, some reports turn
out to be hoaxes. Having said this, we accept that there will always be some
sightings that appear to defy explanation, and we remain open minded about
these.

The Ministry of Defence does not release material on the subject "en bloc",
because, as is the case with all government departments, all Ministry of Defence
files are subject to the Public Records Act, and generally remain closed from
public viewing for 30 years after the last action has been taken. Prior to 1967
all UFO files were routinely destroyed after five years, although a small number
of files survived, and may be viewed at the Public Record Office. The files we
hold in this office go back to about 1985, so we are able to answer specific
queries that people have about particular sightings which have occurred in
recent years, and can let them know whether we have a report on the incident,
and if so, what it contains. Most of the reports that we have are relatively

for this subject, we will refer anybody who asks about them to organisations

about UFOs to groups such as BUFORA or Quest International. As is the case with
UFOs, we hold no fixed views on crop circles, and remain open minded about this
intriguing phenomenon.

Thank you for your letter dated 15 July.

You said that the UFO sightings you had previously asked about occurred on 29/30
March. I am reasonably sure that this does in fact tie in with the wave of
sightings that I mentioned in my previous letter, although our dates were 30/31
March. As I mentioned, these sightings occurred mainly over the South-West,
with most of the reports being from Devon, Cornwall and South Wales. The most
common feature of these reports was that people reported having seen two bright
white lights. It was not clear whether these were two separate objects flying
in close formation, or whether they were lights from one object. There were
conflicting reports about the speed, height and heading; the upshot was that no
firm conclusions could be drawn, and the incident remains unexplained. The UFO
groups may have further information - I know that BUFORA have been looking at

these sightings.

You also asked about a UFO sighted over Cleveland on 23/24/25 March. I was
contacted by two other researchers about this, but had not received any reports
myself. It was alleged that aircraft from RAF Leeming were diverted to the area
of the sighting, but this was not true; what happened was that two aircraft were
returning to RAF Leeming from a routine training flight. Their course took them
through the area where the UFO was supposed to have been, but neither aircraft
reported having seen anything unusual. I was subsequently able to find out that
nothing unusual had been picked up on radar, either at RAF Leeming or at Teeside
airport. There was some speculation that the UFO sightings had been caused by
an inversion layer ~ where an area of warm air meets an area of cooler air,
producing a hazy mist; if car headlights were to reflect off this mist, this
could produce UFO sightings, in much the same way as searchlights reflecting off
clouds are responsible for many sightings. Again, the UFO groups may have more
information.

Thank you for your letter dated 30 June, in which you asked a number of
questions about the release of our UFO records. I will answer your points in
the order that you raised them.

Firstly, I can confirm that when any file containing UFO reports is released,
the file will be released in its entirety, with the names and addresses of
witnesses deleted.

Secondly, decisions about the release of information are taken by the Public
Record Office, in consultation with the division responsible for the subject
concerned. In fact, there is no real difference between a report submitted by a
member of the public as opposed to one submitted by a military officer. I
believe that separate files were opened for the Rendlesham Forest and Topcliffe
sightings, not because of the military witnesses, but simply because it was felt
that there might be enough interest - and papers - generated to justify a new
file. There are no hard and fast rules on this; I file all sighting reports,
regardless of who the witness is, on our general sightings file. Because of
these two different approaches, reports made by military personnel might crop up
in general files, or on a separate one, if this was judged to be more
appropriate; in either case, I do not see any reason why any UFO report should
not be released, subject to the provisions of the Public Record Act.

In answer to your third question, the point to consider when looking at whether
or not to release any previously classified government document is whether its
release would be of detriment to the nation. I am not aware of any UFO material
that might be held back on these grounds.

Your final question related to the release of CAA or police reports. Many
members of the public will report sightings to the CAA or police, but these are
handled in exactly the same way as reports submitted directly to us, and will be
placed on the general sightings files. Even if the witness were a serving
police officer or civil pilot, the reports should go onto these general files.
The only exception to this would be if, as I said in my answer to your first
question, it was felt that the sightings themselves warranted the opening of a
separate file. .

Thank you for your letter dated 16 June.

With regard to the points raised py EE I share your view that no
breaches of the Official Secrets Act have taken place; it is some time since I
saw theBRNBRM coadcast concerned, but as I recall, you were stating your views
on some aspects of the UFO phenomenon, .based on information passed to you from a
number of sources. As far as I am concerned, this constitutes rn more than

rsonal opinion. Reading between the lines of letter to
Section 40 | Ido not think either of us have anything to worry about!

Turning to what you say about secret investigations into UFOs, I am not able to
agree with your conclusions. The process which BEBO was describing to you
is nothing more than the routine examination of UFO reports by the appropriate
military experts. As you know, the MOD’s role in looking at UFO reports is to
see whether there is any evidence of a threat to the UK. In reaching a decision
on this point, it is important that reports are looked at by staff with
responsibilities covering the air defence of the UK. This process of evaluation
does not constitute "secret investigations".

You mentioned the Flying Complaints Flight - part of the HQ P&SS organisation
based at RAF Rudloe Manor. I am aware of your views on this unit, as stated in
"Above Top Secret" and "Alien Liaison". Again, however, I cannot agree with
what you say. As you will know, members of the public report UFOs in a number
of ways; many people will notify a local RAF base. Standard forms exist for the
recording of sightings received in this way, and RAF bases should have copies of
these. I do not see, therefore, that there was anything remarkable in somebody
at RAF Rudloe Manor taking details of a UFO sighting from Ralph Noyes. You
mention a number of other areas where involvement with the UFO phenomenon has
been alleged. All I can say is that this division is the focal point for UFO
work. While we are assisted by other specialists - as explained in my third
paragraph - it is not our practice to name those other departments involved.

I am not aware of the position with regard to the "Scientific Intelligence
Branch" files that you mention; of course, even if other divisions did carry out
some work on UFOs in the past, it does not follow that this constitutes "secret
investigations".

concerning the strange light that you saw in the sky on Sunday 7 June. I have

been asked to reply.

the security of the United Kingdom. Unless we judge that they do, and this has
not been the case so far, we do not attempt to investigate further, or to
identify whatever might have been seen.

seen in the sky, although we believe that explanations could be found for most
of them. I suspect that your own theory about the sun reflecting off an object
of some sort is probably correct. While it is possible for a balloon to have
risen to a great height, it is perhaps more likely that a second aircraft was
responsible - one much higher than the one that you saw. While the actual
aircraft would not have been visible to the naked eye, a reflection may well

have been.

Thank you for your recent letter to the Prime Minister, concerning your
suggestion that a department be set up to investigate UFOs and other strange
phenomena. I have been asked to reply, as this office is the focal point for
all enquiries about UFOs.

I am afraid that the Government's involvement in these matters is so limited
that the establishment of such a department could not be justified. With regard
to UFOs, our only role is to establish whether or not they pose a threat to the
security of the United Kingdom. Unless we judge that they do, and this has not
been the case so far, we do not attempt to investigate further, or to identify
whatever might have been seen. The reports we receive, which are usually
limited to very brief details of what was seen, are passed to staff in
departments concerned with the air defence of the United Kingdom, who examine
them as part of their normal duties.

Thank you for your letter dated 28 April, and for sending me a copy of the tape
recording allegedly made by Lt Col Halt.

I listened to the tape with interest, but can offer no definitive view.
Personally, I suspect that the tape is a hoax, albeit an extremely well
constructed one. My main reason for saying this is that I find the whole
scenario extremely unlikely; I have never heard of any instances of military
personnel investigating an incident, whilst simultaneously making an audio
recording. The fact that the tape has been edited down and re-recorded
(complete with tvo very non-military interruptions) also suggests that the
recording was made by enterprising members of the public. Furthermore, if the
recording was genuine, I would expect that the ufologist to whom the tape was
supposedly first made available would be able to produce an official covering
letter; I have never heard of such a letter. Finally, it is perhaps significant
that there is no reference to the tape recording in Lt Col Halt’s memo.

I have never heard any official mention of the tape, so I do not believe that
any official UK or US department would be able to obtain a full length copy of
the recording.

I hope this is helpful, and I wish you luck with your research.

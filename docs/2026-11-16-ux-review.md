# Where Are They: UX review and direction

16 November 2026. Companion to [the project review](2026-11-15-project-review.md) and [the roadmap](roadmap.md). This document assesses how the archive is organised, presented and navigated on phones and on desktop, and proposes how the UX should evolve so that the roadmap's new functions (sign-up, alerts, On this day, the Congress tracker, the support link) fit in without adding complexity.

## Summary

The archive's content model is strong and the video player is excellent. The UX problem is not a lack of features. It is that the site is organised around its own structure (a network of files with a panel beside it) while visitors arrive wanting one thing: a case, a clip, or today's news.

The five changes that matter most, in order:

1. **Treat every file and video page as the landing page**, because it is. Two thirds of visitors never see the home page, and seven in ten are on a phone. On a phone, a file opens as a full-screen sheet with no site name, no navigation and no next step.
2. **Reorder the file page**: what this person said or what happened first, with the clip; the machinery (counters, mentions, possible connections) last and folded away.
3. **Make the home page content-first**: today's item, the latest, a clip that plays in place, the stories. Keep one line of identity and the search. Move the explanation to About.
4. **Make filters visible and local.** A type filter set once silently follows the visitor to Timeline and World, across sessions.
5. **Give the sign-up one consistent place**: inline, after the first useful content on every file, video and feed, never a pop-up.

On the landing page question: yes, change it. But do not copy UAP Insight's bare feed. Their feed is their product. Yours is the archive, and a daily news feed alone would undersell it. The proposal in section 5.1 is a content-first home that mixes what is new with what is best.

## 1. How this was done

- The live site viewed on 16 November on desktop (1,472 px wide, in Chrome) and at phone size (390 × 844, headless Chrome with a phone user agent and touch). Pages: home, Latest, Explore, Timeline, World, Videos, Claims, a person (David Fravor), an incident (O'Hare 2006), a claim (recovered non-human craft), a video (FLIR1).
- Google Analytics for 18 October to 15 November (views, users and bounce per page, and the Tech overview for devices), from `tmp/google-analytics-report/`.
- `templates/app.html` and the structure of `renderPanel` in `app.js` for what is on each page and in what order.

Not done: a real phone, real users, a screen reader, the connection panel, the reading of stories step by step, and typing in the phone search (the automated attempt failed, so the phone search results are unassessed).

**Seven in ten visitors are on a phone.** From the Analytics Tech overview (18 October to 15 November):

| Device | Users | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Phone | 751 | 69% |
| Desktop | 321 | 30% |
| Tablet | 11 | 1% |

- **The real share is higher, about three quarters.** Roughly 100 of the desktop users have screen sizes no real monitor has (800×600: 59; 1280×1280, 2000×2000, 1024×1024, 16384×16384), which are automated browsers. That matches the data-centre traffic noted in the project review.
- **iPhone first.** iOS 510 users (47%), Android 251 (23%), Windows 191, Mac 91. Safari is 384 users.

What follows: design and check on an iPhone in Safari first, at 390 wide, and treat desktop as the enhanced version. Nothing in this review was tested in Safari on a real iPhone; that is the first check to make by hand.

## 2. How the site is organised today

One app with three layers:

- **Views**, in the header (desktop) or the bottom bar (phone): Latest, Explore, Timeline, World, Videos, and under More: Documentaries, Books, Index, Claims, MoD UFO files, About.
- **Files**: person, incident, hearing, program, organisation, document, film, book, video, event, concept, place, plus claims, records and news.
- **The panel**: a file opens in a panel beside the current view (desktop), full width as a reading view (desktop, from its own address), or as a full-screen sheet (phone).

A left side panel carries filters (types, years, themes) on Explore, Timeline, World and Claims, its own filters on Latest, and filters or the playlist on Videos.

Three readings:

- **Only 322 of 1,043 users saw the home page.** About two thirds arrived on a file or a video and never went there. The deep pages are the real front door.
- **The home page loses 60% of those who do see it.** Every other view keeps almost everyone who reaches it (3–15% bounce). The content holds people; the introduction does not.
- **Video pages are a third of all views** (658 of 2,326), and the view with the best content, Claims, is the least visited (16), because it sits under More.

## 3. What works

- **The video page.** Player, title, the linked files as chips, then tabs for Moments, Chapters, Transcript and Mentions. On a phone it keeps the header and the bottom bar, the player comes first, and a moment is one tap. This is the model for every other page.
- **The file header.** Type and status stamps, name, role, a two or three sentence bio, the portrait. The outcome note on an incident ("Partly explained…", with its source) appears before the title, which is exactly right for credibility.
- **The claim page.** Status, the official finding, a summary, and the count of who says so and who disputes it, with first-hand separated from relayed. It answers the visitor's question in the first screen.
- **The timeline.** A plain dated list by decade. It works identically on both screen sizes and needs no explanation.
- **The phone is a real layout**, with a bottom bar, stacked headers and a filters drawer.
- **Real addresses everywhere**, so any state can be shared.

## 4. Findings

### 4.1 The home page is an introduction, and the content is at the bottom

On desktop the first screen is the title, a tagline, one sentence, a search box, two buttons and a decorative network. On a phone the page is about 5,700 px tall, nearly seven screens, in this order:

1. Hero (title, sentence, search, two buttons)
2. Five counters (226, 201, 478, 10, 1,334)
3. "How to read this archive" and the three stamps
4. "Six threads to pull" (six cards, about two screens)
5. "Ways in" (seven numbered views, about a screen and a half)
6. "Latest", starting about five screens down
7. Disclaimer and footer

Problems:

- **Nothing on the first screen is content.** No case, no clip, no date. The visitor is asked to choose between "Explore the network" and "Watch the testimony" before seeing a reason to.
- **The primary button leads to the hardest view.** "Explore the network" opens the graph, which on a phone is a cluster of small labelled shapes operated by tap and double-tap.
- **The freshest material is last.** Latest is the proof the archive is alive, and it is below everything else. Its first card on 16 November is dated 14 Nov 2004 (the pinned Nimitz anniversary), unlabelled as an anniversary.
- **"Ways in" repeats the navigation** that is already in the header and the bottom bar.
- **The counters describe the archive, not the subject.** They matter to someone deciding whether to trust it, which comes after interest, not before.
- **On a phone the header has no site name on the home page**, only the mark, the X link and settings.

The "Six threads" are the best thing on the page: each is a concrete promise with a concrete action ("▶ Watch at 32:06"). They are the right idea in the wrong place.

### 4.2 Deep pages are the front door, and on a phone they have no door frame

A person, incident or claim opened on a phone is a full-screen sheet. The header and the bottom bar are gone. The only control is a ✕ in the corner, beside "FILE P-A1D6 · 1/17 DOCUMENTED".

A visitor arriving from ChatGPT or Reddit therefore sees:

- no site name anywhere on the screen,
- no navigation and no search,
- a file code and a count ("1/17 documented") that mean nothing yet,
- an "Explore from here" button that leads to the graph,
- and, at the end of a long scroll, nothing: no next case, no related story, no way to follow.

Closing the sheet lands them on a view they did not choose. This is the single most important fix, and it is where the sign-up, the next step and the brand all belong.

On desktop the same page keeps the header and reads well. The gap there is the end of the page: it stops.

### 4.3 The file page puts the machinery before the testimony

A person's page, after the header, runs:

1. A strip of up to thirteen section links with counters: Profile · Positions on claims 7 · Watch 3 videos · Video moments 70 · Connections 32 · Mentioned in transcripts 95 in 31 videos · Mentioned together · Possible connections · Trace a connection · Themes · Notes & claims 17
2. Profile (two long paragraphs)
3. Positions on claims
4. Watch (videos)
5. Video moments
6. Connections
7. Mentions, possible connections, trace, themes
8. Notes & claims: the sourced statements, last

Problems:

- **The sourced statements come last.** "Notes & claims" is the archive's core product (a statement, its status, its source) and it is at the bottom under a name that undersells it.
- **The first moments on Fravor's page are other people talking about him.** The list opens with Kevin Day and Chad Underwood. His own testimony under oath should lead.
- **The strip is an inventory, not a guide.** Thirteen labels in small capitals, several of them tools ("Trace a connection", "Possible connections") or statistics ("Mentioned together in transcripts"). On a phone it is absent, so there is no way to jump at all on a page that is many screens long.
- **Connections are a long list of small groups** ("authored", "statement about", "appears in", "flew with", "fellow witness"…). Accurate, but 32 chips in a column is a lot to read on a phone. The notes under them ("She flew the second Super Hornet in Fravor's flight") are the valuable part and are the smallest text.
- **Everything is the same weight.** Section headings are small grey capitals; a visitor cannot tell the essential from the supplementary.

### 4.4 Filters are global, sticky and easy to miss

While reviewing, Explore showed only organisations, Timeline showed 9 entries, and World showed 3 dots. The cause was a type filter (`wat:types = ["organization"]`) saved in the browser from an earlier visit. It applied to three views at once and survived between sessions. The only sign was a small "clear" beside the Types heading in the side panel, which on a phone is inside a closed drawer.

A visitor who isolates one type on Explore and comes back a week later to Timeline sees a nearly empty archive with no explanation. The same side panel also appears beside Claims, where types and years have no evident effect.

### 4.5 Navigation: the bar reflects the architecture, not the demand

- **Phone bottom bar: Latest · Explore · World · Videos · More.** There is no Home, Timeline is under More, and so is Claims. World (80 views) has a slot; Timeline (73) and Claims do not.
- **"Explore" names the graph.** To a newcomer "explore" means browsing, and they get a network diagram. On desktop that is intriguing. On a phone it is the weakest view and it holds the second slot.
- **Search is the best way in and is not a destination on phones.** The header has a box on inner pages, but none on the home page (the hero has its own), and there is no search tab.
- **More holds eight unrelated things**: a view (Timeline), two shelves, the index, claims, a separate search tool (MoD files), About, privacy and two social links.
- **Vocabulary.** File, dossier, record, official record, moment, claim, statement, position, note, trail, story, key files, thread. A first visit meets most of them. Several pairs mean nearly the same thing (file and dossier; claim, statement and note; story and thread).

### 4.6 Latest: one item per screen, led by 2004

On a phone the Latest list is about 18,500 px for some 32 items: roughly one card per screen. Each card has a large image, a three-line title, six lines of summary, a row of people and two buttons. UAP Insight shows three items per screen. A news list should be scannable.

On desktop, Latest opens with the pinned file already open in the panel, so half the screen is a 2004 incident under a heading that says "Latest". The pin is a good editorial tool; it needs a label ("On this day, 2004") so it reads as intended.

### 4.7 Explore on a phone

The key-files map fits the screen, but the labels are a few pixels high and the instruction is "tap to read · double-tap to follow". Showing connections is a long-press. None of this is discoverable, and a graph is the wrong instrument for a 390 px screen. The stories row above it ("The Tic Tac (Nimitz)", "The 2017 New York Times story"…) is the phone-friendly way in, and it is a single scrolling line of chips.

### 4.8 Desktop

Desktop is in better shape: the header carries everything, the reading view is comfortable, the side-by-side of a view and a file works.

What it does not yet use the space for:

- **The file page's right column is a list of chips.** It could hold what a wide screen is good at: the clip playing beside the statement, a small map or timeline of this file, the neighbourhood drawn as on the share card.
- **Video does not persist.** Opening a file from a moment is fine on the Videos view, but leaving the view ends playback. A mini player that survives navigation is the desktop equivalent of the phone's single column.
- **The home page uses a wide screen for a decorative graph** instead of three columns of content.
- **Hover is unused outside the graph.** A chip for a person or case could preview the bio and status on hover, which would cut many clicks.

## 5. Direction

### Principles

1. **Content before explanation.** Show a case or a clip, then say what the archive is. Trust is earned by the first sourced statement, not by a paragraph about sourcing.
2. **Every page is a landing page.** Each must say where the visitor is, give the essential first, and end with one next step.
3. **One primary action per screen.** Play, read, or follow. Everything else is secondary and looks it.
4. **Same order on both screens.** The phone shows one column in priority order. Desktop shows the same order with the supporting material beside it, not different content.
5. **Fewer words to learn.** One name per thing.
6. **Progressive disclosure.** The depth stays (mentions, possible connections, the trace tool), folded under "More about this file" for the people who want it.
7. **Nothing interrupts.** No pop-ups, no sticky bars over the content. Requests (sign-up, support) sit inline where they make sense.

### 5.1 The home page: "today on the record"

Replace the introduction with a content-first page. Keep the identity to one line and the search.

Phone, top to bottom:

```
┌──────────────────────────────┐
│ ◉ WHERE ARE THEY        🔍 ⚙ │  brand always shown
│ What officials and pilots    │  one line, links to About
│ have said on the record.     │
├──────────────────────────────┤
│ ON THIS DAY · 14 NOV 2004    │  roadmap 0.9
│ [ clip thumbnail      ▶ ]    │  plays in place
│ The Nimitz “Tic Tac”         │
│ 8 military witnesses · open  │
├──────────────────────────────┤
│ LATEST                       │  compact rows, 3 per screen
│ 11 Nov · Video · title…      │
│ 10 Nov · Interview · title…  │
│ 8 Nov  · Interview · title…  │
│ See all →                    │
├──────────────────────────────┤
│ ✉ Next release or hearing    │  roadmap 0.3, after first
│   in your inbox  [email][→]  │  content, inline
├──────────────────────────────┤
│ START WITH A STORY           │  the six threads + stories,
│ ‹ Grusch · Nimitz · AAWSAP › │  as a swipeable row of cards
├──────────────────────────────┤
│ THE BIG QUESTIONS            │  3 top claims with status
│ Disputed · The U.S. holds…   │
├──────────────────────────────┤
│ How this archive works →     │  one line: stamps, sources
│ 1,741 files · built 16 Nov   │
└──────────────────────────────┘
```

Desktop: the same blocks in three columns under a slim masthead: left, On this day and the featured clip; centre, Latest; right, stories and the top claims. The decorative network goes, or shrinks into the Explore card.

What moves where:

- The counters and "How to read this archive" go to About, with one line and a link on the home page. The three stamps are already explained in place by the stamps themselves on every file.
- "Ways in" is removed; the navigation does that job.
- The disclaimer stays in the footer.
- The two hero buttons go. The first action is the play button on the day's clip.

Why not a pure feed like UAP Insight: their items are links out with a score, and on 16 November most of their top items scored 3–5 out of 10. News about this subject is thin on most days. The archive's strength is that it always has something good from eighty years of record, which is what "On this day", the stories and the claims put on the first screens. The feed is one block, not the page.

Measure: home bounce (60% today; target 45%), share of home visits that play a clip or open a file, sign-ups from the home block.

### 5.2 The file page: one template, testimony first

The same order for person, incident, program and the other types, on both screens:

1. **Header**: stamps, name, role or date and place, the bio or outcome note, portrait or picture. As today, minus the file code, which moves to the foot.
2. **Key testimony**: the two or three strongest moments, as cards with a play button, the quote and the date. For a person, their own words first (sworn testimony before interviews). For a case, one moment per main witness.
3. **What is on the record**: the sourced statements, today's "Notes & claims", renamed "Statements" and moved up. Five shown, the rest behind "Show all 17".
4. **Where they stand** (people) or **What it bears on** (cases): positions on claims, with the status of each claim.
5. **Connected to**: connections grouped into at most four headings (People, Cases, Programs and organisations, Documents and video), each chip with its note as readable text. "Show all 32" after the first eight.
6. **Watch and read**: videos, documents, books, official records and footage.
7. **More about this file**, folded: all video moments, transcript mentions, mentioned together, possible connections, trace a connection, themes, the file code and counts.
8. **The foot**: the next step and the sign-up (5.4).

The section strip becomes at most five tabs that stick under the header on both screens: **Overview · Testimony · Statements · Connections · Sources**. On a phone it scrolls sideways like the video page's tabs, which already work.

Desktop uses the second column for the supporting material of whatever is in the first: beside Key testimony, the player; beside Statements, the claims; beside Connections, the neighbourhood drawn as a small graph that links into Explore. Same order, more at once.

This is roadmap item 1.7, in detail.

### 5.3 The phone shell

- **Keep the header and the bottom bar on file pages.** A file is a page, not a sheet over something else. The header shows the mark and the name; ✕ becomes a back arrow that returns to where the visitor came from, or to the home page for a first-page visit.
- **Bottom bar, proposed: Today · Videos · Timeline · Explore · More.** Today is the new home (it absorbs Latest as a block and a "See all"). Timeline comes up from More because it is the simplest reading order and will host the Congress tracker. World and Claims move into More, and Claims also gets its block on the home page. This is a judgement from four weeks of data; the per-view figures are not split by device, so confirm with taps per tab once it ships.
- **Search in the header on every page, including home**, as an icon that opens a full-screen search with recent and suggested items ("Grusch", "Nimitz", "AATIP", "PURSUE").
- **Explore on a phone opens on stories and key files as cards**, with the graph one tap away ("See the map"). The graph stays the default on desktop.

### 5.4 Where the roadmap's functions go

Each new function gets one place and one form, the same on every page.

| Function (roadmap) | Where it appears | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Sign-up (0.3) | Home, after the first block. File and video pages, after Key testimony or the moments, and again in the foot. Latest and Timeline, after the fifth item | One inline block: a line of promise, an email field, a button. On a file: "Get an email when this case changes, and when the next release or hearing lands". After submitting, the block becomes "You're following this" and stays that way (remembered in the browser) |
| Release and hearing alerts (0.3, 0.11) | The same block, plus a dated strip on the home page before a known event ("Hearing on [date] · get the moments within a day") | Not a separate sign-up |
| On this day (0.9) | First block of the home page; a marked card at the top of Latest; the digest | A card labelled "On this day · year", never an unlabelled pin |
| PURSUE explainer (0.8) | `program/pursue`, linked from the home page on release days and from every record | The file template with an FAQ block and the release table |
| Plain answers (0.8) | The first lines of the header on program, concept, claim and key case pages | Two sentences before the bio, no new component |
| Congress tracker (1.9) | A preset on Timeline: chips at the top of the list, "All · Congress · Releases · Incidents" | The timeline's own list with richer cards for hearings (witnesses, three moments, official record) |
| Calendar of what is coming (1.4) | "Coming up" strip on the home page when something is scheduled; top of the Congress view | Hidden when empty |
| Follow a file (1.2) | The sign-up block on that file | Same component, with the file's name |
| What changed (1.3) | A dated line under the header ("Updated 14 Nov: 3 statements added") that opens the change list | One line |
| Support the archive (0.10) | Footer of every page, the About page, the digest, and once at the end of a story | A text link and one sentence with the running costs and the independence promise. Never in the header |
| Next step (1.7) | The foot of every file and video | One card: the story this file belongs to, else the most connected case, else the next video |
| Suggest a correction (2.7) | The foot of every file, after the next step | A text link |

Rules that keep it simple:

- One sign-up component, used everywhere, never as a pop-up or a bar that covers content or the bottom bar.
- A page shows it at most twice. A visitor who has subscribed or dismissed it does not see the request again on that device.
- The order in the foot is fixed: next step, sign-up, correction, support.

### 5.5 Filters

- **Scope them to the view where they were set**, and drop them at the start of a new visit. Settings (credible only, hide explained, theme) stay remembered; filters do not.
- **Show any active filter in the content area**, not only in the side panel: a chip above the list or the map, "Organisations only ✕".
- **Hide the side panel where it does nothing** (Claims).
- On Timeline, replace the type list with the four presets from 5.4 for most visitors, and keep the full filter set behind "More filters".

### 5.6 Latest and lists

- **Phone: compact rows.** Date, type, a two-line title, one line of people, a small thumbnail on the right. About three per screen. The summary and the chips appear when the row is opened.
- **Desktop: do not open a file in the panel on arrival.** Show the list at full width in two columns; the panel opens on a click.
- **The pin is labelled** ("On this day" or "Featured until 20 Nov").
- The same compact row is used on the home page, Latest, the Timeline and search results, so a visitor learns one pattern.

### 5.7 Words

| Keep | Replace | With |
|---|---|---|
| File | Dossier, file card | File |
| Statement | Notes & claims, fact, note | Statement |
| Claim (the 28 propositions) | Position | "Where they stand" |
| Moment | | |
| Story | Thread, trail (in the interface) | Story; "path" for a visitor's own trail |
| Official record | Record | Official record |
| Explore | "Explore the network", graph | "Map of connections" on buttons that open the graph |

Status stamps keep their three words. "Explore from here" becomes "See its connections".

### 5.8 Desktop: use the room

In order of value:

1. The home page in three columns (5.1).
2. The file page's second column as live context (5.2): the player beside the testimony.
3. A mini player that keeps a video playing while the visitor opens files, with the current moment's files offered beside it.
4. Hover previews on person and case chips: portrait, role, status, one line.
5. The connection panel offered from Connections ("How are these two connected?") instead of only from the graph.

### 5.9 What happens to Explore

It stays, with its own place in the navigation on both screens. It is the archive's signature and the only view that shows how things connect. What changes is its role: from the main way in to the tool for one question, "how is this connected to that?".

- **Desktop: unchanged.** It opens on the key-files map with the stories above it, and trails, the connection panel and the filters work as today.
- **Phone: stories and key files first, as cards**, with "See the map" one tap away. The graph is still there; it is no longer the first thing a thumb meets.
- **It is no longer the home page's main button.** The home page leads with content; Explore is reached from the navigation, from a story, and from files.
- **More ways into it from files.** Each file's Connections section shows its neighbourhood as a small drawing (as on the share cards) that opens Explore centred on that file. "Explore from here" becomes "See its connections". That is a better entrance than a cold start on 28 shapes, because the visitor arrives with a file they already care about.
- **Stories stay the guided use of the graph**, and gain a card on the home page.

How much the graph itself is used is not known. Explore had 172 views from 75 users and a 4% bounce, but the 528 file views titled "· graph ·" are files opened with Explore behind them, not proof that anyone used the map. The `explore_start` and `select_content` events would show it; pull them before deciding how much further to invest in the graph.

## 7. Questions for the owner

1. **The network on the home page.** It is the archive's signature image and the first thing to go in 5.1. Keep it as a small element, or let it go?
2. **Bottom bar.** Is World dear enough to keep its slot over Timeline?
